# Phase 3 — Becoming Knowable / The Shared-Story ("Stuff-We-Both-Know") Phase (Deep Knowledge Base)

**Primary source:** Greg Thomson, *"Another 250 Hours of Special-Growth Participation: Getting Underway in Shared-Story Activities"*, Version 1.1, April 2007 (`research/phase3.txt`, 1,185 lines, incl. Appendices 1–4 with contributions by Rebecca Huston).
**Secondary sources:** `research/gpa-research.json` keys `phase3doc` + `phase3pdf`; `research/phase2-3-story-library.md`; app encoding in `src/lib/phases.ts` (id 3).
**Motto printed on the guide's title page:** "Don't learn the language! Rather, discover a new world, as it is known and shared by the people among whom you are living."

---

## 1. Phase identity & goals

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | Phase 3: the **Knowable** phase (app: "Becoming Knowable"); also the **Shared-Story Phase** / **"Stuff-We-Both-Know"** phase |
| Hours | **250 hours** of supercharged participation sessions |
| Position in programme | Hours **250–500** of the 1,500-hour Six-Phase Programme (after Phase 1 Here-and-Now = 100 h and Phase 2 Story-Building = 150 h) |
| Sub-phases | **3A = 100 h**, **3B = 75 h**, **3C = 75 h** |
| Session size | 1 nurturer + **1–4 growing participators**; sessions run **2–5 hours** |
| Recommended pace | **25 hrs/week** with nurturer (5 h/day × 5 days) **plus ~15 hrs/week** of private listening (and possibly reading) |
| Six-Phase table row | Major activity: "Communicating around familiar stories"; social life: "Able to start developing meaningful relationships with a number of people"; ability developed: "Ability to understand complex stories and simple explanations, and to tell simple stories" |

**The master principle.** Stories and descriptions whose content the grower **already largely knows** are understandable in the host language long before unfamiliar material is. Phase 3 systematically exploits shared knowledge — world fairy tales, Bible stories, scripts of everyday routines, cartoons pre-watched at home, experiences the pair lived through together, host stories pre-loaded via translation — so that a whole range of story-understanding abilities can be built ahead of schedule, in preparation for understanding normal, unpredictable host speech.

**Entry state (how far you've come).** From Phase 2 the grower understands **~2,000+ words**, can follow simple picture-supported stories, talks in short simple sentences, and has a deepened relationship with one or a few host people. Self-test for readiness: in the Bridge Story Activity, "If you find you get stuck for an inordinately long time on almost every sentence, you may not yet be ready for Phase 3."

**Where Phase 3 takes you ("done" looks like):**
- Understands **relatively rich and complex stories** (vs. Phase 2's simple, picture-supported narratives).
- Understands **more abstract explanations ("expository speech") without visual-aid support** — the big new step, preparing directly for Phase 4 (Deep Personal Relationships), where host people explain their world in detail.
- Listening vocabulary grows by **another 2,000–3,000 words → total ~4,000–5,000**.
- **"Basic conversational ability"**: can discuss a wide range of topics, including ones never previously discussed, and can **tell simple stories**.
- **Reading may become possible for the first time** — there is finally material at the grower's level (the massaged familiar stories themselves).
- Has begun **new relationships beyond the paid nurturer** (a hard gate: see §7), and could now handle and benefit from living with a host family.
- Knowledge of basic daily host-world activities greatly increased.

**The seven key goals** (the guide's own list, "Lots of variations, but the same key goals"):
1. A deepening relationship with your nurturer
2. New relationships with host people
3. Familiarity with a growing volume of language
4. Steadily growing vocabulary
5. Understanding increasingly complex material
6. Understanding more of the host world
7. Basic conversational ability

**Honest limitations at exit** (the guide is explicit): conversations still need lots of help from the host partner; most native-to-native speech is still not understandable; the grower's own speech "won't be at all native-sounding" and may be "extremely riddled with mistakes." This is expected and acceptable.

**Early exit rule:** "if you begin finding Phase 3 activities quite easy, you may want to consider doing less than 250 hours in Phase 3, and moving on to Phase 4." A signal: being able to understand simple host children's stories that are *entirely new* to you.

## 2. The instructions — how sessions are structured

### What is needed for the first session (the guide's checklist)
1. A nurturer
2. One to four growing participators
3. Two to five hours for the session(s)
4. A notebook or loose-leaf paper for the **word log**
5. A **world story (Bridge Story)** such as Cinderella — either (Variant 1) a host-language children's storybook version of a story the grower knows but has never heard in the host language, or (Variant 2) the nurturer took the story home a day+ earlier in a language she knows, read it 2–3 times, and will tell it in her own words
6. A sample **"attempted" Script of Life** written out by the grower in his native language (model: "Using a Bus in Kazakhstan", 27 steps, Appendix 2)
7. A video/DVD of **Tom & Jerry cartoons** or similar (lots of action, few words) + player
8. A **sound-recording device** (tape recorder, MP3 recorder, laptop, handheld)
9. Pencil and paper

### Day 1 (may realistically spread over several days — "It may be the exceptional case in which someone finishes all of the activities in a day")
**Before the session:** the grower privately watches the action cartoon (so its story is familiar before the nurturer tells it); the nurturer has learned the world story's plot (not word-for-word) well enough to tell it in her own words.

**The session** — direct paraphrase of the guide's plan:
1. **Begin with small talk** — "a few minutes of small talk, putting the personal relationship with the nurturer in first place. Needless to say, all of this small talk is in the host language."
2. **Activity 1: Bridge Story Activity** — hearing and massaging a world story (can take most or all of the 1–3 hours; may be capped at e.g. 1 hour and finished over 2–3 days — "This applies to all of the activities").
3. **Activity 2: Script of Life Activity** — including the four one-time **training steps** (see §6).
4. **Activity 3: Action Cartoon Activity**.
5. **Last activity of the day, Activity 4: A Shared Experience Activity** — go out (or stay in) and do something together.

**After Day 1 and before Day 2:** make notes about the Shared Experience; reflect on the Bridge Story's difficulty level; listen to the day's recordings, pausing and replaying the words that strike you as most useful; watch another action cartoon for tomorrow; make sure tomorrow's Bridge Story is ready (nurturer takes a copy home to learn if necessary). The guide's **admonition**: "start developing good habits now!… Much of the power of Phase 3 will be lost if you don't faithfully listen to the stories you've massaged, and keep listening to them until they are easy to follow, and even after that, listening to them again from time to time."

### Day 2 — "the model for subsequent days" (all of Phase 3A)
1. Small talk
2. **Reminiscing Activity** (about yesterday's Shared Experience)
3. **Strengthening Vocabulary Activity** (optional — "it can eat up too much time")
4. **Bridge Story Activity** (hearing and massaging)
5. **Script of Life Activity** (training no longer needed)
6. **Action Cartoon Activity**
7. **Shared Experience Activity**
8. Things to do before the next day (same homework loop as after Day 1; nurturer can start keeping idea lists for Scripts of Life and Shared Experiences)

**Before each session:** review notes on yesterday's Shared Experience; re-listen to your recordings; re-watch the new cartoon; have everything organised before the nurturer arrives.

### The full-time weekly schedule (Phase 3A sample, 5 h/day, 25 h/week)
- **Monday:** Small talk 10 min · Strengthening vocabulary from Friday 20 min · Recording and massaging a Bridge Story **2 h 30 min** · Recording, massaging and acting out a Script of Life **1 h** · Recording and massaging Action Cartoon story **1 h**. Total 5 hrs.
- **Tuesday:** Small talk 10 min · Strengthening vocab from Monday 20 min · Bridge Story 2 h 30 · Scripts of Life 1 h · **Engaging in a Shared Experience 1 h**. Total 5 hrs.
- **Wednesday:** Small talk 10 min · **Recording and massaging reminiscence of Tuesday's Shared Experience 1 h** · Strengthening vocab 20 min · Bridge Story 2 h 30 · Action Cartoon 1 h. Total 5 hrs.
- **Thursday:** Small talk 10 min · Strengthening vocab 20 min · Bridge Story 2 h 30 · Scripts of Life 1 h · Shared Experience 1 h. Total 5 hrs.
- **Friday:** Small talk 10 min · Reminiscence of Thursday's Shared Experience 1 h · Strengthening vocab 20 min · Bridge Story 2 h 30 · Action Cartoon 1 h **OR** Scripts of Life 1 h. Total 5 hrs.
- Printed under both sample schedules: **"Note: goal for making acquaintances with new vocabulary: 8 words per hour average."**

**Simpler legitimate variant** (minimum schedule = Bridge Story + Scripts of Life only): Small talk 10 min · vocab 20 min · Bridge Story **3 h 30** + Script of Life 1 h (M/W/F) or Bridge Story **4 h 30** (T/Th). Total 5 hrs/day.

**Proportion rule:** the Bridge Story Activity is the mainstay and should take "**at least half of the time, and possibly most of the time**." Everything except Bridge Stories and Scripts of Life may be postponed.

### Phase 3B sample days
**Sample 1 — "for the adventuresome":**
- Before the session: listen once more to the recording of the *translation* of today's host story (or re-read the transcript).
- Session: 15 min small talk · 15 min strengthening yesterday's vocabulary · **2 hrs recording and massaging the new host story** (already familiar in translation) · 15 min Input Flood activity (e.g. sentences of the form "This boy is trying to…") · 15 min role-play of getting a haircut · 1 hr recording and massaging a Familiar-Place Description · **1 hr attempting to start conversations with strangers who sit idly in the park**.
- Evening: listen to today's recordings (especially the massaged portion), the Familiar-Place description, the Role-Play and Input Flood recordings; listen once again to next week's host-story translation; review your idea lists.
- "Note: goal for having strong encounters with new vocabulary: 8 words per hour average."

**Sample 2 — "for someone who likes to keep things simple":** 15 min small talk · 15 min vocab · **3 h 30 recording and massaging a new host story** · 1 hr visiting neighbours who have befriended you; same evening routine.

### Part-time pattern
"Do everything imaginable, including making serious sacrifices… to pursue growing participation as your full-time job for as long as you can, for up to a year and a half or two years… Phase 3 is definitely too early for you to be taking on other major responsibilities. In fact, finishing Phase 3 would be a good minimum" before taking on other work. If unavoidable: **2 hours every morning (e.g. 7:00–9:00)** — Bridge Stories 4 days/week, Scripts of Life 1 day/week, plus daily small talk — and be **"fanatical about listening to your Listening Library"**: while walking, waiting at the dentist, sitting in traffic, trying to fall asleep. (Thomson's own part-time case: four stints of 4–5 months over four years ≈ **650 hours = 43% of the recommended ideal**; at that rate the full programme would take 10–11 years.)

### Universal quality bar (the "anything goes" rule of 3C, applicable throughout)
Any activity is legitimate as long as it delivers:
1. listening to speech understandable **with only a moderate amount of massaging**;
2. acquaintance with **an average of at least 8 new vocabulary items per hour**;
3. **lots of interaction** with the nurturer, including
4. **lots of opportunity for the grower to talk**.

## 3. What the NURTURER does

**Profile:** a host person who "kindly, gently, patiently assists you in your efforts to participate in her world," paid during Phases 1–5. For Phase 3 specifically: "It would be helpful if the paid nurturer for Phase 3 **enjoys telling stories, and has a knack for story-telling, but is also able to simplify stories and gear them to your current level** of understanding ability."

**Core behaviors:**
- **Learns Bridge Stories at home in advance** (reads a version in a language she knows 2–3 times) and retells them spontaneously in her own words, **culturally adapted**: begins/ends the way host children's stories naturally begin/end (never literal "Once upon a time…" / "…happily ever after" translations); makes culturally necessary substitutions (a printed Kazakh Cinderella calls the fairy an "angel"); tells it so it sounds like a host children's story — "However, she should not unnecessarily and radically alter the plot."
- **Tells or reads stories aloud slowly and carefully** while the grower records and stays silent.
- **During massaging:** repeats sentences (2–3 repetitions is normal), breaks sentences into smaller bits, explains unknown words and opaque sentences **only in the host language** — via definitions ("If a man has a wife and he dies, she is a widow"), examples on request ("A child hurts animals. He pulls their tails… He is mean."), pantomime and posing (physically "acting lazy"), voice acting (sounding stern for "Do the work!"), and **example-flooding grammar constructions** ("A doctor made me take medicine. My mother made me get water. The policeman made me stop my car. The shopkeeper made me pay for my purchases. I made my horse go fast." — pausing after each example for the grower to reflect).
- **Confirms or gently recasts** the grower's trial sentences ("GP: She hurts him much. She mean." → "N: Yes. If she hurts him often, she is mean.").
- **Writes every new word into the word log** (the nurturer physically writes; the grower may pencil in a rough native gloss + which story and where).
- **Authors all Scripts of Life** after the one-time training — "only a host person can make up Scripts of Life that describe what host people expect to happen in their world" — and adds steps when the grower probes.
- **Narrates action cartoons live** while the screen is hidden from the grower (information gap), then **retells the story naturally without the cartoon, first reviewing the word log** so all new words get deliberately re-included in the polished Listening Library version.
- **Joins and proposes Shared Experiences**, makes notes on what was observed together, and **leads the Reminiscing** the next day while the grower back-channels.
- **Keeps her own running idea lists** for Scripts of Life and Shared Experiences (from Day 2 onward).
- Later in the phase: **reads written stories aloud** (for diglossic languages like Arabic, reads the formal article/story then retells in the spoken register); lists stories universally known among her people; **retells the grower's life stories** cleanly for the Listening Library; **records herself telling old stories native-to-native to another host person** with the grower absent; suggests multiple options for Lexicarry speech bubbles; **introduces the grower to her own close friends** so new, partly independent relationships can form.

**How her behavior changes across the phase:** early 3A she "may have gone to a lot of effort in order to adapt her speech to your limited listening ability, speaking slowly, enunciating clearly, limiting her vocabulary, and keeping the form of sentences relatively simple." As difficulty must keep rising, she moves to natural-form retellings → written stories read aloud (longer, more complex sentences, richer vocabulary) → genuine host stories (where she holds vast background knowledge the grower lacks, making explanations more complex) → expository material → native-to-native-speed retellings recorded without the grower present.

**Don'ts:**
- Never use the grower's language or translate — everything in the host language. (The guide's joke about small talk: "Otherwise, the host person would become the growing participator in your home languaculture, and you would be the nurturer. Let's keep the roles straight, now!")
- Don't assume large amounts of host-world background knowledge early (that is exactly why Bridge Stories come first).
- Don't radically alter story plots.
- Don't let sessions dissolve into hours of chitchat (plateau trap, §6 "Small talk").
- Don't drill arbitrary grammar (flooding is only for forms the grower is *already* reaching for).
- Don't sit and nod while the grower practices reading aloud — "you don't need to pay a nurturer to listen to you"; her reading aloud *to* the grower is the valuable direction.
- Don't keep material so simple there's no challenge, nor so hard that massaging is tedious.

## 4. What the PARTICIPANT (grower/GP) does

**In-session response modes:**
- **Silent listening** during the initial telling of a story (grower "remains quiet while the nurturer is telling the story").
- **Massaging** (the dominant mode): plays the recording one sentence at a time; asks for repetition ("Please repeat that sentence." "Again?"); asks for breakdowns ("Her father married…?"); asks word meanings ("I don't know the word 'widow'. What does that mean?"); asks for examples ("Tell me an example — someone is mean"); offers his own trial examples for confirmation ("My grandfather died. My grandmother is a widow."; "A man hits his wife. He is mean."); probes cultural side-paths ("Among your people, when a man's wife dies — maybe he will marry a widow?"); orders words into the log ("Please write it in the list."); rewinds and replays; and deliberately "tries to dwell a bit on each new word… to really take note of it and let it make a deep impression." Moves on when he grasps "at least the basic gist" of a sentence.
- **Physical response:** in the Script of Life Activity, plays the recording and **pantomimes each step** as he hears it; performs the hand-washing demonstration during nurturer training; acts in role-plays; handles props (the rope/lamp anecdotes).
- **Back-channel conversation behavior** in Reminiscing: culture-appropriate listener signals ("yeah", "really!", "mhm", "I see" are the Canadian examples), recognizing turn-taking cues, contributing brief turns ("I was afraid"), then returning the floor. "If during Phases 1 and 2 you haven't been paying attention to how conversation works among host people, then you need to start doing so now."
- **Talking modes** (mid-to-late phase): retelling massaged stories ("Let me see if I understood your story…"); telling whole life stories without avoiding the hard parts; serial "epic" installments; weekend recaps; Output Flooding production; Lexicarry bubble-filling with multiple variants; raising community topics with other people.

**Homework / self-directed work (substantial — ~15 hrs/week):**
- **Re-listen to every session recording** before the next session, pausing and replaying personally useful words; keep re-listening "many times in the coming weeks" until each recording is **easy** to follow, and periodically thereafter.
- Distinguish word intents privately: some words only need recognition ("stepsister"), others he wants productively ("lazy").
- **Watch the next action cartoon** privately before each cartoon session.
- **Make notes** after every Shared Experience.
- **Phase 3B host-story prep:** listen to / read the translation of an upcoming host story **every day for several days** until highly familiar — *before* ever hearing it in the host language.
- **Brainstorming and list-building:** carry a small pad through daily life; maintain four lists (Script of Life ideas, Shared Experience ideas, familiar places/routes, role-play situations); exchange additions with the nurturer; deliberately "get out and do things in the host world" (open a bank account, mail a letter, get a haircut, visit a tailor) to feed the lists.
- **Prepare the one-time "attempted" Script of Life** in his own language before Day 1.
- **Social work** (3B onward): taxi conversations, neighbour teas, shopkeeper visits during slow hours, conversations with strangers — 3–5 hrs/week countable toward the 25.
- **Reading** (optional, mid-to-late phase): read the already-massaged familiar stories **alone at home** — never following text while listening in session.
- Evening routine (3B sample): listen to today's recordings; re-listen to next week's host-story translation; review idea lists.

## 5. WHAT they are learning

**Vocabulary (the numbers):**
- Entry: **~2,000+ words** understood. Phase 3 adds **"perhaps another two or three thousand words (bringing the total to four or five thousand)."**
- Pace target, stated on every sample schedule: **average ≥ 8 new-word acquaintances per hour** of session time.
- Per-story calibration: **~5 new words per minute of recorded story is "about ideal"; 10 or fewer is acceptable**; 50 new words in a 2-minute story = too hard.
- Documented Kazakh case: **~2,500 words gained** (≈50 hrs of world fairy tales — about 15 in print — then ≈150 hrs of Bible storybooks, plus ≈50 hrs Phase 2B/C activities).
- Context: "Researchers used to say that good conversational ability in English required about five thousand words. More recent research puts the number considerably higher."
- Kind of knowledge: **listening/recognition vocabulary first** (Iceberg Principle — no memorization; acquaintance now, strength through frequency later). Vocabulary arrives "in blocks organized around the way the host world is itself organized."

**Grammar/structures (all incidental or on-demand — see the guide's "Warning about grammar"):**
- New constructions encountered inside stories and dwelt on during massaging — the worked Cinderella example covers the **causative** ("someone makes someone do something"), plus vocabulary-adjacent structures (widow/divorced kinship talk, "hurt your feelings").
- Structured Input targets (Phase 1-style, repeated as needed): e.g. **"to and from"** grammatical marking via the doll-family-and-candies game.
- Input/Output Flooding targets: **only forms the grower is already trying and failing to produce** — e.g. relative clauses ("the man who is sweeping the street"), "X is trying to…", future/intent forms, and complex subordination via transformed Scripts of Life ("After I walk to the door, I will reach out my hand…").
- The stance: "You'll be encountering lots of new grammar in Phase 3, but be patient. It is generally more advanced than your current speaking ability. Don't panic." Rare forms become common through volume and migrate naturally into speech; Phase 4 adds error-reduction work.

**Discourse skills:**
- Following **rich, complex, well-interconnected stories** (vs. Phase 2's strings of short sentences).
- First sustained **expository discourse** (abstract explanation) — "You have already been hearing some expository speech in massaging stories in Phases 3A and B. In Phase 3C you can start dealing with such abstract speech in larger quantities."
- **Conversation mechanics:** back-channeling, turn-taking, reminiscing as a cultural practice, negotiation of complex meanings.
- First exposure to **native-to-native register** (rapid, unsimplified speech) via retold old stories — training that continues in Phases 4–5.
- Story *telling*: simple narratives, own-life stories, serial epics.

**Cultural / host-world knowledge:**
- **Scripts of Life**: how host people expect everyday activities to unfold, in 25–30-step granularity, from the host point of view (bus riding, buying meat, ordering from a tailor…).
- **How local places are conceived** (Familiar-Place Descriptions of home, street, market, square, routes).
- Host storytelling conventions (openings, closings, style), the host story canon (3B), host interpretations even of Bridge Stories ("the nurturer reflected his Kazakh cultural assumptions in his interpretation" of Bible stories).
- Cultural side-knowledge harvested during massaging (e.g. "If the widow doesn't have children, she may marry her husband's brother. If she has children, she will not marry again.").
- **Matters currently of wide interest** — community events, problems, gossip.
- Friendship formation norms and their variability (the tea-and-sweets door-knocking case study).

**Social expressions:** Lexicarry-style communication situations — many alternative ways to apologise, greet, etc. ("There is more than one way to apologise for knocking someone down."); listener-feedback words; what to say in taxi/shop/police encounters (via role-plays).

## 6. HOW they are learning it — every named activity, with mechanics

**The master loop shared by most activities:** the nurturer tells/reads something *predictable* → it is **sound-recorded** → the grower **massages** the recording sentence-by-sentence (repetition, host-language-only explanation, negotiation of meaning, word-log entries) → the finished recording joins the **Listening Library** → grower **re-listens until it is easy**. "(If a recorded story already exists, go straight to the massaging step.)"

### Core Phase 3A activities

1. **Bridge Story Activity** (THE mainstay; ≥ half of session time; 2.5–4.5 h/day in samples). A Bridge Story belongs to "a third world of fantasy… a bridge world between the host world and our home world" — neither party's world, so the nurturer assumes no host-cultural background, "radically simplif[ying] the growing participator's job." Stock examples: **Cinderella**, Goldilocks, world fairy tales (Grimm, Andersen, Aesop, Arabian Nights, Brer Rabbit), **Bible stories** ("by far the most widely available shared stories… Bible storybooks for children exist in all major languages", pre-recorded in hundreds of languages), and home-culture folk tales told in the host language (e.g. the 10 universally-known stories in *Folk Tales from Korea*). Steps: (1) nurturer tells/reads slowly and carefully; recorded; grower silent. (2) Rewind; play one sentence; if not understood, negotiate: repeat → break down → explain unknown words in host language → explain opaque sentences simply → grower dwells on each new word with examples both directions → nurturer writes the word in the log → move on at basic gist. Repeat per sentence. **Difficulty rubric:** right level ≈ **≤30 min massaging per minute of story and ≤10 new words per minute (5 ideal)**; too hard = 2 hours for a 2-minute story / 50 new words → have the nurturer pre-learn and retell simply, or fall back to Phase 2 wordless-book stories told *without looking at the pictures*; "The activity should be pleasant and interesting"; if slower than the rubric but still enjoyable, continue. Homework: listen a few times before the next session; keep listening for weeks until easy.

2. **Script of Life Activity** (1 h, 2–3 days/week; the other non-postponable core). A Script of Life = a **25–30-step**, blow-by-blow description of an everyday routine **from the host person's point of view** ("similar to what is known as the Series Method. To be a Script of Life, the list of steps must clearly come from the host person's point of view"). **One-time training (first session only):** T1 — grower physically washes his hands, pausing 25–30 times asking "What did I do?/What am I doing?"; nurturer names and writes each micro-step (the guide lists all 27: open the door, turn on the light, walk to the sink … hang the towel, turn off the light, close the door). T2 — nurturer reads her list aloud; record. T3 — massage (nearly everything understood because visually supported — "From now on you won't rely on such visual supports. Rather, you'll rely on the fact that you basically already know the sequence of events"). T4 — grower uses his pre-written "attempted" Script of Life (model: "Using a Bus in Kazakhstan", 27 steps — "Guess where the door will be when it stops", "Put five hundred tenge in his hand", "A man with crutches gets on. Jump up and give him your seat") — *without showing her the text*, negotiating each step in the host language while she writes the host version. After training the grower never authors scripts again. **Normal form:** (1) nurturer lists everything she does in a common activity (e.g. buying meat); (2) walks through it action by action, grower probing "After you do X and before you do Y, what do you do?" / "How do you do step X in smaller steps?"; (3) record the full list; (4) massage (often easier — actions can be pantomimed); (5) **play the recording and pantomime each step as heard**. Topic rule: must be known to both parties first-hand ("A description of an activity you are unfamiliar with would not be a 'Stuff-We-Both-Know'"); a near-familiar stretch like a dentist visit is acceptable; a never-attended wedding goes on the Phase 4 list. Appendix 3 (Rebecca Huston, Malay world) supplies the brainstorm bank: Home (sweeping/mopping, lighting the stove, changing a gas bottle…), Food (making rice/tea/fried noodles, cleaning a fish), Personal care (haircut, shave, doctor/dentist), Errands (paying bills, ATM, recharging a cellphone), Out and About, Clothing (wearing a sarong men's/women's way), Car care (changing a tire), Baby care (changing a nappy), Village life (opening a coconut, feeding chickens).

3. **Action Cartoon Activity** (~1 h, 2 days/week; postponable). Prep: grower watches the cartoon **privately before the session**. (1) Arrange the screen so **the nurturer can see it but the grower cannot** — manufacturing an "**information gap**… the nurturer will feel that she has something to tell you that you don't obviously already know"; she narrates live while watching (may pre-watch silently once); record. (2) Watch together while listening to the recording. (3) Massage; log new words. (4) Nurturer retells **without** the cartoon — "a much more natural story" — **after looking over the word log so she includes all the new words** in this final Listening Library version. (5) Massage the second recording if needed. Media notes: **Tom & Jerry** works from 3A ("Action cartoons are built out of simpler bits of experience"); **Charlie Chaplin** silents are Bridge-Story-like (world removed in time) so usable earlier; **Mr. Bean** "incorporate[s] much British culture… might be better left to Phase 3C."

4. **Shared Experience Activity** (last activity of the day, ~1 h, 2 days/week). Do anything together: coffee shop, shoot baskets, vegetable garden, clean the messy kitchen, buy something, play a game, "go for a walk and observe all you can (this can be repeated by walking a different route each time)". Interact naturally in the host language; make notes afterward. By late Phase 3, occasionally "a whole day or two of doing something together" (weekend outing) → extensive reminiscing.

5. **Reminiscing Activity** (~1 h the morning after a Shared Experience). Grower asks the nurturer to tell what the two did yesterday; **record while interacting** — a *true* reminiscence means the grower back-channels ("yeah / really! / mhm / I see" equivalents), takes brief turns at natural pauses, then hands the floor back. Worked example: N: "We went to a coffee shop." GP: "Yeah." N: "We walked there together. We were talking about the traffic. You said you are afraid to cross the street." GP: "I was afraid." Then massage the recording as usual.

6. **Strengthening Vocabulary Activity** (optional; ~20 min after small talk). The **grower talks**: reminds the nurturer of yesterday's words, putting each back into its context ("I learned 'mean' and 'lazy'. Cinderella's stepsisters were mean… They were lazy. They slept all day." / "I learned 'grind'. You talked about buying meat. Maybe the butcher grinds the meat."). Words may come from any of yesterday's activities. Warning attached: "Be careful with this activity, as it can eat up too much time."

7. **Small Talk** (10–15 min opener, every session, host language only). "Always take an interest in the nurturer's life and feelings, putting that ahead of your other planned activities." See the plateau warning below.

### Optional 3A enrichment ("in the shared-story spirit")

8. **Familiar-Place Description Activity**: nurturer describes a place well known to the grower (home, apartment building, street, nearby market, a shop, the public square, a common route); record → massage → re-listen. Moves the grower "toward understanding the way local places are conceived of by your nurturer."

9. **Discuss-a-Prop / Conversations centred around common objects**: free-ranging interaction around an everyday object while doing things with it — "all the things people do with and to the object" — recording the whole interaction including live negotiation. Anecdotes: an hour on a **table lamp** (burnt-out bulb → electric power → the nurturer led them to the electrical panel and metre) and an hour on a **length of rope** (three growers "tied one another up, dragged one another around, and did everything else imaginable").

10. **Reverse Role-Play**: spontaneous, never memorised. The **nurturer plays the role the grower has in real life** (customer) and the **grower plays the counterpart** (taxi driver, shopkeeper, traffic police officer) "as best as you are able (which won't be very well!)". Point: hearing and recording what the nurturer says in *his* usual role models exactly how he could talk in those situations. Record → massage → re-listen; then days later swap (**Ordinary Role-Play**: grower in his real role, nurturer as counterpart — modeling what he'll *hear*); or start in the ordinary direction with the nurturer assisting.

11. **Phase 2 carry-over activities** — still richly rewarding: Story-Building with the grower in the lead (2A — to talk more), with the nurturer in the lead (2B — to learn more that's new), **Free Cooperative Story-Building** (wordless picture book, both freely contributing; word log along the way; at the end the nurturer tells the whole story with the log as a checklist; record; massage; re-listen), and **Autobiographical Picture Stories (2C)** — sketch-supported own-life stories; the author "would have included it in the essential set" except some people resist sketching; it is "especially valuable to the growth of this personal relationship" (along with Shared Experiences and Reminiscing). *Anno's Journey* is "more suited to Phase 3 than it is to Phase 2."

### Grammar-awareness activities

12. **Structured Input** (Phase 1-style repeats for persistent trouble spots): forces a grammar form to carry meaning. Worked example "to and from": doll family + bag of candies; first "Give a candy to the mother; take a candy from the son"; then the verbs are dropped — "From me; to yourself; from the father; to the baby" — so only the case/adposition marking carries direction.

13. **Input Flooding**: when the grower keeps *trying and failing* to produce a sentence type (e.g. "the man who is…"), expose him to **hundreds of instances**. Use a **busy picture** (Ed King's *William Wanders Off* — 100+ people in the first picture; also *Lucy is Lost*, *Roving Rosie Reports*; Usborne *1001 Things to Spot*; Heather Amery's *The First 1000 Words*; photo albums, National Geographic): the grower shows a few examples of the form; the nurturer produces it 100+ times — pointing ("Here is a man who is…") or better, querying ("**Where is** the man who…?" — "the latter might be better as it forces you to pay attention!"). Record and massage as necessary. **Cardinal rule:** "you do this only with types of sentences and phrases that you find you are actually trying to produce… not with arbitrary grammar patterns" (explicit anti-textbook argument).

14. **Output Flooding**: immediately after input flooding, turn to a **new** busy picture and the **grower** produces the construction over and over. Same cardinal rule.

15. **Scripts of Life as Input/Output Flooding material**: a highly familiar script retold in a transformed frame — habitual → future/intent ("In awhile, I will wash my hands. First I will walk to the bathroom door…") → complex subordination ("After I walk to the door, I will reach out my hand…"). Only with scripts already highly familiar in original form.

### Phase 3B prime activity

16. **Host-Story-as-Shared-Story**: (1) nurturer lists stories **universally known** among her people; (2) someone retells them in English/another strong language of the grower — recorded or written; (3) **homework: listen/read the translation every day for several days** until highly familiar (Thomson's cautionary tale: he read Russian versions of Kazakh texts only right before sessions; months later those recordings "weren't so easy to follow any more" — familiarity must reach world-story level); (4) only then hear it in the host language, record, massage per the Cinderella procedure; (5) re-listen — **more often than Bridge Stories** ("With host stories… down the road it is harder to remember what was being talked about"). Why harder: "There is a vast amount of background knowledge that your nurturer understands and you do not." If too time-consuming, "try postponing it for another fifty hours, and then coming back to it." Pioneers leave recordings for successors.

17. **Make sure the difficulty keeps rising** (3B calibration move): simplified spontaneous tellings → natural tellings → **written stories read aloud** ("longer sentences, more complex sentences, and richer vocabulary") → host stories. Appendix 4 (Rebecca Huston) provides the **12-level Stuff-We-Both-Know difficulty ladder**: 1 wordless books retold without looking at pictures / other GPs' recordings; 2 world stories retold in the nurturer's own words at GP level; 3 silent videos (Tom & Jerry, Mr. Bean, Chaplin) narrated live or described after; 4 local pre-school stories pre-learned in translation, retold; 5 world stories **read aloud** from host-language print; 6 local stories read aloud (pre-learned in translation); 7 more complex/abstract local stories (conflict, misunderstanding, legends, fables); 8 longer translated children's classics read aloud; 9 local children's/young-adult books (nurturer orally translates first if no translation exists, then reads as written); 10 feature-film plot retellings (**the GP** summarizes/retells); 11 abstract youth-level texts (grade 1–2 textbook sections on science/social studies/health) — orally translated first, then read as written; 12 abstract adult-level expository print, same method. "Don't be concerned about mixing difficulty level a bit — for example, doing activities from 2–4 in the same day." For diglossic languages the gap between the spoken levels (1–4, 10) and written levels (5–9, 11–12) widens.

### Phase 3C activities ("beyond literal stories")

18. **Familiar Topic Activity (expository discourse)**: nurturer tells/reads about a topic the grower knows deeply; record/massage/re-listen. "An abstract discussion of a topic that is highly familiar to the listener is often a lot easier to understand than an unfamiliar story." Constraint: the topic must be understood similarly in home and host worlds (arithmetic can fail this test). Sources keyed to vocation/interests: public-health pamphlets (malaria prevention, prenatal health) for health workers; farmer materials; "how to take good care of your car" recorded from a fellow hobbyist; sermons on familiar passages; Thomson's Kazakh trick — parallel Russian/Kazakh children's nonfiction on history, geography, nature, holidays.

19. **Movie Plots**: nurturer tells from memory the plot of a popular movie the grower has seen in his language and she in hers (world hits are dubbed into major languages; minority-language nurturers may have seen it in the regional language or can watch it specially). Record, massage, re-listen.

20. **Book Summary**: same with novels ("classic Russian novels for sale in their English translations" at St. Petersburg State University — "Someone there had discovered the shared-story principle"). Read host novels in translation now for fun; hear them summarised later; read the originals later still.

21. **News Broadcasts**: record TV/radio/Internet news and massage; or nurturer reads a newspaper aloud (Arabic: reads the formal article then **retells it in spoken Arabic**, mixing formal vocabulary in naturally). World news = familiar topics; local news harder — except **serially developing stories (court trials)** where following earlier installments makes new ones predictable. Commitment warning: a few casual hours wastes the vocabulary flood; persistence turns "what seemed like a flood of new words" into "commonplace, familiar friends" and yields a permanent self-serve comprehensible-listening source.

22. **Your Old Stories Retold "Native-to-Native"** (bridge toward Phase 5): the nurturer takes stories she already told the grower and **tells them naturally to another host person, without the grower present, recording herself** — familiar core + familiar vocabulary, but real native-to-native speed and register. Listen together later and massage; re-listen alone. "You might want to try it once toward the end of Phase 3 just to see how much more demanding it is"; continue through Phase 4. (Urdu anecdote: **17 hours** of bazaar-bought children's story cassettes — native-to-native yet familiar world stories — understandable after a few listenings, about a year in, even without a nurturer.)

### Activities to get you talking more (3C, or whenever the grower is too silent)

23. **Story Retelling**: after massaging a story, the grower retells it in his own words at his own level — "Let me see if I understood your story. Here is what I think you said" — deliberately reusing the story's new vocabulary. Best mid-to-late phase; better with Bridge Stories than translation-learned host stories.
24. **Telling Stories from Your Life**: choose an interesting childhood-or-later experience; "Tell the whole story — don't avoid the hard parts. Negotiate meanings as necessary"; then the **nurturer retells it** and her retelling is recorded into the Listening Library; retelling to others later shows measurable improvement each time.
25. **Ongoing "Epic" Stories**: a long, personally important story developed serially — a new installment per meeting, then retold to other people in developing relationships; nurturer records her retelling of each segment; optionally **one scene-setting picture per installment** (not Phase 2's frame-by-frame support); the same epic is retold ever more richly in Phases 4–6.
26. **What I Did on the Weekend (or Yesterday)**: tell it in great detail, hard parts included; nurturer records her retelling. Caveat: "It soon becomes highly repetitive" — reserve it for out-of-the-ordinary days.
27. **Lexicarry Story Strips + busy-book talk**: Patrick Moran's *Lexicarry* — mostly **3-frame** comic strips of common communication situations (bumping into someone and needing to apologise) with **empty speech bubbles** filled orally from imagination. Phase 3 twist: after telling each strip, "make a point of considering many options for what might be said in each of the cartoon bubbles", nurturer suggesting variants; **record a summary of the discussion**. Reusable each phase — "In each phase your discussions of them become richer."
28. **Matters Currently of Wide Interest in the Community**: ask what people are talking about (recent events, community problems, gossip); record the nurturer's opinions; massage; **return to the same topics periodically for updates** (a revisited topic is much easier than a new one); then raise those topics with other people. "Not in the 'Stuff-We-Both-Know' spirit" but a powerful on-ramp.

### Special chores & structural practices

29. **Brainstorming and List-Building**: the four lists (Scripts of Life / Shared Experiences / familiar places & routes / role-play situations); pocket pad carried everywhere; mutual additions with the nurturer; "get serious about this right away."
30. **Producing Bridge Stories** (resource pipeline): free online collections (Grimm, Andersen, Aesop, Arabian Nights); Bible storybooks purchasable in most countries; home-culture collections; the nurturer learns them at home for retelling; **pioneers leave recordings behind** ("as you will do for those who follow after you!"); the LLA gathers GPs' recordings onto a CD and should build a collection of **150+ children's books, silent videos**, and translations in both directions. If no bilingual nurturer exists, "quite a different approach will be required."
31. **Getting Serious Social Life Underway** (3B requirement — see §7 gate): count **3–5 hrs/week** of ordinary social interaction toward the 25 focused hours (no more — don't shrink supercharged sessions further); taxis, neighbour teas, shopkeepers' slow hours, park strangers; the nurturer introduces her close friends; consider a **host family homestay from mid-to-late Phase 3** ("We don't recommend living with a host family much earlier… Often an earlier immersion living situation does not contribute much to growth"), a rural village stay, host roommates, or taking in host student boarders. The tea-and-sweets door-knocking case study shows starting a relationship "may require doing something 'uncultural'."
32. **Starting to Read** (mid-to-late phase; see §8 for the rules): read the massaged familiar stories alone at home; "you may be surprised how quickly you start developing reading fluency."
33. **Small talk — the plateau warning**: small talk = chatting "without much strategy for rapid growth." It is mildly supercharged (the nurturer stays in the growth zone) but "we typically don't grow much through lots of random small talk… It can contribute to the problem many people describe as 'being stuck on a plateau'." Talkative growers risk "develop[ing] a special way of communicating with your nurturer that makes it unnecessary to keep growing steadily." Resolution: a few minutes at session start, plus natural eddies out of activities, plus occasional genuinely important personal sharing — "keeping relationships at centre stage also means keeping growth in view."

## 7. Stages/sub-phases & the grower's state

### Phase 3A — first 100 hours ("Day 3 and subsequent days")
- **Activities:** the Day-2 template — Bridge Stories (mainstay, ≥half of time), Scripts of Life (the other non-postponable), Action Cartoons, Shared Experiences + Reminiscing, optional Strengthening Vocabulary; optional extras (Familiar-Place, Props, Role-Plays, Phase 2 carry-overs, Structured Input, Flooding). "It is O.K. to postpone anything except the Bridge Story activity and the Scripts of Life activity."
- **Grower's state:** understands ~2,000 words; can follow simple stories; talks in simple Phase-2-style sentences ("In your own speech during Phase 3, you'll mainly be using the kinds of sentences that you commonly heard during Phase 2"); comprehension pushed far ahead of production; massaging a story is real work. Socially: "At the beginning of Phase 3… it may still be very difficult for host people to communicate with you, and so you don't need to be concerned if your only really rich relationship at this point is with your nurturer… For now, you can relax." (A second nurturer may be employed.)
- **Chores:** brainstorming/list-building, sourcing Bridge Stories, getting out into the host world for ideas.

### Phase 3B — next 75 hours
- **Prime activity:** **host stories as shared stories** (translation pre-familiarization pipeline). Difficulty deliberately rises (natural tellings, written stories read aloud, the 12-level ladder).
- **Transition:** "need not be abrupt"; if stuck on one or two activities, hold a transition period re-trying the full set (possibly with an LLA) and remake the weekly schedule.
- **Social mandate:** "If your only strong relationship with host people right now is still your relationship with your paid nurturer, it is time to change that." 3–5 hrs/week of real social interaction becomes countable. **HARD GATE: "Consider that you do not graduate from Phase 3B to 3C until you have done this!"** (begun new relationships).
- **Grower's state:** enough conversational ability to branch out; can massage a host story in ~2 hours of a session; still leaning on translations for anything genuinely local; back-channels and turn-takes semi-naturally; communication in new relationships "will still be a struggle, but it is a struggle you need to engage in."

### Phase 3C — final 75 hours
- **Activities:** beyond literal stories — Familiar Topics (expository), Movie Plots, Book Summaries, News Broadcasts, old stories retold native-to-native; talking-more set (Story Retelling, Life Stories, Epics, Weekend recaps, Lexicarry variations, community topics); possibly **starting to read**; homestay consideration; social life must now be deliberate ("you now need to stop making excuses").
- **Grower's state:** may begin to "understand simple host children's stories that are entirely new" (language-pair dependent — "It certainly has not been the case in my experience… but it has been reported to me by growing participators in other language groups"); handles expository speech on familiar topics; "It may be getting easier and easier for you to rattle on talking to your nurturer" — hence the push into harder social contexts; can read massaged stories with quickly developing fluency (non-character scripts). **"Anything goes if it works well, including moving on to Phase 4"** early.

### End-of-phase state
"You are definitely a converser, now! However, you still have far to go as you keep becoming a more host-like converser." Comprehension-only vs. speaking: by now the grower both understands complex stories AND produces simple ones; but native-to-native speech remains mostly out of reach (that's Phase 5's job) and his speech is error-riddled.

## 8. What they need to be LOOKING AT (visual/physical materials)

**The signature move of Phase 3 is *removing* visual support**: after Script-of-Life training step 3 — "From now on you won't rely on such visual supports. Rather, you'll rely on the fact that you basically already know the sequence of events that are being described." Familiarity replaces pictures as the comprehension scaffold. What remains visible:

- **Sound recorder** (the central tool), word-log notebook, pencil and paper, pocket notepad.
- **Action cartoons / silent films** — Tom & Jerry (3A), Charlie Chaplin (earlier OK), Mr. Bean (3C) — with the crucial staging that **during narration only the nurturer sees the screen** (information gap); the grower watches only on the second, watch-together pass. The story-library doc adds **Max7.org "No Language" animations** as streamable Phase-3 shared-story material.
- **Bridge Story source books** — children's storybook versions (host-language print if available); Appendix 2 ships four ready texts (Cinderella, Noah and the Great Flood, Goldilocks and the Three Bears, The First Man and Woman).
- **Wordless picture books** (Phase 2 carry-over + Free Cooperative Story-Building): notably ***Anno's Journey*** ("more suited to Phase 3"); per the story-library catalog, the shelves that reach Phase 3 include *Deep in the Forest* (Brinton — "pairs with Phase 3 Goldilocks"), *Noah's Ark* (Spier — "also Phase 3"), and the Advanced (Phase 3+) tier: *Anno's Journey*, *Free Fall* (Wiesner), *Full Moon Afloat / Full Moon Soup* (Graham), *He Done Her Wrong* (Milt Gross, 1930 graphic novel). Method rule from the library doc: "if a book has text, cover every word — the PICTURES must tell the story." Fallback use: when Bridge Stories are too hard, wordless-book stories are told **without either party looking at the pictures**.
- **Busy pictures / busy books** for flooding: *William Wanders Off*, *Lucy is Lost*, *Roving Rosie Reports* (Ed King); Usborne *1001 Things to Spot*; *The First 1000 Words* (Amery); photo albums, scrapbooks, loose photos, National Geographic, coffee-table books.
- **Lexicarry** (Patrick Moran): ~3-frame communication-situation strips with **empty speech bubbles** (filled orally, never in writing).
- **Real objects/props**: sink, stove, tape recorder, bicycle, car, clock, table lamp, length of rope; the doll family + bag of candies for Structured Input; a **single scene-setting picture** per epic-story installment.
- **The grower's own sketches** for Autobiographical Picture Stories.
- **Translations** (written or audio) of host stories, and later pamphlets/textbooks/parallel-edition children's nonfiction, newspapers.

**Written text — the strict rules:**
1. Reading becomes sensible **only in Phase 3** (earlier there is nothing at level); massaged familiar stories are the ideal first texts; except character-based scripts, a literate adult "can learn to read moderately well in a short time just by doing it a lot."
2. **"Don't cheat by looking at the printed page!"** — during any listening activity, glancing at printed text "short circuits" the listening-comprehension process (print "just sit[s] there motionless" while sound vanishes instantly). Reading happens **alone, at home**.
3. Don't spend paid nurturer time on the grower reading aloud while the tutor nods — that is doable alone; the valuable direction is the nurturer reading aloud **to** the grower.

## 9. How much RECORDING they need to do

**Recording is the backbone of every activity** — "Most of the activities of our supercharged participation sessions depend on, and/or result in, recordings."

**What gets recorded (per activity):**
- Bridge Story: the nurturer's full telling/reading (one recording per story; or reuse a pre-existing recording and skip to massaging).
- Script of Life: the read-through of the full step list (plus, in training, the hand-washing list).
- Action Cartoon: **two recordings** — the live play-by-play narration, then the natural retelling made with the word log in view (the second is the Listening Library keeper).
- Reminiscing: the interactive reminiscence itself.
- Discuss-a-Prop: the **whole live interaction including the negotiation of meaning**.
- Role-plays and Input Floods: recorded and massaged as necessary (both appear in the 3B evening listening list).
- Familiar-Place Descriptions, Familiar Topics, Movie Plots, Book Summaries: the nurturer's telling.
- News: broadcasts recorded off-air, or the nurturer's read-aloud/retelling.
- Talking-more activities: the **nurturer's clean retelling** of the grower's life story / weekend / each epic installment; the Lexicarry variation-discussion summary.
- Native-to-native: the nurturer records **herself, alone with another host person**.
- Word-log stragglers: "If words come up… that are not in the voice recordings we are making and/or using, then it is good to **make a special recording in the host language mentioning those words and the context in which they arose**."

**Volumes and numbers the guide gives:**
- Listening Library trajectory: end of Phase 1 = nonexistent; Phase 2 = "perhaps two or three hours, if that"; **Phase 3 adds "many more hours"** — by the end of 3A the Bridge Story work alone has "added four or five hours of recordings to your listening library."
- Story length calibration implies short recordings massaged long: a 2-minute story ↔ up to ~1 hour of massaging at the ideal rate (≤30 min/story-minute).
- **Re-listening quota: ~15 hours per week** of private listening (full-time plan), i.e. ~3 hours of listening per 5-hour session day. In constrained circumstances, "become extremely fanatical… Listen to the recordings while walking, when waiting at the dentist's office, when sitting in traffic, when trying unsuccessfully to fall asleep. Saturate yourself."
- Re-listen **each new recording a few times before the next session**, then "many times in the coming weeks," **until every item is easy to follow, and even after that… again from time to time** ("Some of the power of this phase results from this practice").
- **Host-story recordings need more re-listening than Bridge Stories** (plots fade; Bridge Story plots never do).
- Frequency rationale: re-listening "refreshes and strengthens" iceberg words — "Some that seem to have vanished from the iceberg will thus be reawakened!"
- Community dimension: recordings should be **left behind for future growers** (they can then "go straight to the massaging step"); the LLA gathers GPs' recordings onto a CD.

## 10. Milestones & phase-specific terminology

### Milestones / progress markers
- Massages a Bridge Story at the healthy rate (≤30 min/story-minute; ≤10 new words/story-minute, 5 ideal) and finds the activity pleasant. (Failing this on entry = "you may not yet be ready for Phase 3.")
- Maintains **8+ new-word acquaintances per hour**; ~2,500 words gained over the phase → **4,000–5,000 total**.
- Every Listening Library recording eventually rated **easy to follow**.
- Understands rich, complex stories without visual support; understands abstract explanations (expository) on familiar topics.
- Converts genuine host stories into understood shared stories via the translation pipeline (3B centerpiece).
- **3B→3C gate: has begun new relationships beyond the paid nurturer** (3–5 hrs/week of real social interaction counting toward the 25).
- Tells simple stories: retells massaged stories, tells whole life stories, maintains a serial epic.
- Uses host-appropriate back-channel/turn-taking behavior; negotiates complex meanings.
- Has sampled a native-to-native retelling at least once near phase end.
- May read familiar massaged stories with growing fluency.
- Can handle and benefit from a host-family homestay (mid-to-late phase).
- "Basic conversational ability… You are definitely a converser, now!" — with acknowledged limits (non-native speech, help still needed, native-to-native mostly opaque).
- Early-exit signal: entirely-new simple host children's stories become understandable.

### Glossary (terms as used by THIS phase's guide)
- **Growing Participator (GP) / grower** — the foreigner becoming an ever-fuller participant in the host world; "This process is never completed."
- **Nurturer** — the paid host person who "kindly, gently, patiently" assists in the growth zone; Phase 3 ideal: a storyteller who can simplify.
- **Growth zone** — activities doable only with a bit of expert help (the Zone of Proximal Development).
- **Supercharged participation sessions** — hired concentrated participation, solving the **catch-22**: "only those who are already able to participate in it will be allowed to participate in it on a scale that will allow them to learn to participate in it."
- **Negotiation of meaning** — clarification requests, repetitions, examples, comprehension checks, re-expression until mutual understanding.
- **Massaging (a story/recording)** — replaying a recording bit by bit and negotiating everything not understood, encountering many new words in the process. (NOTE: in this guide, massaging is **comprehension work done on a recording of the *nurturer's* speech** — not correction of the grower's output.)
- **Bridge Story** — a story from a "third world" (fantasy/world stories, Bible stories) belonging to neither party, slashing the host-background-knowledge load.
- **World story** — a widely-though-not-universally known story (Cinderella is "our stock example").
- **Script of Life** — a host person's 25–30-step account of what host people expect to happen in a common activity (akin to the Series Method); an **"attempted" Script of Life** is the grower's own training mock-up (not the real thing — "only a host person can make up Scripts of Life").
- **Stuff-We-Both-Know** — the umbrella label for Phase 3's shared-knowledge activities.
- **Shared story** — any story made mutually known in advance (familiarity, prior viewing, translation prep, shared experience).
- **Iceberg Principle** — words enter a mental iceberg by acquaintance, not memorization; frequent re-encounters raise them toward the usable top; "We try to put thousands of words into our iceberg."
- **Word log** — the running acquaintance record (context noted, optional pencilled gloss); "not for memorisation."
- **Listening Library** — the cumulative archive of understandable recordings, ordered by rising complexity; re-listening "reawakens" faded words.
- **The importance of frequency** — processing strength tracks encounter frequency at every level; motivates massive comprehensible exposure.
- **Languaculture** — language and culture as one inseparable way of living.
- **Expository discourse** — abstract explanatory (non-narrative) speech; the 3C frontier and Phase 4 staple.
- **Information gap** — staging (hiding the cartoon screen) so the nurturer genuinely has something the grower doesn't know.
- **Input Flooding / Output Flooding** — hearing, then producing, hundreds of instances of one target construction the grower is already reaching for.
- **Busy picture / busy book** — a scene drawing with an unnaturally large number of people doing things (100+ in *William Wanders Off*).
- **Reverse Role-Play** — role-play with roles inverted so the nurturer models the grower's own real-life part.
- **Reminiscing** — jointly recalling a shared experience with host-style back-channels.
- **Communities of practice** — host groups "united in purpose and activities, and united by their shared history and common practices" — the eventual participation target.
- **LLA (Language Learning Advisor)** — coach who helps design schedules and gathers phase resources (target: 150+ books/videos, two-way translations, a CD of shared GP recordings).
- **Small talk / chitchat** — relationship chat; mandatory opener, plateau trap as a main course.
- **Speech-led vs. understanding-led** — traditional speaking-first/writing-dependent learning vs. GPA's deliberate comprehension-first, listening-dependent stance ("we push our listening ability far ahead of our speaking ability, quite deliberately").
- **Host world / home world** — the target community's life-world vs. the grower's origin world.

## App vs. source-guide deltas

The Phase 3 entry in `src/lib/phases.ts` (id 3, slug `knowable`, "Becoming Knowable", 250 h, startHour 250, vocabTarget "+2,000 words → ~4,000 total") gets the shell right, but diverges from Thomson in several substantive ways:

1. **"Massaging" is inverted — the biggest delta.** The app's description says "The nurturer 'massages' your retellings — re-expressing them in better host-language form," and the `p3-massage` activity ("Record-and-Massage Loop") says "You retell today's story onto the recorder; the nurturer re-expresses it… never correcting, just saying it the way an insider would." In the guide, **massaging is the grower working through a recording of the *nurturer's* telling, sentence by sentence, negotiating meaning** — it is a listening-comprehension mechanic, not output-recasting. (The nearest guide analog to the app's loop is "Telling Stories from Your Life," where the nurturer retells the grower's story for a clean recording — a minor 3C talking-more activity, not the phase engine.) Any feature built from phases.ts as-is would train the wrong loop.
2. **Bridge Story mechanics wrong-way-round too.** App: "The nurturer tells it, you retell it, the nurturer massages your version, and you re-record the better telling." Guide: nurturer tells (grower **silent**), recording is made, grower massages the recording, then re-listens as homework. Grower retelling is a separate optional activity (Story Retelling), explicitly "best in the mid to late stages of Phase 3."
3. **Action Cartoon loses the information gap.** App: "Watch together; the nurturer narrates." Guide: the screen must be arranged so **the grower cannot see it** during narration; watching together happens on the second pass; and there are **two recordings** (live narration + natural retelling seeded from the word log). The app also lists Mr. Bean alongside Tom & Jerry, while the guide defers Mr. Bean to Phase 3C because of its cultural load.
4. **Scripts of Life: wrong examples and missing rules.** App gives "getting married" as an example topic; the guide (Appendix 3) explicitly flags weddings as **not yet eligible** unless personally experienced ("If you have not yet attended a wedding, don't try to do this as a script of life yet" — it's a Phase 4 topic). The app's "then describe your home world's version back" has no basis in the guide (the grower's home-world script exists only in the one-time training step 4, never shown or told again). Missing: the 25–30-step granularity, the four training steps, the completeness probes ("After you do X and before Y…?"), the pantomime playback (step 5), and the host-viewpoint authorship rule.
5. **Sub-phases don't match.** App has two parts: "3a Massaged Stories ~125h" and "3b Stuff We Both Know ~125h" (with Shared Experience, Props, Flooding and Host Stories all placed in 3b). Guide: **3A = 100 h** (all core activities including Shared Experiences from Day 1), **3B = 75 h** (host stories + the social mandate), **3C = 75 h** (expository discourse, news, native-to-native, talking-more, reading). "Stuff We Both Know" is the name of the *whole phase*, not of a sub-part.
6. **The 3B→3C social gate is absent.** The guide's hard requirement — new relationships beyond the paid nurturer before graduating 3B, with 3–5 countable social hours/week, homestay guidance, and concrete tactics — has no app counterpart (the milestone "Host people can genuinely get to know you" gestures at it but encodes no gate).
7. **Missing activities** (in the guide, absent from phases.ts): Reminiscing as a distinct recorded-and-massaged activity with back-channel training (the app folds it into `p3-shared` as "talk about it again and again"), Strengthening Vocabulary (20-min cap), Familiar-Place Descriptions, Reverse/Ordinary Role-Play, Output Flooding, Structured Input, Familiar Topic (expository) work, Movie Plots, Book Summaries, News Broadcasts, native-to-native retellings, Story Retelling, Telling Stories from Your Life, Epic serial stories, Weekend recaps, Lexicarry variation work, community-topics tracking, brainstorming/list-building chores, the Bridge-Story production pipeline, the small-talk opener + anti-chitchat guardrail, and the reading rules ("don't read while listening"; don't pay a nurturer to hear you read aloud).
8. **Missing quantitative machinery:** the 8-new-words/hour target; the difficulty rubric (≤30 min massaging per story-minute, 5–10 new words/story-minute, "pleasant not tedious"); the 25 h/week sessions + 15 h/week listening split; Bridge Story ≥ half of session time; Appendix 4's **12-level difficulty ladder**; the several-days translation-familiarity requirement before a host story (the app's `p3-host` does say "Days before the session you soak in a translation… re-listen to these recordings often" — this one is faithful); the "postpone 50 hours" retry rule; the early-exit-to-Phase-4 rule.
9. **Word log and Listening Library mechanics are thin.** The app description says "each massaged story becomes new listening material" (good) but encodes no word log (nurturer-written, context-noted, non-memorization), no re-listen-until-easy tracking, no special recordings for out-of-recording words, and no pioneers-leave-resources sharing model.
10. **Minutes are session-scaled.** App activities carry 15–30-minute blocks vs. the guide's 2.5–4.5-hour Bridge Story blocks in 5-hour days. Reasonable as an app-session simplification, but the guide's *proportions* (Bridge Story dominates; vocab warm-up strictly capped; small-talk opener) aren't preserved: in the app the Bridge Story (30 min) equals the Shared Experience block and is barely larger than the cartoon (20).
11. **Vocab target slightly understated:** app says "+2,000 → ~4,000 total"; guide says +2,000–3,000 → **4,000–5,000** total (documented case +2,500).
12. **Discuss-a-Prop drift (minor):** app frames it as an object "with a story… who made it, what happened with it"; the guide's version is about *doing things with* the object and discussing "all the things people do with and to the object" (lamp/rope anecdotes).
13. **Principles list:** the app's three principles capture shared-knowledge comprehensibility and extended discourse, but principle 2 ("Retell, get massaged, re-record, re-listen") repeats the inverted-massaging error, and the phase's actual signature principles — host-language-only/no-translation in session, Iceberg Principle, frequency/re-listening, information gap, grammar-only-on-demand, relationship-before-curriculum with the plateau warning — are unrepresented at Phase 3 level.
14. **Correct matches worth keeping:** name/hour placement (250 h starting at hour 250), the "stuff-we-both-know" tagline, Bridge Story as "the mainstay," host-story pre-loading via translation, flooding as "grammar experience" not rules, shared experience → reminiscing pairing, and the milestone "Converse on diverse everyday topics like a (simple) adult" (≈ "basic conversational ability").
