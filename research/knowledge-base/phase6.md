# Phase 6 — Ever Participating, Ever Growing / "Self-Sustaining Growth" (Deep Knowledge Base)

**Primary source:** Greg Thomson, *"Balancing Your Competing Lives: A Guide to Phase 6 and Phase un-6, for People in 'Life after Full-Time Language Learning'"*, Version 1.0, October 10, 2012 (`/Volumes/LaCie/GPA_Language_Learning/research/phase6.txt`, 1,739 lines).
**Secondary sources:** `phase6` key of `/Volumes/LaCie/GPA_Language_Learning/research/gpa-research.json`; app encoding at `/Volumes/LaCie/GPA_Language_Learning/src/lib/phases.ts` (entry `id: 6`, lines 614–733).

**Guide epigraph and thesis, repeated as a mantra throughout:** *"It's not a language to be learned, but a life to be lived."*

---

## 1. Phase identity & goals

### Names
- App/GPA phase name: **"Ever Participating, Ever Growing"** (app slug `ever-growing`); the guide itself calls the phase **"Self-Sustaining Growth"** ("the phase we call 'Self-Sustaining Growth'").
- The guide is explicitly a **double guide**: it covers **Phase 6** AND its shadow, **"Phase un-6"** — the condition of people "beyond full-time language learning" who are *not* on the self-sustaining road.

### Hour position
- Phase 6 begins **after the recommended 1,500 hours** of special-growth participation activities in Phases 1–5:
  - Phase 1: Connecting (Here-and-Now Communication) — 100 hours
  - Phase 2: Emerging (Story Building) — 150 hours
  - Phase 3: Knowable (Shared Stories) — 250 hours
  - Phase 4: Deep Personal Relationships (Deep Life Sharing) — 500 hours
  - Phase 5: Widening Understanding (also called Native-to-Native Discourses) — 500 hours
- Phase 6 itself is **open-ended: no fixed hours, no end**. "Phase 6 is not really a 'next stage' after Phases 1 through 5. Phase 6 is a road that we hope to find ourselves on." The 1,500 hours are *hoped* to put you on that road, "if not, they should at least put us within reach of it."
- The 1,500 hours may take "a year and a half or more—sometimes a lot more." There is a **downward viability limit**: "A newcomer spending only two or three hours a week in participation activities is not really a growing participator in any meaningful sense. It won't help them to tell them, 'Anything is better than nothing.' Rather, we want them to know that it won't work."

### The two-condition definition of Phase 6 (verbatim structure from the guide)
> "1) You have the ability to understand most of what you hear host people say (and see them do) around you. In addition…
> 2) You do, in fact, keep hearing host people saying a lot around you (and see them doing a lot)."

"This results in constant growth because day by day you become increasingly familiar with how host people talk and otherwise live." Critically: **if only condition 2 fails, that is "relatively fixable" (a lifestyle problem); if condition 1 fails, "you have a much bigger challenge."**

### Definition of Phase un-6
> "1) You are not in Phase 6, either because part 1 or part 2 of the Phase 6 definition doesn't hold.
> 2) You are devoting little or no time to concentrated 'language learning activities' (special-growth participation activities) and hence you aren't in Phases 1 through 5 either."

In Phase un-6, much of what host people say is still "noise" to you, and "you are not likely to grow a lot without many more hours of special effort."

### Relationship to proficiency levels (ILR / FSI scale, 0–5)
- Phase 6 is **not** a proficiency level. "The point is not where they are, but whether they will keep growing indefinitely."
- Someone *just barely* on the Phase 6 road would be rated **at least ILR Level 2** ("Limited Working Proficiency"), most likely **Level 2+**, possibly Level 3.
- Phase un-6 people plateau at **"Terminal 1+"** or **"Terminal 2+"** — over years, host people watching a video from today vs. five years ago "might have a hard time guessing which was yesterday and which was five years ago. That is the meaning of 'terminal.'"

### What "done" looks like — it doesn't
- The mountain-climbing metaphor: every "summit" reveals a higher peak. The convenience goal the guide adopts is **ILR Level 4 ("Near-Native / Advanced Professional Proficiency")**, illustrated by the "tent pegs" test — a Level 4 speaker knows virtually all normal words universally known among host people (an Egyptian English-user saying "big nails" instead of "tent pegs/tent stakes" was capped at Level 3, per Liskin-Gasparro 1987).
- Leaver & Atwell's research (50+ verified Level-4 attainers): almost no adult starter ever reaches Level 4; those who do average **17 years** of serious language use (shortenable to **~6 years** with intensive intervention); **40%** were/had been married to host people using the host language as the marriage language; **76%** earned a university degree in the host country; **86%** were extensive readers in the host language; **100%** had lived in a multilingual environment during childhood. Six of the 50+ were Level 5; some Level-5s grew mainly in **immigrant/diaspora communities in America** without extensive host-country residence.
- Being in Phase 6 "means to have the opportunity to grow and grow and grow, right to the end of your sojourn." Being in Phase un-6 "means to miss out on the beautiful journey deeper and deeper into host life."

### Goals of the phase
1. **Stay on the road**: keep both Phase 6 conditions true. "If you are in Phase 6, and want to keep growing, you need do nothing. However, lifestyle issues can take you right out of Phase 6."
2. **Give host life a fair share of the ~100 waking hours per week** (16 h × 7 = 112): at least 10%, better 20%+. "Most of us … often spend less than ten per cent of our time truly in the host world."
3. **Optional acceleration** (Phase 6): "you might choose to do special-growth participation activities for perhaps five hours a week in order to accelerate your growth."
4. **Escape the plateau** (Phase un-6): accumulate at least **100 hours** (low levels) or **300 hours** (higher levels, incl. Terminal 1+/2+) of genuine special-growth activity to produce a **"new you"** detectable by host people.
5. Become host-like in specific high-value **host discourses** chosen by needs analysis; find and fill **holes**; move from one-on-one foreigner-directed talk into **host communities of practice**; extend growing participation to **couples, families, teams, businesses, NGOs, schools**.

## 2. The instructions — how sessions and the phase itself are structured

Phase 6 deliberately has **no prescribed session curriculum**. Thomson: "In the guides to Phases 1 through 5 … we tried to give a lot of step-by-step information for each activity in each phase. In this section I've reverted to giving some principles and a couple of examples! That is because the range of possible communication situations in which you may want to become more host-like is practically limitless, and will vary from one languacultural world to the next."

The operative structures are:

### (a) The weekly lifestyle budget (the real "session plan" of Phase 6)
- ~**100 waking hours/week** (16×7=112). Ask: "What percentage of your waking week belongs to the host people—lived in their world, not yours?"
- Benchmark: **≥10%, ideally ≥20%** in host life. (Filipino/Ghosa example: "Can they give at least 10% (or better, 20%) of those 100 waking hours per week to living their Ghosa life? If they are in Phase un-6, can at least five of those hours be special-growth?")
- Phase 6 optional acceleration: **~5 h/week** of special-growth activities.
- Phase un-6: "many hours per week in special-growth activities"; 2–3 h/week explicitly "won't work."

### (b) The discourse-mastery sequence (the repeatable session template)
For each target communication situation chosen by needs analysis, the guide's fully-worked sequence (assembled from the Toast-Making, Getting-Car-Fixed and Using-Beauty-Parlor examples) is:

1. **Prelude — Phase 1 techniques for vocabulary fill**: go to the real object/venue or a diagram; use the Phase 1 **Dirty Dozen** technique for objects ("Where is the lug wrench?") and actions ("Loosen the lug nut."); do **listen-and-point** activities and *record them*.
2. **Stage A — GP (grower) in the lead**, for **10–20 hours**:
   - Step 1: grower performs the discourse act (a toast, a fault description, a "what she said to the beautician" guess), **recording the attempt live** ("Don't worry if you hem and haw").
   - Step 2: go through the recording with the nurturer "in the spirit of the GPA activity **Record Yourself for Feedback**," keeping track of all suggested improvements; much of this is **negotiating meanings** — helping the nurturer see what you were trying to say.
   - Step 3: **record the nurturer performing the revised version of the grower's own text**; add to the **Listening Library**; "Listen to it before the next meeting, and many times after, especially when you are expecting to make a toast."
3. **Stage B — Nurturer in the lead** (after 10–20 h of A; "We find that this 'A-B' pattern keeps the interaction in your growth zone"):
   - Step 1: nurturer performs a fresh example from the brainstormed list; record it.
   - Step 2: **massage** the recording; add to Listening Library.
   - Step 3: listen to it before the next meeting.
   - Step 4: at the next meeting, the grower performs a *similar* act "drawing on the one the nurturer made the previous meeting, but not repeating it verbatim, but rather, as in all 'retelling tasks,' do it in your own words, reflecting what you are personally able to do at the time."
4. **Scripts of Life** (Phase 3 technique reapplied): nurturer describes the whole venue experience "in small steps"; expand/massage as needed; next session the grower tells it in own words.
5. **Role-play**: "have your nurturer pretend she is you, and you are the beautician. She acts out the entire visit… You can then switch roles."
6. **Shared experience + Reminiscing (capstone)**: actually go to the venue *with* the nurturer; make small talk with the host professional; "Return home with your nurturer and do the Reminiscing Activity from Phase 3." Result: "You have now gone a long way toward becoming a host-like participant in the host discourse of beauty parlors."

### (c) Block targets — the 100/300-hour rule (Leaver)
- "To change in a way that is detectable will take **at least 100 hours** if you are at a low level of proficiency, and **300 hours** if you are at a higher level of ability (which might include Terminal 1+ and 2+)."
- These must be "**teacher-mediated exposure and practice**" — Leaver: "a 'teacher' does not have to be a foreign language teacher… We request subject matter professors who do not know English and who have never taught their language… the words 'at least' are very important. Some folks take much more — and without teacher mediation it can take years."
- Generalised 5 characteristics any qualifying hour must have: (1) speech you understand but that challenges you; (2) heavy interaction, clarifying and negotiating meanings; (3) listening vocabulary growing by the hour ("we might aim for 3000 new listening vocabulary in the 300 hours"); (4) constantly learning more about the host world as host people know it; (5) much talking, with help expressing thoughts more host-likely.
- **If 300 hours is impossible**: "you can grow in particular areas of communication in a smaller period of time, such as **twenty or thirty hours**" per discourse.
- **Anti-pattern warning**: "I have seen people pay a lot of money to be with a teacher at a language centre only to sit there and carry on the same sort of unstructured conversation they had with their house helper earlier in the day, and then with the taxi driver on the way to the language centre. Doing more of the same with a paid nurturer is not a strategy for heavy change and growth."

### (d) Between-session homework
Re-listen to Listening Library items (always before the next meeting), massage recordings/broadcasts, read extensively on one's own, watch/record challenging TV, carry hundreds of hours of recordings on an mp3 player "to redeem the time on buses and sleepless nights."

## 3. What the NURTURER does

### Role shift across the programme, landing in Phase 6
- "In the early days … a host person who is willing to play with them interactively while talking to them… Later, the nurturer plays with us less and less, and **explains life to us more and more**." By Phase 6 the nurturer is an **explainer, discourse coach, and subject-matter professor** rather than a play partner.
- Leaver's ideal higher-level nurturer: a **content instructor who does not know the grower's language and has never taught their own language**, who "treats me pretty much like a local student, with the major difference that helping me to grow in my communication ability is also central to his understanding of his role."

### Concrete duties in Phase 6 activities
- Brainstorm discourse lists and run the **needs analysis** with the grower (together with other expats and host people).
- **Review the grower's recorded attempts** (Record-Yourself-for-Feedback): track every needed improvement while **negotiating meanings** to understand what the grower intended.
- **Record the improved version of the grower's own text** — pointedly NOT her own spontaneous version: "This is more effective, we find, than simply having the nurturer tell you what she would have said… Her fully spontaneous version may not feed your growth at this point as much as her improvements of your version." (The nurturer's version "founded on your version keeps it in your growth zone.")
- In **B-stages**: model the discourse from scratch on recording for the grower to massage and later retell in own words.
- Narrate **Scripts of Life** in small sequential steps.
- Play both roles in **role-plays** (including playing the grower while the grower plays the host professional).
- **Accompany the grower into real venues** (the beauty-parlor visit) and lead **Phase 3 Reminiscing** afterward.
- In the reading programme: **read the same materials as the grower on her own**, then discuss them together; discuss the new words the grower met; these discussions are recorded.

### Nurturer selection
- "Note that all nurturers are not created equal when the activities involve such special discourses. In Russia and Kazakhstan, there were people who could only make a toast with a string of clichés, and others who were more eloquent. You might look for a nurturer in the latter category." — pick a nurturer **eloquent in the target discourse**.

### Nurturer don'ts
- Don't use or know the grower's language (ideal case), don't translate.
- Don't carry on unstructured everyday chat (the language-centre anti-pattern above).
- Don't listen to the grower read aloud: "Reading aloud to a paid nurturer is a pretty clear waste of money!"
- Don't hand over spontaneous native versions detached from the grower's attempt (out of the growth zone).
- Don't let sessions drift out of the 5 special-growth characteristics.

### How nurturer behaviour changes across the phase
Early in a discourse track, the nurturer is a *responder/improver* (A-stage); after 10–20 hours she becomes the *model* (B-stage); later still she becomes *co-experiencer* (venue visits, reminiscing) and *reading-club partner*. Across Phase 6 as a whole, the nurturer increasingly resembles a professor of content who happens to also nurture communication — and much of the grower's growth eventually needs **no nurturer at all** (mass media, reading, everyday host life), the nurturer remaining for feedback loops, massaging help, and discussion.

## 4. What the PARTICIPANT (grower/GP) does

### In sessions
- Leads Stage A: performs whole discourse acts (elaborate toasts — "Make sure you actually do the whole act of toasting including what is said and what is done nonverbally"; you may "use milk to make the toasts, if you prefer!"), recording as they go, accepting agony/hemming/hawing.
- Reviews own recordings with the nurturer; negotiates meanings; keeps track of every improvement suggestion.
- In Stage B: **massages** the nurturer's recordings; **retells in own words** — never verbatim from rote memory.
- Performs role-plays (both roles), tells scripts-of-life back, does real-venue visits and reminiscing.
- Brainstorms (with nurturer, fellow expats, host people) lists of communication situations and special-growth activities for each; scores the needs analysis.
- Runs **hole finding** (live-narrating silent films).
- Interviews host specialists (Phase 4 style) as a low-creativity fallback: "interview a famous toastmaster, an auto mechanic and a beautician… this alone has a strong impact."

### Response modes
By Phase 6 the grower's response modes are almost fully productive: extended monologue performance (toasts, fault descriptions), interactive negotiation of meaning, own-words retelling, role-play dialogue, small talk with real host professionals, discussion of shared reading. Early-phase modes (pointing, physical response) reappear **only** in the vocabulary Prelude ("listen and point activities" around the car).

### Homework / self-directed work (a defining feature of Phase 6)
- **Listen to Listening Library items before every next meeting** and "many times after, especially when you are expecting to make a toast."
- **Massage recordings/videos** solo (pausing, rewinding, looking up words). Thomson's model: "at least two hours per day" for several months massaging Russian videos in Canada — after which "in the most challenging multi-person Russian conversational contexts my mental language processor was racing right along."
- **Extensive reading** on one's own ("promiscuously"), with a **monolingual host dictionary**, then recorded discussion with the nurturer.
- **Record and re-watch challenging broadcasts**: "you should really jump at anything you hear on T.V. or elsewhere that is challenging for you and try to record such material."
- **mp3 player** with hundreds of hours of recordings for buses and sleepless nights.
- **Lifestyle work**: auditing and restructuring the week — workplace, residence, social life, courses (see §6, Lifestyle portfolio). This is homework in the largest sense: the guide devotes more pages to it than to activities.

### Attitude work
- Refuse "they stories"; refuse the "expat magnet" ("Refuse to use English with fellow expats who approach you in such a host setting. Talk to them warmly, but only in the host language"); persist in the host language even when host people answer in English ("I've held many a conversation in which the other person persisted for a long time using English with me, and I persisted longer in using their language. And I don't believe I have offended anyone so far").

## 5. WHAT they are learning

### Vocabulary targets (numbers)
- Baseline from Phases 1–5: a **listening vocabulary of over 10,000 words**.
- Ongoing growth in Phase 6: "Our mental store of listening vocabulary grows by the hour (perhaps **five to ten words an hour**)" during qualifying participation.
- Normal Six-Phase goal: **7–8 new words per hour**; for the remedial 100/300-hour push: "since these 300 hours that you are doing are 'remedial,' let's up the goal to putting **ten new words per hour** into your mental store of listening vocabulary" → **~3,000 new listening words in 300 hours**.
- Phase 4 interviews "often do not bring in a lot of new vocabulary," so splice in earlier-phase activities (e.g., a Phase 2 "busy picture") "to bring up your hourly average to ten new words per hour."
- Even admired "advanced" growers have "a ton of 'holes'": one encountered **dozens of new words in a 5–10-minute Cinderella telling**. The Level-4 bar includes universally-known phrases ("tent pegs").
- Domain vocabulary via preludes: complete lexical coverage of chosen domains (every internal/external car part: fuel injector, water pump, lug wrench, lug nut…).

### Grammar / structures
Phase 6 has no explicit grammar syllabus (consistent with GPA's rejection of "Latin-sounding terms like 'accusative case' and 'subjunctive'"). Structural growth comes from: parsing practice (grouping words into phrases/sentences at speed), the nurturer's improvements of grower productions, and exposure to formal/academic registers via reading and media. The fluency-illusion arithmetic is taught explicitly: with 500 words you use each word ~40 times per 20,000 words spoken and sound fluent fast; with 10,000 words each gets used ~2 times — "the cost of growing."

### Discourse skills (the heart of Phase 6 learning)
- Whole **host discourses**: "what host people are saying about a topic, what they agree on and take for granted, and ways in which they may disagree," plus "all the physical objects, actions, statuses and roles of people… associated with particular communication situations."
- Named target discourses (examples): **toasting** (occasions: wedding, anniversary, birthday, housewarming; toastees: a major political figure, an employer, an employee, an ordinary neighbour, a young person, mothers, mothers-in-law, janitors, New Zealanders), **getting the car repaired**, **using the beauty parlor**; skipped by the worked needs analysis: getting a taxi (already skilled), playing football (low value).
- Rhetorical/organisational skill up to the ILR-4 description: "Organizes discourse well, using appropriate rhetorical speech devices, native cultural references."
- Following **host-to-host** speech (not foreigner-directed) — multi-person, colloquial, high-energy conversation; formal registers (news, public addresses, academic prose).

### Cultural knowledge
- Host values revealed through discourse work ("in terms of what it reveals about host values, it [toasting] should be a gold mine").
- How host people *experience* their world — the guide insists that in wordless films "every action is meaningful within the languaculture of the movie, and how many actions, objects and situations are understood differently by your host people than they are by your home people."
- Group life: how host people relate **to one another** ("not host people relating to a foreigner, but host people relating to one another, largely ignoring my foreignness").
- Host office life, host domestic-employment norms, host visiting culture, the host people's own linguistic repertoire (e.g., a minority group's use of the national language).

### Social expressions & pragmatics
Full pragmatic acts with nonverbals (the toast as a complete performance), small talk with professionals (beautician banter), politeness rules of language choice (the "if a host person who doesn't share the language is present, using another language is rude" principle).

## 6. HOW they are learning it — every named activity/technique

1. **Needs Analysis** — a scored brainstorm to pick discourse targets. Brainstorm a long list of communication situations with nurturer + expats + host people. For each: score **frequency 0–5** (0=never, 5=often), **importance-when-it-comes-up 0–5**, then **subtract 0–10 for existing skill** (0=can't do at all, −10=highly skilled). Sum. Worked example from the guide: Getting a taxi 5+5−10=**0**; Making toasts 1+5−2=**4**; Getting car repaired 2+3−1=**4**; Using beauty parlor 3+5−1=**7**; Playing football 2+2−5=**−1**. High totals = targets. Rationale triple: things you (1) can't do well, (2) do infrequently (so daily life never trains them), yet (3) are of utmost urgency before an audience when they occur.
2. **Toast-Making-A** (GP in the lead) — brainstorm occasions/toastees (better: real situations, "a toast you might have given"); perform whole elaborate toasts incl. nonverbals; record live; review via Record-Yourself-for-Feedback; record nurturer's *revised version of your toast*; Listening Library; relisten before next meeting and before real toasts. **10–20 hours before switching to B.**
3. **Toast-Making-B** (nurturer in the lead) — nurturer composes/performs a toast from your list; record; **massage**; Library; listen before next meeting; then make a *similar* toast in your own words, never verbatim.
4. **Getting-Car-Fixed Prelude** — Phase 1 techniques on a new domain: at the real car or a full parts diagram, the **Dirty Dozen** technique for objects and actions; **listen-and-point** with recordings ("Which one is four-wheel drive? Which is a four-door?").
5. **Getting-Car-Fixed-A** — record-for-feedback yourself telling all the things that might go wrong with each learned part; review; record nurturer's final corrected version; Library.
6. **Getting-Car-Fixed-B** — nurturer does the same task on parts *not yet covered*; record; massage; Library; listen before next meeting; retell in own words.
7. **Using-Beauty-Parlor-A** — with hairstyle magazines: say what the pictured person might have said to the beautician; record ("It may be agony for you"); review + negotiate meanings; record the nurturer's *improved version of what you said* ("not what she would have said"); repeat with many pictures; listen before next meeting. Stated design insight: her version "founded on your version keeps it in your growth zone."
8. **Using-Beauty-Parlor-B** — with fresh pictures, nurturer says what *she* would say; record; massage; repeat; listen; retell in own words.
9. **Using-Beauty-Parlor — Scripts of Life** (Phase 3 reapplied) — nurturer describes the whole visit in small steps; expand/massage; next session grower tells it in own words.
10. **Using-Beauty-Parlor — Role-play** — nurturer plays *you*, you play the beautician, acting out the entire visit; then switch roles.
11. **Using-Beauty-Parlor — Shared experience + Reminiscing** — real visit with the nurturer along; small talk with the beautician; return home and do the **Phase 3 Reminiscing Activity**. (Capstone of the 5-part discourse template.)
12. **Phase 4-style Expert Interviewing** (the low-creativity fallback) — deep-interview a famous toastmaster, an auto mechanic, a beautician, "or whatever walk of life you are interested in"; "this alone has a strong impact on your ability to communicate and otherwise act in a more host-like way." Splice in earlier-phase activities when new-word rate sags.
13. **Hole Finding** — "you deliberately find things you can't say. A common version is to watch a silent movie, and try to narrate the plot right as you watch, including talking about the setting, activities, goals, motives, etc." Films named: **Charlie Chaplin, Mr. Bean, and *Kelin*** (Kazakh Film, ~80 min, no dialogue, full plot, "fully intelligible only to contemporary Central Asians"). Bonus: reveals how host meanings differ from home meanings. "Fun and rewarding for those who thought they were more advanced than they actually were." Applies to anyone below ILR 4–5.
14. **Remedial pickup of earlier-phase activities** (the Phase un-6 prescription) — "If you quit the Six-Phase Programme in some particular phase, I would recommend that you go back and pick up from there." For a Terminal 2+ who never did the programme: all of Phases 2–5, or at least 2–4 (Phase 5 "might be over your head"). Named picks: **Phase 2 Story Building with picture stories** ("always useful (even in Phase 6)" — burst of listening vocabulary, perhaps fluency); **Phase 3 Scripts-of-Life** (vocabulary infusion); **Phase 3 World Stories** (abstract vocabulary + discourse devices you've been filtering out — the Cinderella example); **Phase 3 Reminiscing** (how host people experience their world); then concentrate on **Phase 4 Deep Life Sharing interviews**. At a very low level, "you might even consider doing Phase 1 as your first 100 hours to a new you." Target ten new words/hour.
15. **Record Yourself for Feedback** — carried over from earlier phases; the backbone of every A-stage.
16. **Massaging recordings/videos** — "listening intensively to videos, pausing, rewinding, looking up unfamiliar words, and going on." Thomson: 2+ h/day for several months on Russian videos; also his imagined Kazakh version: TV programme in one browser window, online dictionary open in a second — though solitary massaging "counts" only atop a live social foundation ("Such solitary activities were effective, I believe, only because of the level of ability I had already reached through face-to-face interaction").
17. **Listening Library** — accumulating collection of all revised toasts, nurturer texts, massaged broadcasts, word-discussion recordings; replayed many times, "especially when you are expecting to" perform the discourse.
18. **Mass-media listening at optimal difficulty** — radio, TV, video, documentaries, news, "fiction, children's school textbooks, university school textbooks, magazines, newspapers, product instructions." Works only once comprehension processes can actually run ("Research has shown that children do not learn a language by hearing it over the radio. Once they know enough, though…"). Choose material at your challenge level: Thomson's watchable **sitcom** vs. massage-worthy **talk show** ("colloquial, spontaneous, high-energy multi-person conversations about life problems" — "especially valuable in Phase 6, as it was still challenging"). News is late-Phase-3+ material (background knowledge helps). Caveat: listening improves listening; speaking needs interactive speaking — "Interacting conversationally changes people!" Media is a supplement, not a substitute ("far easier to buy a book or DVD… than to change your workplace" — beware the easy path).
19. **Extensive reading + discuss-with-nurturer** — read on your own; nurturer reads the same materials on her own; discuss together; **record the word discussions** "to further refresh and strengthen those words in your mental listening vocabulary store." Use a **monolingual host dictionary**. Start as soon as any readable-for-you materials exist ("Struggling with materials way over our heads is not a good use of time"). Reading "takes you from the area of everyday language to more academic language." **Diglossia case (Arabic)**: written ≠ spoken; learn to read via extensive *spoken*-language discussion of the written text with the nurturer (like teacher–student talk about Latin/Greek) — this also builds formal/news/public-address comprehension.
20. **Content course with a host professor** (Leaver's premier model) — ~300 hours in e.g. a history course with a professor who doesn't know your language; treated like a local student; communication growth central to his role. Lighter versions: **audit university courses** (Thomson audited 2–3 as the only non-Russian student); **take a host-taught course for host people** — "Maybe dancing? Maybe first aid, or hygiene? Maybe something related to your job specialty?" — classroom + coffee shops + recreation + post-course friendships all become host life; homework develops literacy; or enroll full-time.
21. **Lifestyle restructuring portfolio** (the bulk of the guide's page count):
    - **Workplace as host community of practice**: make the host language the office language; if all staff share a native language, "we will hire at least one host person who doesn't know the (former) office language, and at least one additional host person, so that there will be fully normal host-to-host interaction"; thereafter any other language is "clearly rude"; all expat staff expected to be growing participators; conduct office life the host way (Phase 4-style interviews about host office life if not yet done); host ambience — "pictures, decorations, furniture, furniture arrangements, tools, utensils."
    - **Residence**: children **as young as 7** can do Phase 1 with the family ("in the end do better than the parents"; engaging-for-a-seven-year-old is "a good quality control" for adults' activities too; children "probably not need to go beyond Phase 2 and their life with neighbouring children will be like Phase 6 for them"; adolescents benefit from a lot of Phase 3; "An hour or two a week won't accomplish much. **Five hours a week is more viable**"). Host people living with you, or you with them; the politeness rule: "whenever there is a mix of our family members and one or more host people in the room, our family members speak to one another using the host language." Befriend domestic employees (gatekeepers, cooks, cleaners, drivers) — Thomson's counter-story to the "don't mix business and friendship" advice.
    - **Get a television**: "It can be a refreshing spray hose, letting host life into our private home-life dominated place of residence" (vs. cable/satellite + home-world DVD shelves reinforcing isolation).
    - **Social visiting**: host guests to you, you to them; "If our 'work' or other engagements are more important than people who come to visit, they may soon get the message."
    - **Refuse the expat magnet**: only the host language with expats met inside host settings.
    - **Team life**: multinational teams should adopt the host language as team language ("even without a single host person in the room, the team would be a host community of practice"); same-background teams should add host members for some activities; the whispered-sequential-interpretation vignette shows newcomers that "Growing into the host life is not an option, but a necessity."
    - **International schools**: could make growing participation an educational value (staff as GPs, activities mixing expats with non-English-knowing host people) instead of maximizing home life.
    - **Displaced / limited-access host groups**: spend intensive hours with whichever host people appear (hospital patients + caregiving relatives in the city), "made a lot of audio recordings, which they listened to with much devotion when there were no host people around"; or grow via a **diaspora community** (Leaver's Level-5 evidence) then make brief homeland visits (post-Phase-3, with personal referrals).
    - **National-language / lingua-franca strategy**: "You don't choose a language; you choose a people group." Either fold the national language into being nurtured by your minority host people (inevitable on a 17-year road), or run a deliberate separate "**season** for specially loving" the national group first (e.g., French, then Wolof, then Soninke in Senegal). Meanwhile prefer minority-run shops and cafes.
    - **Prerequisites for lifestyle moves to pay off**: "if you at least finished **Phase 3**, you should be able to make these adjustments to your workplace and home… If you've completed **Phase 4**, you should be in great shape… If you only finished **Phase 2**, it might be worth your time to do Phase 3 before attempting such changes."

## 7. Stages/sub-phases & the grower's state

Phase 6 has no dated sub-phases (no "6A/6B" in the curriculum sense). Its internal structure is:

### (a) The governing dichotomy: Phase 6 vs. Phase un-6
- **Phase 6 grower's state**: understands *most* of everything heard around them; "simply hearing speech advances your ability to understand speech, since you successfully carry out the whole range of comprehension processes"; new words "blaze through your understanding machine" because context makes them clear; the mental **parser** keeps accelerating with use; the grower can follow host-to-host conversation and cope with "the most challenging multi-person conversational contexts." Speaking still needs deliberate interactive practice.
- **Phase un-6 grower's state**: much host speech is still "noise"; may sound fluent (small-resource fluency illusion) but is not changing in host people's experience; the parser "may not improve much unless you do special-growth activities to make it improve." Litmus test: "if they become quickly lost when trying to follow a conversation between two host people (who aren't making a point of including them in the conversation), then they are not yet on the Phase-6 road."
- One can be in Phase 6 **without** ever doing the Six-Phase Programme (immersion cases: the Ed story — 15 years in Japan, Japanese wife and children, Japanese-only family life, 4-year Japanese Literature degree, all-day face-to-face sales job; also Ioup et al. 1994's adult who came to pass as Egyptian purely by immersion). And one can finish Phases 1–5 and *not* be truly in Phase 6.

### (b) Quit-point trajectories (state by where you stopped)
- **Quit after Phase 1 or 2**, relying on everyday interaction: talking gets easier for a while (illusory growth); over several years you don't change in host people's experience; likely **Terminal 1+**. ("Likely to be Janes" in the Bill/Harry/Jane/Ernest scenarios.)
- **Quit after Phase 3 or 4**: same illusory-fluency dynamic; with extensive host-life participation, likely **Terminal 2+** ("likely to be Harrys"; "Bill is, of course, in Phase 6"). Thomson: "I can distinguish Harrys from long-standing Bills by the time I'm in Phase 2 or 3 myself—I can understand all that the Harrys say to host people, but until I'm in Phase 6, I'll miss much of what a long-standing Bill says!"
- Thomson's own dual state: **Phase 6 in Russian** (~500 pre-departure hours + 2 saturated years in Russia + audited university courses + months of 2 h/day video-massaging), **Phase un-6 / Terminal 2+ in Kazakh** (150 h in 2001, 250 h in 2002, 250 h in 2003, ~150 h in 2004, then stopped; "For a time I seemed to grow simply by being in Kazakh environments… I was really just holding my own").

### (c) Road dynamics: on, off, back on
- You leave the Phase 6 road "whenever you quit participating in the host world for a period of time, either because your home life or competing host lives strangle it, or because you interrupt or terminate your sojourn."
- "**Getting back into Phase 6 after leaving it is a lot easier than getting into Phase 6 from Phase un-6.** Getting back… is a matter of restoring a healthy lifestyle. Getting from Phase un-6 to Phase 6 may require special-growth activities plus a healthy lifestyle."
- Revival practice: Thomson's 2 special-growth hours/day during a return visit to Kazakhstan; "whenever I'm among one of my former host people groups, unless I'm clearly in Phase 6, in which case just being with people will be enough to revive me."

### (d) A/B stages inside every discourse activity
Within Phase 6's activities the grower cycles through the **A-stage (GP in the lead)** → after 10–20 hours → **B-stage (nurturer in the lead)** pattern, explicitly "parallel to Phases 2A and B." So the phase re-uses the programme's oldest sub-phase logic at micro scale, per discourse.

### (e) Comprehension-only vs. speaking?
No comprehension-only stage remains: the Phase 6 grower both understands nearly everything and produces extended discourse. The persisting asymmetry is that **listening keeps improving from mere exposure while speaking improves only through interaction** — "You can make some progress in speaking ability through a large amount of listening, but nothing comparable to the progress you will make if you also do a lot of speaking and interacting."

## 8. What they need to be LOOKING AT (visual/physical materials)

- **Real-world objects and venues**: your actual car (or a **labelled diagram of all internal and external car parts**); the beauty parlor itself; shops, cafes (preferably minority-run in multilingual settings); the workplace and its host-style ambience ("pictures, decorations, furniture, furniture arrangements, tools, utensils").
- **Magazines with varied photos** — e.g., hairstyle magazines for the beauty-parlor sequence ("Get some magazines in which people in the photos have a wide variety of hairstyles").
- **Wordless/silent films** for hole finding: Charlie Chaplin, Mr. Bean, and *Kelin* (Kazakh Film, ~80-minute dialogue-free drama, available from amazon.com in 2012).
- **Host television/video**: sitcoms (watchable with benefit), talk shows (colloquial multi-person argument — massage-worthy), news broadcasts, documentaries, weather forecasts (Thomson's "overcast" parsing anecdote); a browser window with a host TV programme beside an **online dictionary** window.
- **Written text — now fully in scope** (where literacy is a host practice): fiction, children's school textbooks, university textbooks, magazines, newspapers, product instructions; a **monolingual host-language dictionary** (explicitly preferred over bilingual). In diglossic languacultures (Arabic), written text is studied *through spoken discussion*.
- **Earlier-phase visual kits for remedial work**: Phase 2 picture stories and "busy pictures," Phase 3 scripts-of-life/world-stories materials.
- **The needs-analysis scoring table** (columns: Communication situation | How often 0–5 | How important 0–5 | How well already 0 to −10 | Total).
- **A television set** as a lifestyle object — the "refreshing spray hose" letting host life into the residence.

## 9. How much RECORDING they need to do

Recording is the single most pervasive tool of Phase 6 — essentially **every activity has a recording step**:

- **In-session recordings (every discourse activity)**:
  - A-stages: the grower records **every attempt live** (each toast, each car-fault monologue, each beauty-parlor guess).
  - After feedback: the **nurturer's revised version of the grower's own text** is recorded — one per attempt cycle ("Repeat Steps 1 to 3 many times").
  - B-stages: **every nurturer model** is recorded, then massaged.
  - Preludes: listen-and-point activities are recorded too ("You could also do a variety of listen and point activities (and record them)").
  - Scripts-of-life and expanded versions: recorded and massaged "if need be."
- **The Listening Library**: all of the above accumulate into a personal library. Re-listening cadence: **before every next meeting** (a fixed step in both A and B sequences), and "many times after, especially when you are expecting to make a toast" (event-triggered relistening before real-life performances).
- **Word-discussion recordings**: reading discussions with the nurturer are recorded "in order to further refresh and strengthen those words in your mental listening vocabulary store."
- **Broadcast capture**: "jump at anything you hear on T.V. or elsewhere that is challenging for you and try to record such material." Thomson made "many recordings" of both his sitcom and his talk show.
- **Massaging volume (Thomson's own numbers)**: "at least two hours per day" for **several months** massaging Russian videos — the practice he credits with completing his climb onto the Phase 6 road.
- **Portable listening**: "It is possible to carry hundreds of hours of recordings in your mp3 player (2012 technology) and to redeem the time on buses and sleepless nights."
- **Displaced-host-group case**: growers "made a lot of audio recordings, which they listened to with much devotion when there were no host people around. Audio recordings, in the GPA, allow you to relive those experiences you had with host people—over and over—at times when new experiences are not possible."

The guide gives no fixed minutes-per-session quota for Phase 6 (unlike earlier phases); the operative rules are: **record every attempt and every model, re-listen before the next session, re-listen before real events, and accumulate hundreds of hours of replayable material.**

## 10. Milestones & phase-specific terminology

### Milestones / progress markers
1. Meets the two-condition Phase 6 definition (understands most of what's said/done around them AND keeps hearing a lot).
2. **The litmus test**: can follow a conversation *between two host people* who are not deliberately including the grower.
3. "Mental language processor racing right along" in the most challenging multi-person colloquial contexts.
4. **The video test**: a host person comparing videos five years apart sees striking change (anti-"Terminal" marker).
5. **The "new you"**: after ≥100/300 qualifying special-growth hours, host people (or a certified Oral Proficiency Interviewer) detect a changed host identity.
6. Host-like performance in targeted discourses (elaborate non-cliché toast before an audience; fault-description to a mechanic; beautician small talk).
7. Vocabulary velocity: 5–10 new listening words/hour from ordinary participation; 10/hour in remedial pushes; ~3,000 per 300 hours; total store >10,000 and climbing; words "jump out" of previously-heard streams as the parser improves (the "overcast" moment).
8. Private media and reading are now genuinely growth-producing (can massage challenging broadcasts, read extensively with a monolingual dictionary).
9. Membership in host **communities of practice** / small host social networks where host people relate to one another, largely ignoring the grower's foreignness.
10. Lifestyle sustained at ≥10–20% of ~100 waking hours/week in host life; resists the expat magnet, they-stories, Little-America patterns.
11. Long-horizon: on the road toward ILR Level 4 (avg 17 years; ~6 with intervention) — knowing "Near Native is still far from native — the Phase 6 road is long indeed," and that Level 2/3 people typically over-rate themselves while true Level 4s are "painfully aware of how non-native they still were" (Leaver 2003b).

### Glossary of terms this guide uses
- **Phase 6 / "Self-Sustaining Growth"** — the road where both definition conditions hold and growth is automatic.
- **Phase un-6** — not in Phase 6 AND doing little/no special-growth activity; the plateau condition.
- **Growing Participator (GP) / grower** — the person being nurtured into the host world.
- **Nurturer** — the (usually paid) host person creating optimized participation contexts.
- **Home world / host world** — technical, geography-independent: home = relating on my terms in my language (even abroad); host = relating on their terms, in their language, following their practices.
- **Home identity / host identity** — who I am in the experience of home-world members vs. host people; the "new you" is a change in host identity.
- **Home life / host life** — plus home/host friends, music, discourses, clothing, etc.
- **Languaculture / languacultural world** — language+culture as one inseparable stream of practices (the "dog" example: naming the animal and hunting raccoons with it are one learning process).
- **Principle of Competition Between Lives** — you live only one life at a time; the ~100 waking hours/week are zero-sum across your two, three, or four lives.
- **Multiple lives** — e.g., a Korean in Japan lives three: home (Korean), host-1 (Japanese), host-2 (Anglophone).
- **Home-life "accent"** — the home world colouring not just pronunciation but hearing, understanding, and the whole conceptual system; "it will never entirely go away. It is part of what makes you special!"
- **"They stories"** — outsider narratives about host people, "radically disconnected from the story they themselves are experiencing"; the GPA is "living their story with them."
- **Special-growth participation (sessions/activities)** — concentrated participation meeting the 5 characteristics; they "distill life participation into a 'concentrate'"; "They are the community that we are 'out in'."
- **Seed community** — "You-plus-your-nurturer in your little special-growth community… It will grow into a sapling, then a tree."
- **Wall of noise** — the initial impenetrability of a new languacultural world.
- **Terminal 1+ / Terminal 2+** — ILR sub-levels at which un-6 people permanently plateau.
- **Bill, Harry, Jane, Ernest scenarios** — GPA archetypes; Bill is in Phase 6; quit-after-1/2 people become Janes, quit-after-3/4 people Harrys.
- **The 100/300-hour rule & the "new you"** — Leaver's thresholds for detectable change via teacher-mediated hours.
- **Host discourse** — an ongoing area of host communication plus its objects, actions, statuses, roles.
- **Needs analysis** — the 0–5 / 0–5 / minus 0–10 scoring method for choosing discourse targets.
- **A/B pattern (GP-in-the-lead / nurturer-in-the-lead)** — growth-zone-preserving alternation, parallel to Phases 2A/2B.
- **Growth zone** — where input/feedback anchors to what the grower can almost do; the nurturer's improvement of *your* version stays in it.
- **Record Yourself for Feedback** — record attempt → review with nurturer → track improvements.
- **Negotiating meanings** — interactively getting the nurturer to understand what you were trying to say.
- **Retelling tasks** — always "in your own words, reflecting what you are personally able to do at the time," never verbatim.
- **Massaging (a recording/video)** — intensive solo work-through: pausing, rewinding, looking up unfamiliar words, going on.
- **Listening Library** — the accumulated, much-replayed collection of recordings.
- **Hole finding** — deliberately finding things you can't say (silent-movie live narration).
- **Parsing / the parser** — turning the sound stream into words, phrases, sentences, discourse; improves only by being used.
- **Community of practice** — a group together periodically for a purpose; "They know who belongs and who does not. Belonging means conforming to the practices."
- **Home-away-from-home life** — the massive expat home-life subculture abroad.
- **Expat archipelago** — expat households as islands "in the sea of nondescript locals."
- **The expat magnet** — the pull that sucks expats together in host settings ("as if by a giant magnet").
- **Little America (Little Korea, Little China)** — a workplace turned into a home-world outpost where locals become the growing participators in *your* world.
- **Survival expert / seasoned veteran expat** — home-life coping mastery (vanilla extract, napa cabbage, taxi tricks) mistaken for cross-cultural competence.
- **"Story making piece"** — a home-languaculture concept (e.g., "friendship") that pre-shapes how you story other people's practices.
- **"You don't choose a language; you choose a people group"** — the national-language "season" doctrine.
- **Season** — a deliberate period of growing participation focused on one people group (e.g., "specially loving Nepalis before focusing on specially loving Ghosa people").
- **Growing-participating couples/families/teams/businesses/NGOs/schools** — the anti-individualist extension of GPA.
- **Content instruction** — Leaver-style subject-matter courses as premier higher-level special-growth.
- **Diglossia strategy** — spoken-language discussion of written text (Arabic/Latin analogy).
- **ILR/FSI levels** — 0–5 scale; Level 1 Elementary/Survival, 2 Limited Working, 3 General Professional, 4 Advanced Professional/Near-Native ("tent pegs"), 5 functionally native-equivalent.

## App vs. source-guide deltas

The app entry (`phases.ts` id 6: name "Ever Participating, Ever Growing", tagline "Self-sustaining growth — a life to be lived", `hours: 0`, `ongoing: true`, `startHour: 1500`, 8 activities, 3 parts, 5 milestones) is a faithful *compression*, but the following prescriptions are omitted, simplified, or altered:

### Omitted entirely
1. **The two-condition Phase 6 definition and the un-6 definition** — the app never states the diagnostic ("understand most of what you hear" AND "keep hearing a lot"), nor that condition-2 failure is fixable-by-lifestyle while condition-1 failure is the bigger problem.
2. **The litmus test** — "can you follow a conversation between two host people not deliberately including you?" — absent from milestones and activities. (The guide's clearest self-assessment; a natural app diagnostic.)
3. **The 100-hour variant of the rule** — the app mentions "~300 hours" only ("6-target" part and principle 3). The guide says **at least 100 hours at low proficiency, 300 at higher**, and that the hours must be *teacher-mediated* and must meet the **5 special-growth characteristics** (which the app never lists).
4. **Hole Finding** — the silent-movie live-narration activity (Chaplin, Mr. Bean, *Kelin*) is completely missing.
5. **The Listening Library, massaging, and the entire recording regimen** — the app's only reference is one passing phrase in `p6-rare` ("for your listening library"). Missing: record every attempt, record the nurturer's revised version, massage nurturer/broadcast recordings, re-listen before every next meeting, re-listen before real events, mp3-player hours, recording word-discussions from reading, encounter recordings for displaced groups.
6. **Mass-media special-growth** — TV/radio/video/documentary/news listening at optimal difficulty, recording challenging broadcasts, the sitcom-vs-talk-show difficulty ladder, and the "media is supplement, not substitute" caveat: none present.
7. **Most of the lifestyle portfolio's concrete moves**: hire host staff who don't know the office language; the family-language politeness rule (host language whenever host people are in the room); children's family Phase 1 (age 7+, 5 h/week viable, 1–2 h/week won't accomplish much, kids stop after Phase 2, adolescents do Phase 3); befriending domestic employees; "get a television"; social visiting; refusing the expat magnet; host-language team meetings; international-school redemption; the displaced-group/diaspora strategies; the national-language "season" doctrine ("You don't choose a language; you choose a people group").
8. **Readiness gating for lifestyle changes** — guide: finish Phase 3 before workplace/home restructuring pays off; Phase 4 done = great shape; only Phase 2 = do Phase 3 first.
9. **Terminology & research anchors** — Terminal 1+/2+, ILR levels, the 17-years/6-years figures, Leaver's statistics (40% host-spouse, 76% host degree, 86% extensive readers, 100% multilingual childhood), the tent-pegs criterion, the fluency-illusion arithmetic, the Bill/Harry/Jane archetypes, the video test.
10. **The un-6 re-entry rule** — "go back and pick up where you quit"; Phase 5 activities may be over a Terminal 2+'s head; Phase 1 as "your first 100 hours to a new you" at low levels; and the warning that paid unstructured chat is not a growth strategy.
11. **The monolingual-dictionary prescription and the diglossia (Arabic) reading strategy** — `p6-read` covers shared reading + recorded word discussion but omits both, and omits "reading aloud to a paid nurturer is a waste of money."
12. **On-road/off-road dynamics** — getting back into Phase 6 after sliding off is much easier than getting there from un-6; the revival practice (2 special-growth h/day when re-visiting a former host group).

### Simplified
13. **The discourse-mastery pipeline** — `p6-rare` compresses the guide's 6-stage sequence (Prelude → A ×10–20 h → B → Scripts-of-Life → Role-play → real venue visit + Reminiscing) into a single 40-minute activity, dropping the **10–20-hour A-before-B gate**, the **role-play** stage, and the **real-world capstone with Reminiscing** entirely.
14. **Needs analysis** (`p6-needs`) — the scoring formula is correct (0–5 + 0–5 − 0–10), but the guide's worked examples/thresholds and the rationale triple (can't do it / infrequent / high-stakes-before-an-audience) are absent.
15. **Vocab numbers** — `p6-remedial` keeps "ten new words an hour" and the Cinderella anecdote, but the 7–8/hour normal target, the ~3,000-in-300-hours figure, the 5–10 words/hour ambient rate, and the >10,000-word baseline are all missing. App `vocabTarget` is the vague "Ever-growing — like an insider's."
16. **`p6-expert`** merges two distinct guide prescriptions — Phase 4 expert interviewing (the low-creativity fallback) and Leaver-style 300-hour content courses / auditing / host-taught hobby courses (dancing, first aid) — losing the distinction and the audit/enroll options.
17. **The 10%/20% benchmark** — the app description says "Most newcomers give under 10%" and principle 2 says "claim more back," but the explicit *target* (≥10%, better ≥20%, and un-6's "at least five of those hours special-growth") never appears, and the milestone "Most of your week's host hours are genuinely lived in the host world" is circular where the guide is numeric.

### Contradictions / app inventions
18. **Misuse of "massage"** — `p6-rare` says "your nurturer massages it for feedback." In the guide, *massaging* is what the **grower** does to a recording (pause/rewind/look-up), and the A-stage review is **Record Yourself for Feedback** performed *with* the nurturer. Terminology should be corrected before building features around it.
19. **"Become a Nurturer" (`p6-nurture` / part "6-give")** — nowhere in the Phase 6 guide. The guide's closing moves are lifestyle and community (growing-participating teams/schools), not nurturing the next grower. It's a defensible product extension (and consistent with GPA's anti-individualism) but must be understood as an **app invention**, not Thomson.
20. **The three "parts" (Live It / Target / Give Back)** are an app-invented structure; the guide has no sub-parts. "Live It" and "Target" do map well onto the guide's two halves (lifestyle vs. special-growth).
21. **Per-activity `minutes` values (20–60)** are app inventions; the guide works in hours (10–20 h per A-stage, 20–30 h per discourse, 100/300-hour blocks, 5 h/week, 2 h/day massaging).
22. **`p6-hours` framing** is faithful, including the guide's crucial motive clause ("not 'to learn the language', but because you belong there") — one place the app *keeps* the guide's spirit precisely.

### Highest-value additions for the app (from the guide, echoed by the research JSON's `appIdeas`)
Needs-analysis wizard with auto-ranked discourse tracks; the gated 6-stage discourse pipeline; a record-for-feedback workflow that enforces "improve their version, don't replace it"; a Listening Library with pre-session and pre-event replay prompts; a massage player (pause/rewind loops + dictionary + new-word capture); a hole-finding studio (silent-film narration with stall-marking); a 100-waking-hours dashboard against the 10/20% benchmarks; a 100/300-hours-to-a-new-you meter that only counts sessions passing a 5-characteristics checklist; a per-hour vocabulary counter with a "splice in a busy picture" nudge; an un-6 re-entry diagnostic routing plateaued users to the correct earlier phase; family/team multi-user mode.
