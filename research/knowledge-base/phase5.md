## 1. Phase identity & goals

- **Name:** Phase 5: Widening Understanding — with the parenthetical purpose baked into the title: "(such that we can fit in naturally in groups of host people)". Subtitle of the guide: "Another 500 Hours: Getting Underway with Conversations about Native-to-Native Discourses."
- **Author/version:** Greg Thomson, Version 2016. Motto on the title page: "It's not a language to be lived but a life to be lived" (Thomson's recurring GPA slogan, quoted verbatim in the guide as "It's not a language to be learned but a life to be lived").
- **Hour range:** hours ~1,000–1,500 of the recommended 1,500-hour six-phase programme. Phase 5 is **500 hours** of "special supercharged participation meetings with special mentors" — exactly one-third of the whole programme, the same size as Phase 4. Phase 6 then "goes on for as long as we are involved in host life."
- **The problem it attacks:** through Phases 1–4 the growing participator (GP) understood host speech largely because hosts were *simplifying and adapting* it for a foreigner. Thomson quotes the classic expat complaint: "I can do pretty well communicating with one host person who knows me, but when host people are talking to one another and ignoring me, I understand very little." Foreigner-directed speech "puts a cap on the level to which you can grow" — it is an unnatural model, like learning only from people shouting in a noisy room or talking to the hard-of-hearing.
- **Core purpose:** work "very hard to understand speech that is not addressed to you" — speech by host people to host people in normal host life, recorded and clarified: **native-to-native discourses**. The main special-growth activity of the phase is *conversations with a mentor about recorded native-to-native discourses*. Thomson's image: "you're going to shed your training wheels and ride with the gang."
- **Destination:** Phase 6, Ever Participating/Growing — **self-sustaining growth**, where you keep growing through ordinary life alone because you understand most of what you hear. That requires (Thomson is explicit): a comprehension vocabulary of many thousands of words, "probably over ten thousand," recognized and understood "instantly"; sentence processing fast and full enough that you can "confidently let each sentence go, and tackle the next incoming sentence." All of it comes from "experience, experience, experience."
- **What "done" looks like:**
  - 100–125 hours of native-to-native recordings collected and clarified, now living in the personal listening library.
  - Communities of practice have become "supercharged": "you'll understand all that is going on, even when people are talking to one another and not you," and you have moved from outside guest to a "clear belonger whose role in the group is valued."
  - Words/constructions met massively in listening are emerging in your own speech.
  - Grammar is still visibly non-native — that is *expected and normal* (see §5) — and sociolinguistic/pragmatic/kinesic polish is explicitly deferred to Phase 6.

## 2. The instructions (session plan)

Thomson notes that Phase 1 got a hundred hours of detailed instructions, while Phase 5's detailed instructions run "about two and a half pages… From there you are on your own!" — because the phase simply re-applies familiar techniques to a new resource class.

### 2.1 Before the meeting
Needed: (1) a host person who can record native-to-native discourses; (2) recording equipment for them. Two acquisition options for self-collected recordings:

- **Option A — hired life-story interview:** hire someone you did Phase 4 life-history interviews with (they know the format) to record *a person you don't know well* telling their life story. Best if interviewer and storyteller also don't know each other — strangers state details explicitly instead of leaving them implicit. Target ~1 hour of recording, **20 minutes minimum**. The GP must NOT be present, "so that it will be told by a host person to a host person with no thought of accommodating a newcomer."
- **Option B — hot-topic discussion:** ask friends what a lot of people are currently talking about; arrange two or three talkative acquaintances to discuss one or more hot topics (or have a friend arrange it with people you don't know). GP absent, ~1 hour hoped for, 20 minutes minimum. "It might help if refreshments are provided."

**Solo pre-clarifying:** since you have the recording in advance, try clarifying it alone — listen "in units of a phrase or two at a time, pausing to give yourself the chance to process what you heard before going on." If it's within reach, repeated listening alone can achieve high comprehension; everything understood without help "will build your confidence and… improve your understanding machine."

### 2.2 The 10-step master template
Quoted from the guide's summary box, "Steps in a Conversation with a Mentor About a Native-to-Native Discourse":

1. Acquire a recording of a native-to-native discourse.
2. Listen once through and see how much you (think you) understand.
3. Summarize to your mentor what you (thought you) understood.
4. Record your mentor's response to your summary.
5. Clarification step: go through the original with the mentor.
6. Be making the new-word recording as you go along.
7. Watch for and note details you don't understand because you don't know something about life in the host world.
8. Expand on the points noted in Step 7.
9. (Optional) Ask Grand/Mini-Tour questions, List-of-Life questions, experience questions, etc. from Phase 4.
10. GP retells the discourse (at his/her current level of ability, with the mentor's help).

### 2.3 The two-hour meeting breakdown ("supercharged participation meeting")
The guide's suggested split for a 2-hour meeting:

| Segment | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Small talk | 5–10 min | Genuine relationship time. "Find out what is important to your mentor today. Learn some things about her life that are new to you. Share something of interest about yourself." Example opener: ask "What are you thinking about?" and wait five seconds. Rationale: "You grow into the life of an ethnolinguistic group by growing into the lives of individuals in it." |
| GP retell of yesterday | (a few min, listed inside the clarifying block) | "In your own words, summarise the portion of the recording that you clarified yesterday" — the retelling-by-GP technique used since Phase 2. |
| Clarifying the recording | **80–90 min** | Listen to a new **5–10 minute segment** of recorded speech together; then go back to the beginning and work through it bit by bit (mechanics in §6.1). Repeat with additional segments. Word recording runs in parallel throughout. |
| Supercharged talking activities | **20–30 min** | E.g., discuss illustrated-magazine pictures for 15–20 min hunting for holes in speaking ability; explain a topic from your professional training the mentor doesn't know; defend a firm opinion against opposing views. More in the Appendix (§6.8). |

### 2.4 After the meeting
"Listen to the whole portion of the recording that you clarified in the meeting and to the word recordings. Do this soon afterward, such as the same evening. Also, listen to some recordings that were clarified previously, and previous word recordings. As you accumulate more and more clarified recordings… in your listening library, listen to all of them periodically."

### 2.5 Variant: meeting with ready-made resources (movie/TV drama)
- Before: record a TV broadcast, e.g., a drama.
- Once you have it: try clarifying on your own — pausing a lot, rewinding, using a dictionary for unknown words.
- During (same 2-hour skeleton: small talk 5–10 min, GP retell of yesterday, clarify 80–90 min, talking activities 20–30 min): watch/listen to a segment together for the overall idea, then go back through bit by bit with all standard clarifying moves; make the word recording; possibly have the mentor give a simpler summary paraphrase. Built-in bonus talking activity: much of a movie is non-verbal, so the GP gives a highly detailed description of what they see "simply, interpreting it as little as possible," and the mentor supplies the insider's interpretation — like a Phase 4 interviewee.
- Cautions: "what you hear is a wall of noise, and what you see is an illusion — you know you don't understand what you hear, and mistakenly think you understand what you see." Dubbed foreign films are "not our first choice if there are plenty of host films." Even then, "often the mentor is not seeing what you are seeing." In a movie less is expressed verbally than in a radio play — visuals carry meaning.
- After: same-evening re-listening as above.

### 2.6 Time-budget rules for the whole phase
- Roughly **two-thirds of supercharged time** goes to clarifying recordings (stated in the Li Fang worked example: 16 of 25 weekly hours).
- Talking activities may occupy "up to one quarter to one half of your supercharged participation time" (Appendix header) — the in-meeting default is the 20–30 minute block.
- Rule of thumb: **~3 hours of work to clarify 1 hour of recording**; equivalently **1 meeting hour consumes ~20 minutes of recording**.
- Total target: **clarify 100–125 hours** of native-to-native speech. "If that takes 330 hours, it leaves 170 hours for other Phase 5 activities."
- Ordinary social visiting may be counted toward the 500 hours only up to **100–200 hours** (see §7.3).

## 3. What the NURTURER does (in Phase 5: the MENTOR)

**Terminology shift:** the Phases 1–3 helper was a *nurturer* — few in number, knew the GP well, needed finding and training (possibly via a "Growing Participation Support Centre" or language school). From Phase 4 onward the helper is a **mentor**: "the mentors do not need training, as the communication is natural, but they [GPs] still need people who will interact with them at a level they can understand and participate in." In Phase 5, "you typically meet with a *paid* mentor for the sake of clarifying such recordings." (The guide's paragraph on when the renaming happens is garbled — it says "In Phase 3 the GPs need to branch out… so the special host people… are called mentors" — but the operative fact is that Phases 4–5 helpers are untrained, natural-communication mentors.)

**What the mentor does, concretely (from the instructions and the worked Huckabee–Obama transcript):**
- Engages in real small talk and shares her own life and opinions (in the worked example she volunteers that she's worried about her country's future; later "N goes into a long digression about families and how family life has changed" when the GP asks her own view).
- Listens to each segment together with the GP.
- **Repeats phrases "slowly and clearly" when they were "too rapid and 'sloppy' or 'garbled' for a non-native listener at your level"** — including reconstructing a long sentence in pieces over several replays ("I want to ask you… Can I hear it again?… is your passion for Let's Move primarily a policy passion… Again?…").
- **Elaborates each new word conversationally** rather than just defining it: gives contexts, examples, contrasts, register judgments. E.g., for "join": "She just joined Mike Huckabee. It means she came to him and did something with him. For example, I joined you here in your home…" — and when the GP tries "Thank you very much for join me in my home," she corrects the *register*: "It sounds really fancy. No I wouldn't say that here. They are on national television." For "focused" she runs through cameras, binoculars, glasses, blurry vs. clear, then "focus on children = especially look at/think about/help children." She distinguishes a program *name* ("Let's Move") from the phrase's everyday meaning ("let's go from the table and sit on a chesterfield").
- Corrects the GP's trial sentences gently ("Parental. I am not parent. I am not parental?" → "No. We don't talk like that. It means 'having to do with parents'… parental responsibility, parental rights, parental love").
- Knows when to punt: for "sexist" — "Never mind. Sexism is another issue. It's too complicated." (Thomson flags this as the one word in the transcript that did NOT get a strong encounter.)
- Explains **whole areas of host life** when incomprehension is cultural, not lexical (First Lady / second lady / mayor's wife; drive-through food; how family life has changed).
- Answers expanding questions, descriptive (grand-tour/mini-tour) questions, domain-of-life questions ("What else do people share?" → a joke, dreams, secrets, problems).
- Gives **summary paraphrases in simpler, clearer "native-to-non-native" language** (retelling by the mentor) to give the GP an overall grasp.
- Retells the *GP's own* stories "in a more host-like way" when asked (the second use of mentor retelling).
- **Writes things down** on request if literate ("Please write these down" → she writes the three examples), and can keep the written new-word list.
- Reads printed texts aloud "as naturally as possible" so the recording can be clarified (§8/§11 of the guide).
- Gives feedback on the GP's recorded speech ("ways your speech was not like that of host people" — system from the Phase 4 guide).
- Supplies the "insider's view" on movie scenes after the GP describes them.

**Mentor quality & coaxing:** in the worked example, "these encounters with new words are mainly strong because this is a very gifted mentor. **Often you will find that you have to coax the elaborations of new words out of the mentor.**" The mentor also "is probably often talking just a bit over the head of the GP. The GP will generally be getting the point, but missing a lot of the details" — this is acceptable and normal.

**Mentor/setup don'ts:**
- Neither mentor nor GP may be present/participating as audience when the source recordings are made (would de-nativize them).
- Don't reduce word help to bare definitions — elaboration is the point.
- Don't let clarifying become "an impersonal and mechanical process" — never just "What does that word mean? OK, I get it. Let's hear the next bit."
- Don't have the GP read aloud to the mentor — "It doesn't make any sense for me to read to her, as I can practice that at home without paying anyone to listen." The mentor reads aloud instead.
- Don't demand host-like grammar at this stage (see §5).

**How mentor behavior changes across the phase:** the scaffolds thin as the material hardens. Early: easier discourses (strangers talking, explicit detail), more mentor slow-repetition and simplified retelling. Later: "eventually we will value those conversations between people who know each other well, and are therefore extra hard to understand!" There is no separate "training" arc for the mentor — the change is in the difficulty of the discourse and the shrinking need for accommodation.

## 4. What the PARTICIPANT (GP) does

**Around the sessions:**
- Organizes the recording pipeline: hires/arranges hosts to record discourses (Options A/B), stays away during recording, keeps ~20 minutes of fresh recording flowing per planned meeting hour (at Li Fang's 25 hrs/wk that's ~5 hours of new recording weekly — "just organising and managing that amount of recording every week will be a challenge"). Thomson suggests starting the collection back in Phase 4 or even Phase 3, and hopes GPs inherit/bequeath shared collections ("those who come after her will indeed be this fortunate, having Li Fang's recordings to make use of").
- Pre-clarifies solo: phrase-by-phrase listening with pauses; with media, pause/rewind/dictionary.

**In the session (response modes are now fully conversational — no pointing/acting repertoire remains from early phases):**
- Makes genuine small talk; asks the mentor about her life.
- Retells yesterday's clarified portion in his/her own words (stretching activity).
- Drives the machine: pauses the recording every phrase or two, rewinds, replays ("[GP rewinds and plays it again]" recurs throughout the transcript), repeats sentences aloud to check hearing ("Repeats the sentence to make sure he heard it right").
- Confesses non-understanding precisely: "I don't understand it," "Please repeat slowly," "Can't get it," "Something I miss," and offers mishearings for repair ("hocus?" for *focused*, "Payental?" for *parental*, "An eye shed?" for *I shared*, "Policy? Police?").
- Tries new words in his own sentences and accepts correction ("Focus children mean emphasis children," "My children are important. I will focus on my children").
- Records the word recording in the moment, one mini-conversation per new word.
- Notes details not understood because of missing *life* knowledge (Step 7) and asks for expansions (Step 8).
- Asks Phase 4 questions: domain-of-life ("What else people share?"), descriptive/grand-tour/mini-tour ("Tell me about drive-through food"), list questions ("What are all the problems this man faces?"), experience questions/first-person narratives ("Can you tell me a story about when something similar happened to you?"), and the mentor's own opinions ("What you think? How have things changed for families?").
- Asks the mentor to write lists down.
- Retells the whole discourse at the end (Step 10), and does detailed observation-and-description of movie scenes.
- Does the 20–30 min talking activities: picture description hunting for holes, teaching his specialty, defending opinions, lectures, epic storytelling.

**Homework / self-directed work:**
- Same-evening re-listen to the day's clarified portion + word recordings; periodic rotation through the whole listening library.
- Maintains the word recording and its "shrinking copy" review file (§9.3).
- Records himself speaking (retellings, lectures) for later mentor feedback.
- **Solitary reading, "a lot"** (in literate languacultures): at home, with a bilingual or monolingual dictionary; note that in some languages using a dictionary "may require a certain level of understanding of the grammar" (recovering citation forms).
- Participates in **communities of practice** (work group, club, class, church/mosque, teashop regulars — or a self-organized group "dominated by host people doing things their way"), progressing from outside guest to valued belonger.
- Keeps a healthy volume of ordinary social visiting (hundreds of hours ideally) while counting at most 100–200 of them toward the 500.
- Plans his own pacing: "Analyse your own plans in such a table as the above" (the Li Fang/Amelia/Sam table, §7.2).

## 5. WHAT they are learning

**Vocabulary (the headline numbers):**
- Target state for Phase 6: comprehension vocabulary of many thousands — "probably over ten thousand" — words recognized "instantly."
- Yield mechanics: "Even if only one-percent of the words encountered while clarifying a recording are new, that could amount to **ten or fifteen new words in thirty minutes** of a supercharged participation meeting during which ten minutes of recorded speech are clarified. That comes out to perhaps **1,500 new words per twenty hours of recording**. It is not unreasonable to expect to clarify a hundred hours of recordings." (⇒ on the order of 7,500 strong first encounters over the phase.)
- Phase 5 increasingly surfaces **lower-frequency words** ("initiative," "parental" — vs. higher-frequency "focus," "policy," "issue" in formal discourse). "The rarer a word is, the less opportunities there will be to re-encounter it naturally in life" — hence deliberate word-recording review matters more than ever.
- Below ~10,000 words, "our vocabulary just doesn't grow much without special effort" — ordinary social interaction alone stalls it.

**Comprehension/processing skills:**
- Understanding rapid, "sloppy," unaccommodated native-to-native speech in real time; instant word recognition; high-speed sentence-to-sentence integration; "ever-increasing familiarity… with how host people talk, the words they choose and the ways they combine them, how they talk in specific situations or… with particular goals."

**Cultural/host-life knowledge:**
- Filling the gaps that block comprehension: institutions and roles (First Lady = president's wife; the near-nonexistence of "second lady"; first lady of a city/state), everyday practices (drive-through fast food), society-wide "discourses" (hot topics like immigration), humor and religious allusion (the "chair verse" joke — a Koranic verse read before sleep to ward off evil beings), values/beliefs/roles of people in stories. "Your knowledge of host experience will steadily become closer to the host people's knowledge."

**Register/pragmatics awareness (light-touch):** noticing what "sounds fancy" vs. everyday, program names vs. literal phrases, that different people would say things differently. But formal mastery of sociolinguistic competence, pragmatic competence, textual competence, conversational turn-taking, stylistic varieties, and kinesics is **explicitly deferred**: "we don't really see them as a major issue in Phase 5, but rather in Phase 6" — research shows learners only get good at these near the near-native level; in Phase 6 they're "some other things to pay attention to."

**Grammar (expectation management, with the guide's data):**
- GPAers "still be making lots of grammar errors still even when you are in Phase 5" — and so does everyone else, by any method. US government (ILR) data: ~480 hours to Level 1 ("Elementary Proficiency"), where "structural accuracy is likely to be random or severely limited… little precision in the information conveyed." After **1,320 hours** (past the halfway point of Phase 5) many reach Level 2 ("Limited Working Proficiency"), whose weaknesses still include "plurals, articles, linking words, negatives… tense/aspect usage, case morphology, passive constructions, word order, and embedding." Betty Lou Leaver's study of 51 people rated Level 4 ("Near-Native"): **average 17 years** for an adult to get there. Moral: don't judge Phase 5 GPs by grammar polish.

**Literacy (where culturally appropriate):**
- Phase 5 "is the point at which you can really get into heavy-duty reading, since your language ability has reached a level where there will be a large amount of materials you can read, with some patience."
- Theory the guide teaches: reading = **decoding** (mapping written words onto the **auditory mental lexicon** — the words you know by ear) + the same comprehension processes as listening. "The better your listening ability, the better your reading ability has the potential to become." Reading itself then improves by "doing it lots and lots."
- Evidence: Leaver found **86%** of her 51 near-native achievers "were voracious readers in the host language."
- Cultural calibration: don't import your home culture's role for reading (everyday spoken Arabic isn't what's written; Chinese devotes ~⅓ of six years of elementary education to characters; some languacultures are pre-literate). Grow into the community's actual literacy practices.

**Speaking:**
- Talking ability keeps developing via the talking-activities block: describing, explaining specialist topics, argumentative discourse ("important to your continued growth"), lecture register, epic narration. Passive-to-active pipeline: "as you are encountering words, word combinations, and patterns of words (constructions) with such increased frequency, these… increasingly emerge in your own speech."

## 6. HOW they are learning it (every named technique, with mechanics)

Thomson's own list of "key practical skills you already have" (nothing techniques-wise is new in Phase 5 — they are re-aimed at native-to-native discourses): clarifying recordings; expanding what is said; detailed observation and description; applying the iceberg principle to vocabulary; using Deep-Life-Sharing interview questions; making and using word recordings; retelling/summarising by the mentor; retelling/summarising by the GP; enlarging and using your listening library; recording yourself for feedback.

### 6.1 Clarifying a recording (the core engine)
"Taking a text (usually an audio recording) that is a bit beyond your comprehension ability, and discussing it with a host person until you understand it." Mechanics inside the 80–90 min block: listen to a 5–10 min segment together → return to the start → bit by bit: (a) mentor repeats too-rapid/garbled phrases slowly and clearly; (b) unknown words are discussed and captured in the word recording; (c) GP asks for explanations of puzzling longer stretches ("often you won't know the reason you don't understand at first"); (d) expanding (below); (e) mentor explains unfamiliar areas of host life; (f) optionally mentor gives a simpler native-to-non-native summary paraphrase. Repeat per segment. Ratio: ~1 hour of clarifying per 20 minutes of recording; ~3 hours total work per recording hour.

### 6.2 Expanding
"Asking the mentor to 'tell me more details.' For example there may be a single sentence referring to an event, and in expanding, the same event is described, but with many more of the details." Trigger phrase: "Tell me more about this!"

### 6.3 Detailed observation and description (a "hole-finding activity")
"Observing a situation and describing it in abnormally great detail." GP describes what s/he sees; mentor helps the GP say it and adds the insider's interpretation. Phase 5 form: watching a host movie together, GP describes scenes/events, hunting for "details s/he is unsure how to describe, thus finding holes in his/her knowledge of the language, and filling them in."

### 6.4 The Iceberg Principle (vocabulary strategy)
"Memorising all vocabulary to the point of permanent, easy, rapid recall is unnatural and inefficient." Instead: (1) make each new word's *first* encounter strong — get it "into the iceberg"; (2) rely on massed re-encounters (hours of comprehensible listening per week) to make words "keep rising" with their natural frequency; many "(though by no means all) will reach the tip of the iceberg — the point of permanent, easy, rapid recall which enables me to use them fluently in my own speech."

### 6.5 Phase 4 interview techniques reused on discourse content
While clarifying you will: spot **domains of life** and ask for more members (e.g., "kinds of things one can share": jokes, dreams, secrets, problems; "levels of government and their leaders" from the First-Lady exchange); ask **descriptive questions** — **grand-tour** and **mini-tour** questions as in walk-of-life interviews ("Tell me about drive-through food"); ask **list questions** ("What are all the problems this man faces?"); ask for **first-person narratives / experience questions** ("Can you tell me a story about when something similar happened to you?").

### 6.6 Word recordings (see §9.3 for volume/review mechanics)
A separate running sound file with a little *conversation* about each new word — "elaboration," "known to benefit vocabulary learning." Canonical model entry (the "whetstone" example): name the word, recall where it occurred ("In the story, Jack sharpened his knife on a whetstone"), then describe shape, material, sizes, and use, conversationally. The Huckabee–Obama chapter shows live entries for "initiative" (program vs. "show initiative" = effort; "a pretty fancy word. We don't use it that much") and "focus" (optics → attention → "The Let's Move initiative focuses on children"), with the GP trying the word and being corrected. If the mentor is literate (or the GP can transcribe), also keep **a written list of the new words in the order they occur in the word recording, if possible giving the location of each in the sound recording.**

### 6.7 Retelling — three distinct uses
- **Retelling by the mentor, use 1 (simplify):** mentor gives a simpler native-to-non-native version of the native-to-native recording, an overall grasp that "can aid the process of understanding."
- **Retelling by the mentor, use 2 (host-like model):** after the GP tells a story/explanation, the GP "wants to hear it told in a more host-like way (a way that host people would tell it)."
- **Retelling by the GP:** since Phase 2(b) — after grasping a story, retell it in your own words; "a stretching activity that helped you grow in your speaking ability." In Phase 5 this happens (a) as next-day warm-up summary of yesterday's clarified portion and (b) as Step 10, retelling the whole discourse. Any high-output retelling can feed §6.9.

### 6.8 Supercharged talking activities (in-meeting 20–30 min; Appendix allows up to ¼–½ of all supercharged time; "every day you should include one or more")
- **Picture discussion:** illustrated magazines (e.g., National Geographic), 15–20 minutes, "trying to find holes in your speaking ability — activities, objects or situations in the picture that you cannot describe or cannot explain."
- **Teach your specialty:** explain aspects of a topic you're trained in and the mentor isn't.
- **Opinion defense:** discuss a topic you hold a firm opinion on, "supporting your point of view against opposing ones" (argumentative discourse matters for growth).
- **Appendix — Hole-finding activities:** contrast an aspect of home-country life with host-world life; describe every detail of complex "busy book" pictures; watch a cartoon/silent movie (or any action-heavy movie) and discuss everything that happens and how you understand it.
- **Appendix — Ongoing topic discussions:** hobbies, your profession, your academic field, or passion areas (environment, poverty, moral issues, spiritual experiences, home-country politics). "Choose two or three such topics and attempt to continue the discussion of each of them for ten or fifteen minutes each day."
- **Appendix — Epic Storytelling:** carried over from Phase 4 — an ongoing story "continued for perhaps hours, adding a bit each day," with each increment improved by retelling it to several separate people who interact and help.
- **Appendix — Talks / teaching lectures:** switch from conversational to lecture style on any topic, everyday ("driving safety") or specialized ("Overview of molecular biology"), with the constraint that it "must take the audience's background into account" (the audience is probably a single mentor). "You may especially want to use the Record for Feedback technique with this activity."

### 6.9 Record yourself for feedback
"Record yourself speaking for several minutes, and get feedback regarding ways your speech was not like that of host people." (Full system in the Phase 4 guide.) Pairs with retellings and lectures.

### 6.10 Keeping it interesting, fun and personal — discussion-question sets
Antidote to mechanical clarifying. For each segment of a **story**, discuss: What happened in this stretch? What do we know / want to know about the characters? What did they say — why like that, could they/someone else have said it differently? Did problems arise; what might other people do when such a problem arises? What things were used (tools, objects, food) and what was their role? Was what the person said/did good or bad — why? What can we learn from this that will make us wiser? What does this make me think about in my own life?
For a **conversation** recording: Who are they (roles) and what is their relationship, and how did that affect the conversation? What did they talk about? Did they agree/disagree; how did they support their views? Do you agree with one or the other — why? Do you think anything will change because of the conversation? How do you think they felt afterward?
For a **political speech**: Who is the speaker and where does he fit into the political situation? What points did he try to make and why? Did he make them? Will people agree with him, and why? How important are these issues to me? Why?
Meta-lens: consider what the people are **saying**, what they are **doing**, who they are **being** (roles), what they **value**, and what they **believe**. These discussions "can also be recorded and added to the Listening Library." Standing rules: pay special attention to anything that doesn't make sense and discuss until it does; expand parts; ask descriptive questions; ask list questions; **retell later**.

### 6.11 Clarifying culture-loaded items (the joke method)
Model anecdote: Thomson clarified a local joke (husband: "I read the chair verse and go to sleep" — hence his wife stays out of his dreams). Google/Wikipedia could give facts, "However, I couldn't have seen [the mentors'] body language in response to the punch line!" — and reference sources don't know how *local friends* understand a reference. Mentors explained the chair verse as something in the Koran read before sleep to ward off evil beings. Lesson: humor, allusion, and shared cultural knowledge are clarified through mentors, and a clarified joke can still be genuinely funny.

### 6.12 Reading programme (literate languacultures)
- **Solo, at home, a lot** — with bilingual or monolingual dictionary.
- **In meetings: mentor reads aloud, as naturally as possible; record it; clarify the recording.** Never the reverse (GP reading aloud to a paid mentor is wasted money).
- **Never follow the written text with your eyes as you listen** — "your listening process may be short-circuited, if you are able to 'figure out' what is printed more readily than you can hear and understand what is spoken." (Thomson's own Kazakh-vs-Russian asymmetry illustrates: strong listening makes reading profitable; weak listening means reading can outrun and thereby starve listening.)

### 6.13 Communities of practice
"A group of people who spend time together for some particular purpose" — workers on a job, a leisure club (aerobics), a class of host people, a church or mosque, teashop regulars — or a group the GP organizes, "as long as it is dominated by host people doing things their way, such that you initially seem like an outside guest… but steadily move toward being a clear belonger whose role in the group is valued." By end of Phase 5 these become "supercharged" learning environments in themselves.

## 7. Stages/sub-phases & the grower's state

### 7.1 No named sub-phases — but a clear internal progression
The Phase 5 guide defines no lettered sub-stages (unlike Phase 1A/1B). The internal gradients are:
- **Early Phase 5:** the guide's first worked chapter is titled "A meeting with a mentor in *early* Phase 5" — self-collected recordings (Options A/B), easier discourse. The GP still mishears common words ("hocus?"), can't parse a single long sentence without multiple replays, and speaks in visibly non-native syntax ("How they change?", "Please right these down").
- **Difficulty ladder within the phase:** start with conversations **between strangers** ("They will leave less details implicit (unstated) which makes their discourses easier to understand"), move toward conversations **between intimates** ("extra hard to understand!"). Similarly formal broadcast varieties are deprioritized early ("you may not want to suddenly clarify hundreds of hours of speech that is quite different from normal spoken language").
- **Late Phase 5:** heavy-duty reading is running; communities of practice tip over into "supercharged" (understanding all group talk); the GP is crossing into self-sustaining growth.

### 7.2 The grower's state at entry vs. exit
**Entering (end of Phase 4, ~1,000 hours):** knows individual host people deeply; handles rich complex explanations and interviews one-on-one with accommodating partners; "may understand little or nothing" when hosts talk normally among themselves; grammar still visibly non-native; vocabulary well short of 10,000.
**During:** native-to-native speech "is really getting to be in your growth zone — with a bit of help you can understand all sorts of native-to-native speech that you can't understand without help."
**Exiting (→ Phase 6, ~1,500 hours):** "whenever host people are talking to one another and we are listening, what we hear feeds our growth, since we understand it… All of life has become supercharged" — self-sustaining growth. Vocabulary at/near 10,000+ comprehension words. Still makes grammar errors (normal); sociolinguistic/pragmatic/stylistic/kinesic host-likeness remains a Phase 6, "seventeen-year path" project.

### 7.3 Pacing scenarios (the guide's own table)
| GP | Weekly hours | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P5 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Li Fang | 25 hrs/wk throughout | 4 wks | 6 wks | 10 wks | 4.5 mo | **4.5 mo** | 15 mo |
| Amelia | 30 hrs/wk for first 90 days (tourist visa), then 10 hrs/wk alongside job | 3 wks | 5 wks | 15 wks | 1 yr | **1 yr** | 2.5 yr |
| Sam | 10 hrs/wk for 2.5 mo, then 6 hrs/wk, then 3 hrs/wk in Phase 5 | 10 wks | 5.5 mo | 9 mo | 9 mo | **3 yrs** | 5 yrs+ |

All are "viable scenarios," but "the quicker you get into Phase 6… the farther you will grow in the end" — Li Fang gets ~4 extra years of self-sustaining growth over Sam in the same 7 years abroad. Advice: front-load supercharged hours before work responsibilities begin (Amelia's 90-day tourist-visa sprint bought her "basic conversational ability" before starting work); GPs are told to "analyse your own plans in such a table," even speculatively.

**Social-visiting rule:** ordinary social interaction counts "for a lot" *if* partners meet you in your growth zone — but relying on it "may spell the end of our growth." Count **no more than 100–200 of the 500 hours** as ordinary social visiting; deliberately allow up to the full 200 if you're retreating into reclusive or expat-bubble life; enjoy many hundreds of *uncounted* social hours regardless. Warning example: the GP with a host tenant who declares themselves "done language learning" and quietly stops growing.

## 8. What they need to be LOOKING AT (visual/physical materials)

Phase 5's defining resource is *audio*, but the visual/physical inventory is substantial:

- **Recording & playback equipment:** recorder(s) for host people to use unsupervised; a player with easy pause/rewind for phrase-level work; a second recording channel/device for the word recording; capture tech for broadcasts (internet streaming capture, USB TV device or internal TV card + software, DVD recorder — "these technologies may be obsolete by the time you read this").
- **Video as visual material:** host feature films, TV dramas, sitcoms, soap operas, documentaries, nature/cooking/how-to/reality shows, stage shows, stand-up, cartoons, children's shows, sports. Used both for clarifying and for detailed scene description. Caution rule: "what you see is an illusion" — treat the visual channel as something to *describe and have interpreted*, not something you already understand. Dubbed films are second-choice.
- **Pictures for talking activities:** illustrated magazines (National Geographic named), other picture sources, complex "busy book" pictures, cartoons and silent movies for exhaustive description.
- **Written text — now allowed and encouraged, with rules:** newspapers; magazines on many topics; school textbooks (elementary, middle/high school, university); children's books and comic books; technical books; popular-interest prose (health, recreation, crafts, cooking, history, current events); fiction (novels, short stories, plays); humour/joke collections and plays (valuable because they hold "everyday vernacular language in a written form"); internet sites (educational, news, blogs, Wikipedia in other languages); poetry, proverbs; religious books. Plus dictionaries (bilingual or monolingual) for solo reading, and the mentor's **written new-word list** with sound-file locations.
- **The two prohibitions on text:** (1) if printed material is used as a native-to-native discourse in meetings, "have them read aloud, as naturally as possible, and clarify the voice recordings. **Don't follow the written text with your eyes as you listen**"; (2) beware that in many languacultures "the language of publications may not be the language of everyday life."
- **Not in view anymore:** the toys/objects/pictures of Phase 1, wordless picture stories of Phase 2 — the guide's own resource table: Phase 1 "Toys, objects, actions, pictures"; Phase 2 "Wordless picture stories"; Phase 3 "World stories, shared experiences, host stories known from translations"; Phase 4 "Kinds of interviews"; **Phase 5 "Recordings of host to host speech."**

## 9. How much RECORDING they need to do

Recording is the backbone of Phase 5 — more recorded material is processed here "than in previous stages." Four distinct recording streams:

### 9.1 Source recordings (native-to-native discourses) — collected, not made by the GP
- Per discourse: **~1 hour hoped for, 20 minutes minimum**; GP always absent.
- Consumption rate: **20 minutes of recording per meeting hour** (≈ 1 hour of clarifying per 20 minutes of recording; ~3 hours total per recording hour).
- Weekly at full intensity (Li Fang, 25 hrs/wk, ~16 hrs clarifying): **~5 hours of fresh recording per week** — a logistics challenge for minority languages (mitigations: start collecting in Phase 3/4; inherit other GPs' collections; for major languages, record broadcast media freely).
- **Phase total: collect and clarify 100–125 hours of native-to-native recordings** ("tentatively suggested"), consuming ≈330 of the 500 hours and leaving ≈170 hours for other activities.
- Source types (minority-language-safe): hangout conversations someone records; family mealtime conversations (the guide cites a linguist getting an American family to record supper conversations daily); public speeches, sermons, community-health talks, announcements; campfire stories and legends; memorable-life-incident stories; semi-natural: autobiographical narratives, host-conducted walk-of-life interviews; less natural but still native-to-native: assigned-topic conversations, "just converse about whatever you want" small talk. Major-language extras: shortwave radio (hundreds of languages, archived online), local AM/FM (news, interviews, talk/phone-in shows, radio plays, children's stories), TV of every genre, commercial CDs (children's stories, humour, audiobooks — Thomson found 24 hours of Urdu children's stories on bazaar audiocassettes), commercial DVDs (from clay modelling and aerobics to childbirth education and speech-therapy programs, per his Russian haul).

### 9.2 In-session recordings made BY the GP
- **Step 4:** record the mentor's response to your initial summary of the discourse.
- **Word recording (Step 6):** running throughout every clarifying block — see 9.3.
- **Discussion recordings:** the fun/personal discussions of the discourse (§6.10) "can also be recorded and added to the Listening Library."
- **Mentor retellings:** simplified paraphrases "such as when the mentor retells something in simpler form" get recorded and join the library.
- **Mentor read-alouds** of printed texts: recorded, then clarified.
- **Self-recordings for feedback:** GP records himself for "several minutes" (retellings, lectures) for the mentor's not-host-like feedback.

### 9.3 The word recording — volume and review mechanics
- Rate: ~**10–15 new words per 30 minutes of meeting** (at ~10 minutes of speech clarified per 30 meeting minutes, even with just 1% new words) → **~1,500 new words per 20 hours of recording clarified** → several thousand across the phase.
- Format: one little *conversation* per word (elaboration), in order of occurrence; optional **written index of the words in order, ideally with each word's location in the sound file**.
- Review: listen the same evening and "from time to time"; the **shrinking-copy technique** — "You can make a copy of the sound file, and gradually delete discussions of words that are already highly familiar. Then when you listen to the recording again it will be shorter. It may get shorter and shorter in that manner until there is nothing left in it. This really helps you to emphasise more those pesky words that you just have trouble learning well." Bonus: "These recordings make fascinating listening months and years later."

### 9.4 Re-listening schedule (the listening library)
- **Same evening after every meeting:** the whole portion clarified that day + that day's word recordings.
- **Plus:** some previously clarified recordings and older word recordings each time.
- **Ongoing:** "As you accumulate more and more clarified recordings… listen to all of them periodically." The library grows by ~100–125 hours of now-intelligible native-to-native audio over the phase, plus word recordings, discussion recordings, and mentor retellings — the repetition engine that drives the iceberg.

## 10. Milestones & phase-specific terminology

### Milestones / progress markers
1. Native-to-native recordings, once clarified, become "highly intelligible"; increasing amounts are understood without help.
2. 100–125 hours of recordings collected and clarified into the listening library.
3. Vocabulary trajectory on track for 10,000+ instantly recognized comprehension words (~1,500 new words per 20 recording-hours clarified).
4. Understanding group talk not addressed to you; communities of practice become "supercharged"; status shift from outside guest to valued "belonger."
5. Sentence processing fast/full enough to release each sentence and track the next; familiarity with situation- and goal-specific ways of talking.
6. Listening-fed words and constructions emerging in the GP's own speech; can retell whole discourses, defend opinions, deliver audience-adapted talks.
7. Heavy-duty solitary reading underway (where culturally relevant).
8. Grammar: realistic, not perfect — still non-native at ~1,320 hours like everyone else (ILR Level 2 weakness profile); sociolinguistics/pragmatics/turn-taking/stylistics/kinesics consciously deferred to Phase 6.
9. The finish line is a *state change*: self-sustaining growth — "All of life has become supercharged."

### Glossary of Phase 5 terminology (as used in this guide)
- **Growing Participator (GP):** the learner, growing into participation in host life rather than "learning a language."
- **Nurturer vs. Mentor:** nurturers (Phases 1–3) were few, knew the GP well, needed training; **mentors** (Phases 4–5) need no training "as the communication is natural," and in Phase 5 are typically **paid** for clarifying meetings.
- **Native-to-native discourse:** speech by host people to host people in normal host life, recorded with the GP absent, no accommodation to foreigners. Contrast **native-to-non-native** speech (simplified/adapted), which is what mentors produce when giving clarifying paraphrases.
- **Discourse (four senses):** (1) anything actually said/written in real life; (2) any stretch of speech/writing beyond a sentence; (3) society-wide ongoing "conversations" (the election discourse, the discourse about women's role); (4) Paul Gee's **discourse (small d)** = what is said, vs. **Discourse (big D)** = the whole situated practice — setting, roles, actions, objects (the checkout-stand example: the words plus the code reader, card-swiping, roles of customer and cashier).
- **Clarifying:** discussing a slightly-too-hard text with a host person — pausing, slow repetition, word discussion, life-background explanation — "until you understand it."
- **Supercharged participation activities/meetings:** the structured growth sessions; far more growth-dense than ordinary interaction.
- **Word recording:** the running audio file of conversational discussions of each new word.
- **Elaboration:** expanding on a word, using it in different contexts — "known to benefit vocabulary learning"; what fills word-recording entries.
- **Iceberg Principle:** strong first encounters put words into the iceberg; massed re-encounters make them rise; the **tip of the iceberg** = permanent, easy, rapid recall enabling fluent productive use.
- **Listening library:** the accumulated body of clarified recordings, word recordings, and recorded discussions/retellings, cycled through periodically.
- **Growth zone:** material understandable with help but not without — where growth happens. Related: the **temporal dimension** — GPA's principle that "authentic" native-to-native material is *not* appropriate from day one; it becomes relevant "basically in Phase 5, and possibly later portions of Phase 4."
- **Expanding:** "tell me more details" about something said or seen.
- **Hole-finding activity:** any talking task designed to expose what you cannot yet say, so the holes can be filled.
- **Grand-tour / mini-tour questions; walk-of-life interviews; List-of-Life questions; experience questions; domains of life:** the Phase 4 interviewing toolkit, reused on discourse content.
- **Retelling by the GP / by the mentor:** see §6.7 — GP retells to stretch speaking; mentor retells to simplify or to model the host-like version of the GP's own story.
- **Record for feedback:** self-recording several minutes of speech and getting mentor feedback on non-host-like features.
- **Communication ability as joint ability:** ability "resides in a pair of communicators jointly, or a group collectively, rather than in the isolated individuals" (young mothers vs. mother-and-trucker; two astrophysicists vs. astrophysicist-and-students). Rationale for why foreigner-directed pairs cap growth.
- **Community of practice / belonger:** purpose-based host group; the GP's target status is "clear belonger whose role in the group is valued."
- **Self-sustaining growth:** Phase 6 state — most heard speech is understood, so all of life feeds growth.
- **Host / host-like:** GPA prefers these to "native-like," which "has more of an individualistic and cognitivist flavour."
- **Languaculture:** language + culture as one inseparable whole (minority languacultures, Anglo-American languaculture).
- **Understanding machine:** informal term for the comprehension system that solo repeated listening "improves."
- **Decoding / auditory mental lexicon:** reading = mapping print onto words already known by ear; after decoding, comprehension rides the listening machinery ("Figure what that written word 'sounds like' and you get the rest of the understanding process for free").
- **Growing Participation Support Centre:** an organization (or language school) that can supply nurturers/mentors.
- **"Wall of noise" / "what you see is an illusion":** beginner-facing aphorism reused as a Phase 5 movie-clarifying caution.

## App vs. source-guide deltas

The app entry (`phases.ts` id 5, slug `widening`, name "Widening Understanding", tagline "Native-to-native discourse", 500 h, startHour 1000, vocabTarget "→ ~10,000 words recognized", 7 activities, 3 parts: Collect ~60h / Clarify ~320h / Belong ~120h) captures the skeleton accurately. Deltas:

### Faithful matches (for the record)
- 500 hours starting at hour 1,000; ~10,000-word comprehension target; mentor-replaces-nurturer principle; GP-absent recording collection; ten-step template named as "the master template"; next-day retelling as meeting warm-up; listening-library same-evening + rotation review; movie/TV clarifying; community of practice; "Clarify ~320h" matches the guide's ≈330-hour clarifying budget almost exactly.

### Omissions (guide prescribes, app lacks)
1. **The 2-hour meeting structure is absent.** No small-talk opener (5–10 min, with its relational rationale), no 80–90-min clarifying block, no closing 20–30-min supercharged talking block. The app has per-activity `minutes` but no session template combining them; `p5-tensteps` is listed at 60 min vs. the guide's 80–90 min clarifying inside a 120-min meeting.
2. **The word recording is invisible as a practice.** It appears only as two words inside `p5-tensteps.how` ("word recordings"). Missing entirely: the elaboration format (whetstone model), the ~10–15 new words per 30 min / ~1,500 words per 20 recording-hours arithmetic, the written word index with sound-file locations, and the **shrinking-copy deletion review** technique — arguably the most app-able mechanic in the whole guide.
3. **Almost all supercharged talking activities are gone.** Of the guide's list (magazine-picture hole-finding, teach-your-specialty, opinion defense/argumentative discourse, home-vs-host contrast, busy-picture description, cartoon/silent-movie description, 2–3 ongoing daily topics at 10–15 min each, talks/teaching lectures with record-for-feedback), only Epic Storytelling survived (`p5-epic`). The "up to ¼–½ of supercharged time on talking" allocation is unrepresented.
4. **Record-yourself-for-feedback** is reduced to a clause in `p5-retell.how`; the technique (record several minutes → mentor flags non-host-like speech) has no activity.
5. **The reading programme is missing from Phase 5.** The guide devotes a full section: Phase 5 "is the point at which you can really get into heavy-duty reading"; solo reading with a dictionary at home; **mentor reads aloud in meetings, recorded and clarified**; never GP-reads-to-mentor; never follow the transcript while listening; Leaver's 86%-voracious-readers stat. The app moved all of this to Phase 6 (`p6-read`, "Read Promiscuously") — a genuine phase misplacement relative to the source (Phase 6's guide continues it, but it *starts* in Phase 5).
6. **Recording-acquisition mechanics are thin.** `p5-record` mentions hiring hosts and hot-topic debates, but omits: GP absence as a hard rule, the 20-min-minimum/~1-hour target per recording, Option A's "use a past Phase 4 interviewee as interviewer, of a stranger" design, the strangers→intimates difficulty ladder, the formal-broadcast-register caution, collecting from Phase 3/4 onward, and inheriting/sharing collections between GPs.
7. **Quantitative pacing content absent:** the 3:1 clarify ratio, 20 min recording per meeting hour, 100–125 recording-hours target (the parts' hour labels gesture at it but never state the ratios), 5 hrs/wk recording logistics, and the Li Fang/Amelia/Sam scenario table ("analyse your own plans in such a table").
8. **Social-visiting cap absent:** count at most 100–200 of the 500 hours as ordinary social visiting; the "host tenant ≠ done learning" warning. (`5-belong` at ~120h implicitly gestures at this range but the cap rule and rationale are unstated.)
9. **The fun/personal discussion-question sets** (per story / conversation / political speech; the say–do–be–value–believe lens; "discuss anything that doesn't make sense until it does") are absent — the guide's main defense against clarifying becoming mechanical.
10. **Grammar expectation management absent:** ILR 480-hour and 1,320-hour proficiency descriptions, Leaver's 17-years-to-near-native finding, and the explicit deferral of sociolinguistics/pragmatics/turn-taking/stylistics/kinesics to Phase 6.
11. **Solo pre-clarifying** (listen phrase-by-phrase alone before the meeting) appears only as "Solo pre-listen" inside `p5-tensteps.how`; the guide's Steps 2–4 (summarize what you *thought* you understood; record the mentor's response) are compressed away.
12. **Mentor-behavior guidance absent:** coaxing elaborations from ungifted mentors, register commentary, the mentor-must-not-appear-in-source-recordings rule, mentor writes word lists, mentor retelling's two distinct uses.

### Simplifications / soft contradictions
1. **`p5-media` ("Clarify a Movie or TV Drama", kind: culture, 45 min):** "Watch, pause, unpack idioms and cultural references… then rewatch and feel it land" loses the movie session's *centerpiece talking activity* — GP describes scenes in exhaustive detail before the mentor gives the insider's view — and the "what you see is an illusion" caution.
2. **Milestone "Recognize ~10,000 words; switch registers appropriately":** the vocabulary half matches; "switch registers appropriately" overstates the source, which explicitly assigns stylistic-variety competence to Phase 6+ ("second language learners only become really good at these aspects… perhaps [at] the near-native level"). Phase 5 only *notices* register.
3. **`p5-epic`** frames epic storytelling as native-length narration ("your country's history, a novel's plot"); the guide's version is an *incrementally grown* story, a bit each day, improved by retelling each increment to several helpers — the iteration mechanic is lost.
4. **Part `5-collect` at "~60h":** the guide never budgets GP hours for collection (recordings are made *by hosts*, GP absent); collection is a logistics stream, not a 60-hour activity block. Harmless but invented.
5. **`p5-record.minutes: 30` / kind "listening":** collecting is neither a 30-minute practice nor listening per se; the numbers here are app-invented defaults.
6. **App principle "The mentor replaces the nurturer: a guide through authentic discourse"** is fine, but the app implies the swap happens *at* Phase 5; the source has mentors from Phase 4 (with a garbled reference to Phase 3).
7. **Description says "You join your first community of practice"** — consistent in spirit; the guide adds the self-organized-group option and the guest→belonger trajectory, which `p5-community.how` only hints at ("You are inside the room").
