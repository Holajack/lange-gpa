## 1. Phase identity & goals

### Name & position in the programme
- **Phase 2 of 6: "Emerging."** Guide title: *The Next 150 Hours: Story-Building with Pictures in Phase 2 (Emerging)*.
- **Hour range: hours 100–250** of the ~1,500-hour Six Phase Program. Phase 1 (Connecting) = the First 100 Hours; Phase 3 (Knowable) = "the next 250 hours" (hours 250–500).
- **Duration: 150 hours of special-growth participation meetings** with a nurturer. The guide is explicit that "the figure 150 is arbitrary, but we believe that anyone will benefit from that many hours of story-building with pictures."
- **Recommended pace: 20–25 hours per week** of the activity at the beginning of Phase 2.
- **Sub-phase budget:** 2A = 50 hours (GPs take the lead), 2B = 75 hours (nurturer takes the lead), 2C = 25 hours (personal life stories with simple pictures).

### The identity definition (Thomson's own words)
> "The short answer is: I am emerging when host people are starting to see me as a real person in their world with a particular personality. This is because I have intermittent, limited success—but success nevertheless—in efforts to interact, especially to interact verbally using their words."

The GP's own perspective: "I can try talking to that person. Maybe it will fall flat. But maybe it will come to something."

Positioning between phases: in Phase 1 host people see us as **connected**; in Phase 3 they find us **knowable** ("they can have conversations with them on a variety of topics"); "Phase 2 falls in between... more than just connected, and less than knowable to a range of people."

### Purpose
- Phase 2 is explicitly **the bridge from "here-and-now" speech to "story speech"**: "Continued story building provides a powerful transition from being able to understand and produce 'here-and-now' speech (in Phase 1, Connecting), to being able to understand and produce 'story speech' (in Phase 3, Knowable)."
- "Phase 2 is thus a bridge, building on here-and-now interaction ability, in order to develop displaced communication ability."
- The **single primary special-growth-participation activity** is *Story-Building with Pictures* using wordless picture storybooks — considered "a kind of play, rather than a kind of normal adult communication" (like Phase 1's games).
- Because the range of topics is still extremely limited, "the growing participators in Phase 2 are still heavily dependent on special-growth participation" — lifestyle immersion alone yields almost no growth yet.

### Entry prerequisites (Appendix 1)
1. Very minimal ability to **negotiate meanings** (get a point across / understand one with effort and nurturer cooperation).
2. **Familiarity with between 500 and 1,000 vocabulary items.**
3. Here-and-now language: pronouns in all roles; names for basic objects/actions/properties/animals/humans; properties (size, shape, emotions); spatial-locational descriptions ("in the box, under the table"); kinship terms and some roles (shopkeeper).
4. Simple sentence forms: statements, questions, describing in-view situations, giving instructions.
5. **Power tools**: "Please repeat," "Please speak more slowly," "Please speak more clearly," "What is this called?", "What am I doing?", "What is he doing?", "What kind of X is this?"

### What "done" looks like (Appendix 1, "How Far Might You Grow During Phase 2")
Relationally:
- Greatly increased ability to negotiate meanings, but **not yet** having conversations on many topics; relationships "cannot generally go deep yet... That comes in Phase 3."
- May reach the point of profitably living with a host family (if the family will commit to negotiating meanings).
- Relationship with the main nurturer "deepened considerably because you will have learned much about one another's lives" — in *her* world, not through English socializing.

Mental language processes:
- Greatly increased ability to talk about the here-and-now, including everything in pictures.
- **Familiar with over a thousand additional vocabulary items** ("Aim to become acquainted with seven or eight new words per hour of meeting, on average").
- Using increasingly complex sentences in everyday speech.
- Starting to understand long connected stories built with the nurturer.
- Starting to understand simple explanations the nurturer provides "if she keeps it simple, and you can negotiate the meanings."
- Reality check: "When you hear natives talking to one another without regard to your need to understand, you will still understand relatively little."

Learner autonomy:
- "Much less dependent on guidance from others in your language learning activities than you were in Phase 1." (The guide itself embodies this: ~90 pages of instructions for Phase 1's 100 hours vs. ~30 pages for Phase 2's 150 hours.)
- End state: ready for the "stuff-we-both-know" activities of Phase 3. Debriefing in a shared language "should altogether vanish by the end of Phase 2."

### Group format
"It is usually best if growing participators meet with the nurturer in **groups of two to four**, though the activities also work well with a single growing participator... care needs to be taken to insure that all participants have ample opportunity to participate." A solo GP should expect to need **more** picture books because all the creativity demand falls on one person.

## 2. The instructions — how sessions/meetings are structured

The guide gives literal "Meeting at a Glance" tables plus detailed instructions. All Phase 2A/2B meetings share this skeleton: **small talk (5–10 min) → repeating ~55-minute activity cycles for the rest of the meeting → after-meeting listening homework.**

### Phase 2A, Meeting 1 (the canonical plan)

**Resources needed:** 1) a timer with bell (or watch/clock); 2) something to write on; 3) the book *Frog, Where Are You?*; 4) a sound-recording device; 5) you; 6) the nurturer; 7) if possible one or two fellow GPs ("the activity can be more fun and natural in a small group than in a pair"); 8) paper for drawing something you're trying to explain; 9) optional notebook for debriefing notes.

**A. Preparation:** examine the wordless book beforehand "to become aware of the main events."

**B. The Meeting at a Glance:**
| Timing | Activity | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| 5 to 10 min | Small talk. Explain the procedure to your nurturer. | picture story |
| 55 min cycle | Centred around "the monolingual half hour" with a picture story: Steps 1 through 5 | picture story, recording device |
| — | Repeat the 55 minute cycle as time allows | |

**The 55-minute cycle, broken down (verbatim table):**
| Times | Steps | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| 30 min | Step 1, The monolingual half hour with a picture story | picture story, recording device, pencil and paper, a watch, or better yet, a kitchen timer |
| 5 min | Step 2, Debriefing | picture story, any jottings made during Step 1 |
| 10 min | Step 3, Page-by-page recording | picture story, recording device |
| 10 min | Step 4, Listen and point (and act out) and Step 5, Record a brief sample of this | picture story with new words numbered |
| 30 min | Step 1 again. The monolingual half hour ("as time goes on, this step can be lengthened more and more—it may eventually be a monolingual hour") | |

"Etc. (Continue cycling through the five steps as time allows.)" … "Take a tea break, and give your nurturer a break, when you grow weary!"

**Small-talk instruction (quote):** "At the beginning of Phase 2, your ability to carry on small talk is limited. Avoid the temptation to converse extensively with your nurturer in English... A good practice is to **learn at least one new fact about your nurturer each day**, using only the host language... You can also share something parallel about yourself." In a group, GPs also learn about each other, "growing slowly together into a new little host-world community."

Before starting: "Show the nurturer the picture story you're about to use. Allow her to flip through the entire picture story. Otherwise she might want to race ahead... Explain what you want to do during the meeting today."

**Step 1 launch (quote):** "O.K. Time for Step 1. Set the timer. For thirty minutes you must function entirely in your new language until the bell rings. This is going to be a struggle. That is how you will grow."

Steps 6 and 7 (End-of-Book, GP retelling) are flagged with repeated "ALERT, ALERT, ALERT!" — they fire only when the book is finished (maybe meeting 3, "Perhaps meeting 7. Or 10.").

### Phase 2A, Meeting 2
- **Before:** re-listen to previous meeting's recordings; look over the upcoming pages and "try to think of many things you might say about each page, but don't spend time planning specifically how you might say them."
- Same at-a-glance plan, plus **new Step 0**: "Go over the pages of the book that you covered during Meeting 1, trying to quickly refresh all the things you talked about," then proceed to new pages.
- "Always have your nurturer write the numbers of new words and expressions, and include them in the word recordings. This is a continuation of your numbered word recordings from Phase 1."

### Phase 2A, Meeting 3 and subsequent
Identical pattern (small talk → Step 0 refresh → cycles of Steps 1–5), with the monolingual portion allowed to grow: subsequent-meetings table shows Step 1 at **"30 to 90 min"** and Steps 3/4 at "10 minutes or more."

**The general Phase 2A pattern (guide's own summary list):**
1. Begin each meeting with 5–10 minutes of small talk in the host language.
2. Step 0: monolingually review the pages worked on in the previous meeting (or two).
3. Go on to new pages, cycling through Steps 1–5 (and possibly 6).
4. Step 1: 30+ minutes talking about pictures monolingually.
5. Step 2: optional debrief in a shared language after each monolingual block.
6. Step 3: record what you talked about, page by page.
7. Step 4: listen and point (and act out).
8. Step 5: brief recording capturing the listen-and-point content.
9. Step 6: when a book is complete, record the whole story "in the past tense."
10. Do as many monolingual half hours per meeting as time allows.
11. "Remember your after-meeting activities of re-listening to your recordings."

### Phase 2B, Meeting 1

**Before:** GP needs **no preparation** other than listening to recent recordings. The nurturer may take a new book home the night before "in order to have a chance to look it over. However... it is not her job to plan exactly what she is going to say... come prepared to talk extemporaneously."

**Meeting at a Glance:** 10 min small talk; then a "55 min (or more) Cycle" centred on the picture story:
| Timing | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 minutes | Step 1: Nurturer talks freely, describing successive pages of a new wordless picture story (recorded) |
| 30 to 80 minutes | Step 2: Monolingually clarify the recording |
| 5 to 10 minutes | Step 3: Optionally debrief in English (or other shared language) |
| 15 minutes | Step 4: Sound recording for new vocabulary |
| 5 to 10 minutes | Steps 5, 6: Listen and point (or act out) and record a sample of this |
| — | Step 7: GPs tells the story as a continuous story |
| 10 minutes | Step 8: Story retelling by GPs |
| — | Repeat cycle as time allows |

(Note: the guide's 2B numbering shifts — the nurturer's whole-story past-tense telling becomes Step 7, GP retelling Step 8.)

"By Phase 2B, you may feel little need ever to resort to English... To the extent that you still feel a lot of need to use English... you need to continue setting the timer for a monolingual half hour or more, and then allowing brief times for debriefing." Try "monolingual days," then "a monolingual week."

### Phase 2B, Meetings 2, 3, etc.
"Begin by going over what you talked about in the previous meeting, attempting to tell the nurturer as much of it as you can, and getting her to help you recall things as needed (which will be a lot). Then go on with the story from where you left off." May be enriched with the supplementary activities (Lexicarry etc.). "Alert! Don't Forget Steps 7 & 8 when You Reach the End of the Picture Book" — nurturer tells the whole story (same three versions as 2A Step 6), then GPs retell at their own level.

### Phase 2B, "Perhaps Meeting 5 (or 10 or 15...)" — the inverted format
When page-by-page description becomes "highly redundant" ("These are trees. There is a bird in this tree."), switch to a **whole-story-first** meeting (50–110 minute cycle):
| Timing | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min or more | Step 1: The nurturer tells the story "in the past tense" as you follow the pictures. You record this as she talks. |
| 30 to 70 min | Step 2: Clarify the story that she told. |
| 5 to 10 min | Step 3: Optional debrief |
| 15 min | Step 4: Make a word recording |
| 10 min | Steps 5–6: Listen and point (and act out), then record a sample |
| 5 min | Step 7: Nurturer's retelling of story |
| 10 min | Step 8: GPs' retelling of the story |

### Phase 2C sessions (25 hours)
5–10 min small talk, then a "55 to 110 min cycle":
| Timing | Activity | Resources |
|---|---|---|
| 30 to 70 min or more | Step 1: GP tells a story from his/her life, drawing key characters as introduced and pictures symbolising key components. "Since this is tied to pictures, much of it may be 'in the present tense'." | Pencil and paper |
| 10 min | Step 2: Nurturer relies on the drawings to retell the story naturally "in the past tense"; recorded (can even be video). | Drawing, recording device |
| 15 min | Step 3: Make a vocabulary recording | recording device |
| 10 min | Steps 4–5: Listen and point (and act out), then record a sample | recording device |

Escape hatch: "If you attempt this activity and find it too difficult... postpone it until later, perhaps during Phase 3. In that case, simply continue with Phase 2B activities for another twenty-five hours."

### After every meeting (homework)
"Your homework is to listen several times to the sound recordings of the descriptions of the pages you talked about today. Also listen to the recording summarising the 'listen and point' activity, once again pointing appropriately as you listen." Re-listening to earlier meetings' recordings refreshes those words too.

## 3. What the NURTURER does

### Role definition
"Their role is to nurture the newcomers in such a way that those newcomers will participate more and more deeply in the life of the host community. We could also call them 'mentors' as they function as highly skilled participators who let newcomers participate with them." The nurturer is "the first member of the speech community with whom the GP participates," meeting the GP in their **growth zone**.

### In Phase 2A (responsive role — zero preparation required)
- "The nurturer will not have to do any preparation for the meetings" (GPs own the meeting shape).
- **Guess and recast:** "With the help of the pictures, your nurturer will often guess the point of what you are trying to say, and help you to formulate it well. That is how you will grow." Model exchange: GP: "This is boy." → N: "That is a boy." GP: "This is a frod." → N: "Yes, but not frod. Frog." Note this is recasting, not correction-lecturing — the GP echoes the improved form and moves on.
- **Pronunciation shaping in passing:** "N: Yes, jar. jar-r-r. [emphasising the r sound]".
- **Answer power-tool queries** and co-negotiate meanings (the "barn" negotiation: GP describes "Cows in the building. Horses in the building." until N supplies "barn").
- **Write numbers on the pages:** at "Please write it," she numbers the picture item corresponding to each new word/expression (continuing the sequential lifetime word count).
- **Note her own failures:** "If your nurturer fails to get a point across to you... She is to make a note to herself, and not break into English." Notes go to debriefing.
- **Step 3 recording discipline:** she records a detailed description of each page "but talking only about things you talked about together during Steps 1 and 2, and using all of the new words and expressions that arose."
- **Run Listen-and-Point:** using the page numbers as her word log, she says words/phrases/sentences containing the new words ("Where is the window frame?"; "The moon is bright."; "The dog catches a fly.") and repeats new words many times so GPs can mentally process them.
- **End-of-book narration (Step 6):** tell the story "in the past tense" in **three recorded versions**: (6A) careful, highly detailed, page-by-page — being reminded of every agreed detail per page before narrating it ("Once upon a time there was a boy and a dog. One evening the moon was shining bright..."); (6B-2) the whole story without pausing, still watching the pages, "not to worry about every detail, but to still include as much as possible"; (6B-3) the whole story **from memory, not looking at the pictures at all** — naturally less detailed.
- **During GP retelling (Step 7): assist conversationally, do NOT teach.** "This is a point where nurturers can easily go wrong and act like 'teachers' rather than nurturers. In retelling, the GPs are not expected to talk in ways that are close to how the nurturer told the story."

### In Phase 2B (leading role — light preparation allowed)
- May take the new book home overnight, but "she needs to know that it is not her job to plan exactly what she is going to say. She should just figure out what is happening on each page, and come prepared to talk extemporaneously."
- **Describe fully and naturally while pointing:** "She should point at the parts of the picture that she is describing as she speaks." GPs don't interrupt; it's recorded.
- **Her "misreadings" are data, not errors:** "She will describe the picture from the point of view of her experience. It will be her story that you will be trying to understand." (Kazakh nurturer: police officer reading a *law code book*, not a children's book; a bear-girl judged six years old "because she is wearing shoes from a school uniform.") Only plot-breaking misinterpretations (calling a mouse a dog) get corrected — "Occasionally this results in momentary disagreement and discussion, which is good. Keep the interaction as real and lively as possible."
- **Answer clarifications monolingually with example scenarios:** e.g. for "in vain": "You swept your floor, and then a bunch of people came in and scattered trash and left. You swept in vain." Quick English translations would "short-circuit this process."
- **Build word-sentence-word vocabulary recordings** whose context sentence reveals meaning: "Churn. The woman is churning the butter. Churn." — and the GP must "help your nurturer to understand how to create context sentences that clarify the meaning" (bad counter-example: "Churn. I don't have a churn. Churn.").
- In later 2B, **open the meeting by telling the entire story past-tense** while GPs follow the pictures.

### In Phase 2C
- Retell the GP's life story "freely from start to finish, pointing to relevant parts of the drawing as she speaks" — in past tense, recorded (video allowed).
- **Share her own life stories** the same way, with the GP helping her draw ("The very activity of drawing helps to slow down the story, letting it grow bit by bit, and keeping it understandable to you").
- Model native conversation: "Have your nurturer and another host person interact over a wordless picture story, so you see a 'native' model of how to interact"; co-build stories with the GP "in a balanced, natural conversational manner."

### Nurturer don'ts (across the phase)
- Don't revert to English when communication fails — note it for debriefing.
- Debriefing is "absolutely NOT supposed to be used to get English translations of all the new words... nor to hear lengthy lectures in English about the host language grammar, nor... lengthy efforts to 'explain the culture' in English."
- Don't script or memorize her speech in advance.
- Don't act like a teacher during retellings.
- Don't race ahead in the book (hence letting her flip through it first).

### How her behavior changes across the phase
2A: reactive helper, zero prep, recasting the GP's broken output. → 2B: primary speaker and story-teller, minimal prep, explainer-in-language during clarification. → 2C: story partner and life-sharer, and a bridge to other host people. The guide adds: "The Phase 2A activities are also always useful for orienting a **new** nurturer to her role... Phase 2B activities can be used to orient a nurturer to the process of clarifying recordings."

## 4. What the PARTICIPANT (grower/GP) does

### Core behaviors
- **Speak spontaneously in broken language, a lot.** "The point is to get your tongue loosened up—to get you communicating spontaneously—a lot—not just memorising and reciting, but rather expressing yourself in your own words as best you can." "You will speak in broken language."
- **Negotiate, negotiate, negotiate** (the guide's own heading): "say what you can, and get help from your nurturer as often as you need."
- **Use power tools** learned in Phase 1 ("What is this? What is he doing? What is he thinking? What kind of X is this?" plus "Please write it").
- **Never revert to English inside the monolingual block**: "When you try to get an idea across to your nurturer and fail, do not revert to talking in English... Leave it for the time being, making a written note to yourself about the difficulty."
- **Anti-preparation rules (emphatic):** "The point is not to translate this list into your host language in advance of the meeting and memorise the sentences!!... If you were in fact to have the items in this list translated... and were then to memorise them... it would mean that you had **profoundly missed the point of growing participation!!**" Also: "making things up on the fly is what language is all about."
- **Respond nonverbally in Listen-and-Point**: point at picture details; **act out verbs** as charades ("your finger is a fly, and your other hand is the dog's mouth. You say 'arf arf' as your one hand catches the finger").
- **Recognition, not recall:** "don't focus a lot of energy on memorising them... Rather, just become very familiar with them so that you recognise them and understand them when you hear them. Memorisation is time-consuming, and for many people, inefficient."
- **Be creative and invent beyond the picture** — invented dialogue ("The frog is saying, 'I am in jail. I will go out.'"), speculation ("Maybe someone stole the frog. Maybe a kidnapper took him."), predictions ("What will happen next?"). "Such attempts at creativity will make the Story-Building activity more fun... better for relationship-building... They will also result in more interesting stories." Minimum pace: "you should attempt to spend at least fifteen minutes on a page" (elsewhere "not less than ten minutes"); "You probably won't get beyond page three in half an hour."
- **Retell stories at their own level** (Steps 7/8): "'Hey, let me see if I can tell it now'... The GPs' version... will be 'inspired' by the nurturer's version, but it will be told at the GPs' level of ability" — possibly still in present tense early on, shifting to past tense across the phase.
- **In 2B, clarify**: listen back to recordings with the nurturer, flag everything not understood, negotiate meanings monolingually, invent confirmation examples ("Once you think you understand the word, you may want to make up further examples to be certain").
- **In 2C, draw and tell life stories** — stick figures / "triangle people" (from The First 100 Hours), draw-as-you-go or prepared in advance; only key details drawn.
- **Observe host conversation** (late 2C): eye contact, listener back-channel, nonverbal reactions, interruption vs. silence in turn-taking — "You don't want to make great generalisations based on observing a single pair."
- **Small talk + one new fact** at every meeting; share one parallel fact back.
- **Take equal turns** in a group ("especially if one is quiet and the other more outgoing").

### Homework / self-directed work
- **Before meetings (2A only):** re-listen to the previous meeting's recordings; skim upcoming pages for *idea types* only (never wording). In 2B: "you no longer need to do any preparation before the meeting, other than to listen well to recordings of recent meetings."
- **After every meeting:** listen several times to the day's page-description recordings; re-listen to the listen-and-point summary while physically pointing at the pictures again; occasionally re-listen to older material.
- **Long-term:** maintain and re-listen to the Listening Library "occasionally over the next few years... during long flights (better than airline movies), cross-country motor-trips, holidays at the lake, while relaxing in the bathtub, walking to work, etc. **You really can't listen too much.**"
- **Phase 3 prep in parallel:** get "out and about" experiencing everyday host activities (feeds Phase 3's Scripts of Life), and collect/record familiar stories (Grimm tales, fables, Bible stories — grimmstories.com, languagelizard.com, multilingualbooks.com, bilingualavenue.com, bibleforchildren.org) in a known language, listening repeatedly so they become "shared stories" for late Phase 3.
- **Self-evaluation** (Appendix 5) against the CUTE principles: Did the nurturer work hard to help me understand and express myself? Did I hear massive comprehensible speech and meet **at least eight new words per hour**? Did I get adequate, challenging-but-manageable talking opportunities? Are activities still matched to my level — "How long has it been since I've added major new activities?"

## 5. WHAT they are learning

### Vocabulary (with numbers)
- Entry stock: **500–1,000 familiar items** from Phase 1.
- Phase 2 target: **"over a thousand additional vocabulary items"**; pace target **7–8 new words per hour of meeting** (evaluation checklist says "at least eight words per hour"). 150 h × 7–8 ≈ 1,050–1,200 new words → roughly 1,500–2,200 total familiarity by phase end.
- Tracking: **continuous sequential numbering from Phase 1** — "Suppose that in Phase 1, your Auditory Picture Dictionary ended with word number 947. Then the word recordings of Phase 2 will start with word 948... the GPs will always know how many words they have had strong encounters with (following the Iceberg Principle)."
- Nature of the words: Phase 1 words were picturable objects/actions; "In Phase 2 there are more words that are not so picturable" — they arise from discussion and are anchored to picture regions by number (guide's example numbers 1–4 on a page: *seam, tipping, swing, "Oh my word!"*). Word types visible in the idea lists: body/clothing detail (windowsill, window frame, pane, pillowcase, heel, webbed feet), states (wrinkled, striped, plaid, bright, clear, missing), aspectual/adverbial meanings (*still*, *any more*, *about to*), emotions and speech-act verbs (surprised, frightened, shouting, suffocating), modality/speculation (maybe, might, must, will), idioms/exclamations.
- Level of knowledge sought: **recognition/comprehension familiarity, not recall** (Iceberg Principle).

### Grammar / structures
- Consolidation of **here-and-now forms**: present/progressive description, existence/location ("There is a pillow on the bed"), possession, negation, comparatives ("The boots are longer than his feet"), modals of possibility ("The frog might go out"), quoted speech for characters.
- The phase's engineered grammar payoff: **"story event forms" (past tense / narrative morphology)**, first heard massively in Step 6 end-of-book narrations. The famous illustration: after hours of Russian *podkhodit* ("is coming up to"), the story form *podoshol* ("came up to") "hit the learners like a flying brick! Where did that come from?!" — English parallel: *is going… is going…* suddenly becomes *went*. "This new verb form especially stood out because it was surrounded by the familiar elements of the story... moving from one phase or sub-phase to the next by using appropriate learning activities can make certain grammar forms suddenly much more salient. The progression of phases is not arbitrary, but principled!"
- Increasingly complex sentences in the GP's own speech; question forms; instruction forms.
- Grammar *talk* is optional: "Not all growing participators love to talk about grammar... don't let it discourage you" — the learning happens through the activity regardless. GPs may also repeat Phase 1 grammar-awareness activities.

### Discourse skills
- Building and following **long connected stories**; hearing a story world recast from here-and-now description into flowing narrative; producing retellings of increasing quality; conversational interaction skills through clarification negotiation; beginning small-talk ability; observation of host turn-taking, back-channelling, eye contact.

### Cultural knowledge
- **Seeing stories through host eyes** — the Appendix 6 material: the nurturer interprets pictures with "host-world story-making pieces and realms of life" (law-code book; school-uniform shoes; the *Pancakes for Breakfast* guest whose arrival at mealtime is read by a Kazakh nurturer as *good fortune* and her taking most of the food as *delightful*, versus the Western reading of an imposing neighbor). "If the GPs are alert to differences in the way their nurturer understands stories... the transformation is underway—from seeing the host world in home-world ways to seeing it more and more in host-world ways... a transformation which takes time and is never complete."
- The nurturer's life story and local ways of living (2C); everyday-life scripts via role-plays and getting out and about.

### Social expressions / relationship skills
- Daily small talk; learning one new fact per day about the nurturer; sharing one's own life story; exclamations and social expressions arising in stories ("Oh my word!"); phonetic discrimination continues for still-difficult sound contrasts.

## 6. HOW they are learning it — every named activity and its mechanics

### Core: Story-Building with Pictures (the phase's primary activity)
Umbrella name for the whole 2A/2B/2C progression using wordless picture storybooks. Rationale ("Why Story-Building with Pictures?"): visual support enables depth; stories are pleasant, lower stress, aid motivation ("we understand that our life is a story"); continuity across days with built-in repetition ("go from the known to the unknown"); new words learned "in a meaningful, memorable and personal context" with recordings returning them "into their remembered context"; review = re-listening; it bridges here-and-now → story speech; and it grows social interaction ability. Works worldwide except in "traditional, pre-literate people groups," where locally adapted picture stories double as pre-literacy activities. Book count: "as few as five or six such books to fill up a hundred and fifty hours... or every wordless picture storybook they can find" (a less creative GP "may use thirty picture stories").

### The Monolingual Half Hour (2A Step 1: "Describe What You See")
Timer with bell set for 30 minutes; host language only; describe everything on a page; nurturer recasts; power tools + negotiation of meaning; failures become written debriefing notes; ≥10–15 min per page; the block lengthens over the phase (30 → 60 → 90 min, toward monolingual days/weeks).

### Step 0: Refresh (from Meeting 2 onward)
Monolingual quick re-description of the previous meeting's (or two meetings') pages before new pages — built-in spaced review in visual context. In 2B this becomes the GP retelling as much of the prior content as they can, with heavy assistance.

### Debriefing (Step 2, optional, ~5 min)
Only after the bell; only if a shared language exists; strictly for the noted failures ("Here I was trying to say that the boy is dreaming about his frog. How would I have said that?"). New items from debriefing also get numbered and recorded. Explicitly NOT for wholesale translation, grammar lectures, or culture lectures. Should fade to zero by end of Phase 2 ("monolingual days" → "monolingual week" → can work with a nurturer who knows no English).

### Page-by-Page Recording (2A Step 3, ~10 min)
Review the page with the nurturer, then record her describing it in detail using **only** the content actually discussed and **all** the new words that arose. Repeat per page covered.

### Numbered Word Recordings / Auditory Picture Dictionary continuation
Every new word/expression gets the next lifetime number, written on the picture at the relevant detail (GPs "make extra copies of the pictures used on a given day" per Appendix 4), and is included in the word recordings. Hearing the word "tied to its place in the discussion is usually enough to remind the GPs of their (approximate) meaning."

### Listen and Point (and Act Out) (2A Step 4, 5–10 min)
Nurturer works from the numbered new words, saying words/phrases/sentences; GPs point at the picture detail (nouns/properties) or act out (verbs, charades-style). Purpose: many repeated encounters to strengthen the mental lexicon toward recognition.

### Record It (2A Step 5, brief)
Short summary recording of the listen-and-point content for evening re-listening (homework includes pointing along while listening).

### End-of-Book Activity (Step 6 — "Hearing the Story Form")
6A: page-by-page maximal-detail past-tense narration, each page prefaced by joint review of its agreed details; recorded page by page into one continuous story. 6B: (1) nurturer re-listens to 6A; (2) tells the whole story without pausing, watching the pages (recorded); (3) tells it from memory without the book (recorded). "In normal story-telling, a storyteller would probably not give this much background for each scene, but this is not a normal story!" Both full versions are labeled and added to the Listening Library "for the next few years."

### Story Retelling by GPs (Step 7 in 2A / Step 8 in 2B; added 2016)
"Language play": GP retells at their own level, inspired by but not imitating the nurturer's version; nurturer assists conversationally; present-tense early, drifting to past tense across the phase.

### Clarifying the Recording (2B Step 2 — the cornerstone)
Listen back together; GP flags non-understood stretches; negotiate meanings monolingually via definitions and example scenarios (the "in vain" sweeping/cheer-up examples); GP invents confirming examples; new words numbered and recorded. "This is a basic technique that will play a major role in the remaining phases of the Six Phase Program." It deliberately generates "lots of conversational interaction."

### Word–Sentence–Word Vocabulary Recording (2B Step 4, ~15 min)
"Churn. The woman is churning the butter. Churn." — bare word, meaning-revealing context sentence, bare word. Enables "refreshing and strengthening a large number of vocabulary items in a short time." GP must coach the nurturer on writing context sentences that actually carry the meaning.

### Phase 2C story options
- **Option One: Draw As You Go** — draw characters/key components as the life story unfolds (Thomson's example: grandmother locking grandfather out in the 1930s, the suitcase on the porch, father working 800 km away in Nevada, mother in a café...). "Note that only key details are depicted... These were enough to enable the nurturer to recall and tell the entire story." Honest footnote: "This example is a bit misleading. There was lots of struggling, negotiation of meaning and vocabulary learning."
- **Option Two: Prepare in Advance** — pre-draw the story (Angela Thomson's parents-meeting story, triangle people); telling is uninterrupted by drawing.
- **Option Three: All-in-One Picture** — one rich setting map (a nurturer's childhood home and grounds) reused for telling many events set there.
- **Stories from the Nurturer's Life** — she draws-and-tells her own life, GP assisting with the drawings.
- **Conversation practice** — watch the nurturer + another host person build a story; co-build stories in balanced conversational turns; structured observation of host conversational behavior.

### Additional/supplementary Phase 2 activities ("for an occasional change of pace")
- **Lexicarry** (Moran, Pro Lingua, 2nd ed. 1990): GP tells what happens in each Lexicarry story strip — "really just story building using Lexicarry"; moves Phase 1 comprehension-only expressions into productive speech.
- **Picture Strips and Acting Out**: wordless cartoon strips of everyday procedures (*Action English Pictures*, Takahasi & Frauman-Prickel, 1985 — the storybooks PDF calls it "Essential addition to Phase 2," ~$30); or GP acts out an activity (tooth-brushing) while the nurturer narrates. Preview of Phase 3 "Scripts of Life" (there: ~10 minutes of minute detail; here: "less than a minute of recorded talking").
- **Role-Plays**: continue Phase 1-style role-plays of real situations — shopping, taxi, paying the phone bill.
- **Grammar and Phonetic Awareness**: repeat Phase 1 grammar-awareness activities and phonetic discrimination drills for still-difficult sound distinctions.
- **Busy Pictures**: describe scenes with many actors/actions (*The Big Book of Things to Spot / 1001 Things to Spot*, Usborne 2003) "to add exposure to more vocabulary, **if you are not adding enough words to your iceberg** in your meetings."

### Appendix 2: Story-Building Without Wordless Books (Cuisenaire rods / small objects)
For book-scarce or pre-literate settings. Objects stand for characters (Little Red Hen walkthrough: "This is a little red hen... 'Who will help me plant the wheat?' 'Not I,' said the cat... The hen planted the wheat [Act this out]"). First pass full of negotiation; repeat more smoothly; finally the host person tells the whole story in story form. Substitute natural objects — "A rock might be used for a sheep, a fat stick for a canoe." Includes Sarah Janek's ESL rods lesson (childhood-home story; students retell by pointing at blocks; picture-quiz comprehension check; used to/would grammar focus) as adaptable raw material. (Elaine Thiessen demonstrated the same with small toys.)

### Story-building forever
Even in Phases 3–6, story-building remains profitable "in different ways as our ability increases"; reuse old picture stories months later at a new level; 2A activities orient new nurturers, 2B activities teach nurturers clarifying.

### Quality bar for any substitute activity (Appendix 3 summary)
Any alternative technique must: "provide massive 'comprehensible input'; provide extensive opportunities to interact richly on a wide range of topics; foster steady growth in relationships with the nurturers (and other host people); help the growing participator become a redemptive presence in the lives of host people."

## 7. Stages/sub-phases & the grower's state

### Phase 2A — "Getting GPs' Tongues Loose, They Take the Lead" (50 hours)
- **Grower's state at entry:** can understand here-and-now speech about visible things, knows 500–1,000 words mostly receptively, has memorized survival expressions, speaks only haltingly — "talking was a much bigger struggle fifty hours ago than now" is said *looking back from 2B*. Speech is broken ("This is boy... Frog is in... what is this?"); small-talk ability "is limited."
- **Can:** describe pictures in here-and-now forms with heavy nurturer support; use power tools; point/act-out to demonstrate comprehension; learn a fact a day about the nurturer.
- **Cannot yet:** follow a nurturer-led story ("You weren't yet ready to follow the stories that the nurturer might tell"), sustain conversation beyond a narrow range, use past-tense narrative forms.
- **Lead:** GP controls the meeting shape and the perspective on the pictures. Nurturer prep: none.
- **Trajectory within 2A:** monolingual block grows 30 → 90 min; first exposure to story form arrives at each book's end (Step 6) — "your first heavy exposure to 'the past tense', though you have had a lot of experience with the same verbs in the 'here-and-now' forms."

### Phase 2B — "Shift Perspective, the Nurturer Takes the Lead" (75 hours)
- **Why the flip:** "Now that you are talking a lot more freely... You need to be hearing a lot more of what your nurturer would say, because you want to eventually sound like her and other host people... In Phase 2A, you controlled the perspective, which limited the variety of new vocabulary."
- **Grower's state:** talks freely enough that tongue-loosening is done; has listened many times to the 2A listening library and gained "greater ability to follow a simple story that has pictures to go along with it"; may "feel little need ever to resort to English"; can clarify recordings; retellings shift toward past tense.
- **Within-2B progression:** early 2B = nurturer describes page-by-page (here-and-now) then whole story at book's end; later 2B (meeting 5/10/15...) = descriptions have become redundant, so meetings invert to whole-story-past-tense-first + long clarification. Debriefing "may be well on its way to vanishing."
- **Lead:** nurturer. Prep: she may take the book home to understand (not script) it.

### Phase 2C — "Telling Stories from Life with Simple Pictures" (25 hours)
- **Grower's state:** "Your basic ability to participate in host life will have grown considerably... It is time to think about more than getting your point expressed" — attention turns to *conversational naturalness*. Can tell a true life story with drawing support (much of it still present-tense because it's picture-anchored); can follow the nurturer's life stories; begins observing native conversational style.
- **Cannot yet:** converse on many topics; understand native-to-native talk well.
- **Escape hatch:** if too hard, postpone to Phase 3 and extend 2B by 25 hours.

### Comprehension vs. production across the phase
Phase 2 is emphatically **not** comprehension-only (that was Phase 1's early register): 2A exists precisely to force spontaneous production. But new *vocabulary* is still handled comprehension-first (recognition not recall), and new *grammar* (story forms) is met receptively (Step 6 listening) before being produced (retellings drift into past tense "through the course of Phase 2"). The overall growth stated for the whole programme: "The foundational language skill... is the ability to understand speech"; "The ability to put your thoughts into your own words will emerge out of your ability to understand what others say."

## 8. What they need to be LOOKING AT (visual/physical materials)

1. **Wordless picture storybooks** — THE phase resource. Flagship worked example: *Frog, Where Are You?* (Mercer Mayer) — chosen for its simple story line and permanence in print; the guide reproduces its first five pages. Recommended very first book: *The Big Fat Worm* (Nancy Van Laan) — "an excellent choice for the very first picture story... Five stars (if early learners are in mind)." Rule for books *with* text: "cover up the words" (post-its — *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* needs "about 25 small post-it notes"). Test for usability: illustration fills the pages with ≤1–2 lines of text and the pictures alone carry a complete story line (alternating full-page-text/full-page-picture books do NOT work). Quantity: 5–6 books minimum, up to ~30.
2. **The annotated bibliography** (guide Appendix 7 by Rebecca Huston + the `Phase+2+Storybooks.pdf`): ~50 titles with star ratings (1–5), cultural-fit warnings, and OOP flags. The first two are easier than the second two, but require color printing") → **Beginner** ("do some of these, NOT all, just some... 5. **Lexicarry** strips (Moran) — situational picture strips with speech contexts.

9. **Cuisenaire rods / small objects / natural objects** as picture substitutes.
10. **The timer with bell** — a physical fixture of the meeting table.
11. **Written text: essentially absent by design.** The phase is aural/oral; writing appears only as (a) numbers on pictures, (b) failure-notes for debriefing, (c) the nurturer writing a requested word ("Barn. Please write it."), and (d) an optional *add-on* for extreme visual learners: "adding on (not substituting) having host people write down the stories they have built and recorded, and then during review time, read those stories as an additional kind of input... they shouldn't lull themselves into thinking that they can develop adequate listening comprehension ability by reading."

## 9. How much RECORDING they need to do

Recording is compulsory in **every single meeting** of all three sub-phases — the recording device is on the resource list of every meeting plan.

### What gets recorded, by sub-phase
**Phase 2A (per meeting):**
1. **Page-by-page descriptions** (Step 3, ~10 min per cycle): nurturer describing each discussed page in detail, restricted to negotiated content, using all new words.
2. **Word recordings** (continuation of the numbered Auditory Picture Dictionary — new words from Step 1 AND from debriefing).
3. **Listen-and-point summary** (Step 5): "a brief, summary sound recording of this activity to listen to in the evening."
4. **End of each book (Step 6): three full-story past-tense versions** — (a) careful maximal-detail page-by-page (one continuous recording built page at a time), (b) flowing whole-story while watching pages, (c) whole-story from memory without the book. "Add both versions of the full story to your Listening Library... Label them well."

**Phase 2B (per meeting):** the nurturer's page descriptions are recorded **live in Step 1** (~10 min of new narration per cycle) — "The page-by-page recording that was Step 3 in Phase 2A is not necessary in Phase 2B, since it was already done in Step 1!"; plus a **15-minute word-sentence-word vocabulary recording** (Step 4); plus the listen-and-point sample (Steps 5–6); plus, per book, the nurturer's whole-story telling(s) (Step 7 — same three versions) and, in the later-2B inverted format, the opening whole-story telling (10+ min) is itself the recording that then gets clarified for 30–70 minutes.
**Phase 2C (per cycle):** the nurturer's past-tense retelling of the GP's life story (~10 min; "can even be captured on video"); a 15-minute vocabulary recording; the listen-and-point sample. "The nurturer's clear, natural retelling of your stories will be a great addition to your listening library."

### Rough per-meeting arithmetic (from the tables)
A 2A meeting of two 55-minute cycles produces ≈ 2 × (10 min page recordings + a few min listen-and-point summary) ≈ 25–30 minutes of new audio. A 2B cycle produces ≈ 10 min narration + 15 min vocabulary + 5–10 min listen-and-point ≈ 30 min of new audio per cycle. Every finished book adds three full story versions. Over 150 hours this compounds into "hours of recordings, which the [GP] will be able to largely understand" (Appendix 4's definition of the Listening Library).

### Re-listening quotas
- **Same evening / before next meeting:** "listen **several times** to the sound recordings of the descriptions of the pages you talked about today," plus the listen-and-point summary **while pointing at the pictures again**.
- **Days following** each end-of-book recording: "Listen to each one a few times in the days following the meeting in which it was recorded, and occasionally after that."
- **Years-long horizon:** "You will find profit in listening to them occasionally over the next few years... You really can't listen too much."
- 2B preparation is *only* "to listen well to recordings of recent meetings."
- Review doctrine: "Review becomes as simple as re-listening occasionally to the stories that were built earlier."

### Clarifying as a recording practice
In 2B the recording is not just an archive — it is the *working object*: Step 2 (30–80 min, the bulk of every 2B meeting) is spent replaying it and negotiating everything not understood. This "clarifying the recording" is flagged as "a basic technique that will play a major role in the remaining phases of the Six Phase Program."

## 10. Milestones & phase-specific terminology

### Progress markers / milestones
- Host people start seeing the GP as "a real person in their world with a particular personality"; GP can initiate interactions that "may come to something."
- Monolingual block stretches 30 → 60–90 min; first successful "monolingual day," then "monolingual week"; ultimately "you may find that you work as well with a nurturer who knows no English."
- Vocabulary iceberg passes ~1,000 *additional* words at 7–8 words/hour, all sequentially numbered (lifetime count always known).
- First end-of-book Step 6: follows a full past-tense narration of a co-built story with ready understanding — "a clear step forward in your experience of the host language"; the "flying brick" moment for story-form grammar.
- Retellings shift from present-tense to past-tense across the phase.
- Fewer than 7–8 new words/hour from the current book tier = signal to move up a tier (storybooks PDF) or add busy pictures (guide).
- Page-by-page description turning "highly redundant" = signal to invert the 2B format (whole story first).
- Debriefing need trending to zero = the shared-language crutch is gone.
- Can exchange life stories with the nurturer (2C) — relationship "taken to new depths"; possibly ready to live with a host family.
- Meeting self-evaluation (Appendix 5): pleasant interaction; ≥8 new words/hour; challenging-but-manageable listening AND talking; activities recently refreshed.
- Exit: ready for Phase 3 "stuff-we-both-know" activities; "You aren't yet to the point of sitting around with lots of people having lots of conversations on lots of topics, but it won't be long... It will come sometime during Phase 3, the next 250 hours."

### Glossary (terms as this phase's guide uses them)
- **Emerging** — the Phase 2 identity: intermittently, limitedly, but genuinely succeeding at verbal interaction, so host people see a real person with a personality.
- **Growing Participator (GP)** — replaces "language learner": growth = becoming increasingly able and active in the host community.
- **Nurturer (N)** — replaces "language helper/tutor/teacher/LRP": the first host person the GP participates with, meeting them caringly in their growth zone.
- **Host language / host world** — replaces "target language": joining a community, not completing a task.
- **Special-growth participation meetings (SPS) / activities** — replaces "language class/lessons": designed interaction times maximizing participation and growth.
- **Lifestyle growing participation** — a.k.a. "immersion"; being among host people in normal life. Of ~100 waking weekly hours, **home-world hours** vs **host-world hours**; in early phases lifestyle time yields "only a few minutes of 'growth time'" per ten hours.
- **Here-and-now language** — speech tied to what speakers/listeners see and do now; vs **displaced communication** (stories about other times/places, abstractions).
- **Story speech / story form / "story event forms"** — normal narrative register ("in the past tense"): *went*, *kicked*, *podoshol*.
- **Negotiating meaning** — the interactive repair work through which learning occurs.
- **Power tools** — host-language expressions used to obtain more language.
- **Monolingual half hour** — timer-enforced host-language-only block; grows toward **monolingual days/weeks**.
- **Debriefing** — the short scheduled shared-language window for failed negotiations only.
- **Clarifying (the recording)** — replaying a recording with the nurturer and monolingually working out everything not understood; cornerstone of Phases 2B–6.
- **Listening Library** — replaces "comprehensible corpus": the labeled archive of recordings (picture descriptions, stories, interviews, "massaged" broadcasts) for repeated pleasurable listening. (Note: "massaged" appears in this guide only inside this definition; massaging as a technique is elaborated in Phase 3.)
- **Word recordings / Numbers for new words** — sequentially numbered vocabulary audio keyed to numbers written on (copies of) the picture pages; continues Phase 1's **Auditory Picture Dictionary** count (word 947 → 948).
- **Iceberg Principle** — (defined in the Phase 1 guide, invoked here): track the large mass of strongly-encountered, recognition-level words, not just the tip you can recall.
- **Growth zone** — the GP's current ability, "which may only be evident as they interact with a sympathetic native speaker."
- **Communities of practice** — host-community relationship networks that deepen over time.
- **CUTE principles** — the four principles undergirding the Six Phase Program: **Communing** (growth happens inside host relationships — "your highest concern should be to give yourself to them"), **Understanding** (comprehension is the foundational skill; builds the "understanding machine"), **Talking** (speech emerges from understanding; hundreds of hours of talking build the "talking machine"; "The path to 'speaking well' runs through the large country of 'speaking atrociously.' There is no other path!"), **Evolving** (activities must keep changing with ability; "Neither should you be settling down permanently in any one phase").
- **Triangle people** — the quick-draw human figures from The First 100 Hours, reused in 2C drawings.
- **Busy pictures / busy books** — many-actors-many-actions scenes for vocabulary flooding.
- **Learner-type challenges** (Appendix 3, for advisors): introverts, extraverts (risk: befriending English-speakers), visual learners (may add written story transcripts as a supplement only), concrete learners, high need-for-structure ("sink-or-swim spirit — with a caring nurturer making sure that they swim!"), **loss of previous identity** ("talking about children's picture stories might be the last straw... growing into adulthood may work better than trying to start out full-grown"), tolerance for ambiguity ("I'm just not getting it right now—let's leave it and come back to it in a few months!").

## App vs. source-guide deltas

Comparison of `src/lib/phases.ts` (id 2, "Emerging", 150 h, startHour 100) against the Thomson guide.

### Numbers that disagree
1. **Sub-phase hours contradict the guide.** App: 2a "~50h", 2b "~80h", 2c "~20h". Guide: **2A 50 / 2B 75 / 2C 25** (each printed as a "Recommended Goal" heading). The 2b/2c split should be corrected or the deviation made deliberate.
2. **Vocab target slightly inflated:** app says "+1,200 words → ~2,200 total"; guide says "over a thousand additional" at 7–8 words/hour (= 1,050–1,200 across 150 h). Defensible, but the guide's operative number — **the 7–8 new words per HOUR pace target and its use as a book-tier advancement/busy-picture trigger** — is absent from the app entry.
3. **Weekly pace absent:** guide recommends **20–25 meeting hours/week** at the start of Phase 2. Nothing in phases.ts encodes cadence.

### Core mechanics the guide prescribes that phases.ts omits
4. **The timer-enforced Monolingual Half Hour as a *rule*, and its growth curve.** p2-describe borrows the name in its description, but the app has no timer/bell, no "host language only until the bell," no lengthening 30→60→90 min, no "monolingual day/week" milestones.
5. **Debriefing (Step 2) is entirely missing** — the 5-minute shared-language window, the failure-notes mechanic (both GP *and* nurturer jot failures instead of code-switching), the strict anti-translation caution, and its designed fade-to-zero.
6. **Step 0 refresh** (monolingual review of previous pages at the start of each meeting) — a built-in spaced-repetition mechanic — is missing.
7. **Listen and Point (and Act Out) — Step 4 — is missing entirely**, along with its Step 5 summary recording and the pointing-along-at-home replay. This is one of the most app-able mechanics in the whole phase (tap-the-picture comprehension checks) and appears nowhere in the eight activities.
8. **The End-of-Book Activity (Step 6) with its THREE recorded story versions** (detailed page-by-page / flowing with pictures / from memory) is missing — this is the guide's engineered grammar-salience payoff ("flying brick" moment). p2-storybuild's "then retell parts yourself" gestures at Step 7/8 retelling but the three-version nurturer narration structure is absent.
9. **Numbered word tracking is absent:** the continuous lifetime word numbering from Phase 1 (word 947 → 948), numbers written on picture-page details, extra copies of pages, and the Iceberg-Principle counter. (Phase 1's entry mentions "p1-dictionary" but Phase 2 drops the thread the guide explicitly continues.)
10. **The 55-minute cycle structure is not represented.** The app lists activities with flat minute counts summing to a single ~2.5 h linear session; the guide's meetings are *repeating cycles* (Steps 1–5, 55 min each, "repeat as time allows," tea breaks), inside meetings that run multiple hours.
11. **Homework/re-listening is under-encoded:** the guide's mandatory evening re-listening ("several times"), pointing-along replay, and years-long listening-library rotation appear only as a one-line principle ("re-listen at home"), not as an activity or feature.
12. **Anti-translation / anti-memorization / anti-planning prohibitions** ("profoundly missed the point!!") aren't stated; likewise the idea-list aid for "I can't think of very much to say" (a natural in-app prompt deck) is absent.

### Structural / attribution mismatches
13. **2a's activity set is too thin:** only p2-smalltalk + p2-describe. The guide's 2A also contains debriefing, page-by-page recording, listen-and-point, summary recording, end-of-book narration, and GP retelling. As encoded, a user doing "2a" would record nothing — contradicting a phase whose 2A meetings each produce ~25–30 min of audio.
14. **2c is a single activity (p2-lifestory).** The guide's 2C has three story options (draw-as-you-go / prepare-in-advance / all-in-one setting picture), the nurturer's own life stories, the vocabulary-recording + listen-and-point follow-ups, the conversation-observation/co-building activities, and the "postpone to Phase 3, extend 2B by 25 h" escape hatch — all missing. p2-lifestory also adds "and photos," which the guide never mentions (drawings only; photos are a Phase 1/3 device).
15. **Supplementary activities partially missing:** Busy Pictures and Process Series are present (good), but **Lexicarry story strips, role-plays (shopping/taxi/phone bill), grammar-awareness and phonetic-discrimination repeats, and the Cuisenaire-rod/objects alternative** are all absent. The app also files Busy Pictures/Process under 2b specifically, whereas the guide offers them phase-wide as change-of-pace items — minor.
16. **Busy Pictures trigger condition is preserved** ("When the iceberg's growth slows below pace") — but "pace" is undefined in-app because the 7–8 words/hour target (delta 2) is missing.
17. **Nurturer behavioral spec is absent:** recast-don't-correct is stated for Phase 1B but Phase 2's entry says nothing about the nurturer's don'ts (no teaching during retells, no scripted prep, misreadings-are-culture, pointing while describing, meaning-revealing context sentences that GPs must coach her on). No nurturer-facing guidance exists in the entry.
18. **Phase 3 preparation strand missing:** getting out and about + pre-recording familiar stories (Grimm/fables/Bible sources) during Phase 2 has no representation.
19. **Group format missing:** guide prefers 2–4 GPs per nurturer with equal turns; app is implicitly solo.

### Tone/emphasis tensions (not strictly errors)
20. App principle "Speech emerges — it is never forced. Like a child's first words, it comes when the base is ready" sits awkwardly against 2A's actual doctrine: 2A deliberately **pushes** production ("For thirty minutes you must function entirely in your new language... This is going to be a struggle. That is how you will grow"; "sink-or-swim spirit"; the path through "speaking atrociously"). Emergence-not-forcing is a GPA-wide truth about *readiness to start*, but the app's phrasing could license passivity in exactly the sub-phase built to loosen the tongue.
21. Milestone "'probably somebody'" is an app paraphrase; the guide's phrase is "a real person in their world with a particular personality." Fine as voice, but the KB should treat the guide's wording as canonical.
22. p2-storybuild merges wordless books, busy pictures, and process strips into one description; the guide keeps story-building (the primary activity) categorically distinct from the supplementary items.
