## 1. Phase identity & goals

- **Full name:** *Phase 1: CONNECTING (to a whole new world, primarily through one of its members)* — subtitle *"The First 100 Hours — Special Play in the Here-and-Now"*, by **Greg and Angela Thomson** (guide dated Summer 2016; Phase 1B section dated Jan 2016). Copyright 2016; informal copying freely allowed.
- **Hour range:** Hours **0–100** of a recommended **1,500 hours** of "special growth participation" across six phases. The guide notes Phase 1 is "less than ten percent of the total set of special-growth participation activities" but provides "a rapid and powerful 'kick-start.'"
- **Position in the six-phase table:** 1 Connecting → 2 Emerging ("having become 'somebody' to a few people") → 3 Knowable → 4 Deep Personal Relationships → 5 Widening Understanding → 6 Ever Participating/Growing.
- **Two sub-phases (official):**
  - **Phase 1A: "Games for Listening and Responding Nonverbally"** — Meeting Plans 1–15, **30–40 hours**, word gain: **first 300+ words**.
  - **Phase 1B: "More Listening Games and also Constrained Two-Way Interaction Games"** — Meeting Plans 16–40, **50–60 hours** (the 1B introduction says "the next twenty-five meetings (50 to 80 hours)"), word gain: **add 600+ words**.
- **Core metaphor / purpose:** The GP stands outside a **"wall of noise"** — host speech that is meaningless sound. The **nurturer** creates a space inside the wall by doing exactly two things: *(1) "The nurturer talks to us in such a way that we can understand his or her words and word combinations." (2) "The nurturer helps us express ourselves in ways that he or she (and host people in general) can understand."* Phase 1's deepest goal is **relational**: "Your main accomplishment in Meeting 1 was to launch such a personal relationship with a host person." Language learning is reframed as growing participation in a "languacultural world"; the guide's motto (1B title page): *"Don't learn the language! Rather, discover a new world, as it is known and shared by the people among whom you are living."*
- **What "done" looks like (end of Meeting Plan 40):**
  - Auditory recognition vocabulary of **~1,000 words (stated goal 1,000–1,200)** — "Experience has shown that the goal of 1,000 words was probably reached somewhat ahead of Meeting 39," so MPs 39–40 become review.
  - Most of the **first 300 words moved from understanding into speech**.
  - A **Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary of 800+ numbered pictures** with matching audio recordings.
  - Can hold constrained two-way exchanges: describe busy pictures, retell simple cartoon-strip stories at own level, correct false statements, make true statements from any cue card, answer/ask names, ages, day/date/time, role-play buying and selling with bargaining, use the "power tools."
  - Grammar *awareness* (never mastery) of the here-and-now essentials.
  - The guide's closing line: *"You're ready to start Phase 2! Congratulations! Celebrate!"*

## 2. The instructions — how sessions/meetings are structured

### Programme mechanics
- **Meeting Plans ≠ meetings.** "Meeting Plans are designed to last from 2 to 3 hours. If you finish a plan before the end of your time, continue to the next meeting plan. Start the next meeting wherever you left off, even if it is partway through a 'meeting plan.'" Plans must be done **in order** ("each builds on the ones before"). Stop *between* games when time runs out.
- **Pacing:** "If a group completes an average of two meeting plans per day, five days per week, then the total set of plans will take four weeks to complete." (i.e., 40 plans ≈ 20 working days ≈ 100 hours.)
- The plan numbering is deliberately frozen: "we don't want to change the division into days, as this guide has been translated into many languages and they all need to have the same meeting plans and games in the same order."
- **Players per session:** *"Three kinds of players in the games: • A host companion (the nurturer) • The growing participators (two to six) • Possibly a coach (who is also a growing participator — either a beginner, or someone who has already grown a little or a lot)."* A coach present all the time "is a great way to train a new nurturer"; without one, "nurturers would ideally get at least a week of advance training."
- **Before Meeting 1:** an initial informal **social event** (party/potluck) plus an **orientation meeting** where everyone reads the intro, skims all 100 hours' plans, shares hopes and fears. At one of these: take the photos for "Photos of Us in Action," and play the **Name Game** (appendix) in Rough-and-Ready-Dozen format so every GP receives a **personal host-world name** the nurturer uses thereafter.
- **Plan format:** every Meeting Plan is a three-column table — **GAMES! | EXPLANATION, INSTRUCTION | OBJECTS AND PICTURES TO GATHER** — with an interleaved **Record** row after nearly every game.

### Phase 1A meeting shape (MP1–15)
A chain of **4–8 games** per plan, typically:
1. Several **Rough-and-Ready Dozen** games introducing new word sets (objects, animals, body parts, colors...).
2. **LISTEN & DO** action/command games (TPR).
3. **Recombination/strengthening games** (Make It True, cue-card-stack games, "Grand Refreshing").
4. A few minutes of **Photos of Us in Action**.
5. A **Cartoon Bubbles / Lexicarry** segment (social expressions).
6. **End of every game:** record a ~1-minute sample; **end of every game or day:** record the Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary.
Homework and prep instructions are embedded between plans (print photos, market trip, bring family photos).

### Phase 1B meeting skeleton (MP16–40)
The 1B introduction prescribes a "regular meeting pattern": *"In a typical meeting, new vocabulary can be learned in a listening game, while the new words from a previous meeting can be re-encountered in a talking activity."* The fixed recurring segments, in order, are:
1. **"Day and Time" Opener** (from MP31 onward): "From now on, begin each day's first meeting with the nurturer telling — then in later meetings asking — What day is today? What time is it? What day was yesterday? The day before yesterday? Tomorrow? The day after tomorrow?" (Defended as legitimate "display questions" / language play.)
2. **~10 minutes of sound-focus games**: "Hear the Difference?", "Sorting Sounds into Columns", "Spot That Sound", optional Word Dictation — "Spend up to ten minutes a day focusing on sound in these ways."
3. **Cartoon Bubbles / Lexicarry segment** — 3–4 new expressions per meeting plus refreshing earlier pages (later evolving into story narration and retelling).
4. **A major TALKING activity** re-using the *previous* meeting's new words (Ladder of Success, info-gap, states-and-needs, role play). Rule: *"We attempt to stick to a pattern of learning new material in listening games at least one meeting plan prior to using it in a [talking game]"* — relaxed as an explicit experiment at MP32 Game 6.
5. **New-vocabulary LISTENING games** (Rough-and-Ready Dozen, LISTEN & DO) — "we mustn't slack off for long on encountering new words."
6. **Form-focused / structured-input games** as needed for the specific host language.
7. **"Yesterday's Actions"**: "Whenever a new set of actions are learned... the following day it is good to quickly strengthen them... Actions seem to need more help than nouns!"
- **MP36 is "Plan Your Own Meeting"**, with a printed planning form: *"Plan for Meeting___ Game #___ / Resources needed / Areas of new vocabulary planned / How many new words are you aiming for? / Recent words to be strengthened / Older words to be refreshed and strengthened / Instructions for the game / Are there aspects of grammar you expect will receive special highlighting? / How does this activity combine earlier learning with new learning?"*

### The canonical rules (quoted): "Rules of the Rough-and-Ready Dozen"
**Do:**
- "Stay connected to the nurturer as a person"
- "Have fun—laughing is good!"
- "Start with two items… Add one new item at a time, when all the GPs are ready to go on."
- "Be satisfied with your hearing of a word as long as you can point correctly."
- "Listen intensely to the nurturer's voice and at the same time, think about the 'meaning'…"
- "After adding a new item, the nurturer asks the whole group to understand and point together a few times. Then the nurturer has GPs respond one at a time."
- "Nurturer asks questions in RANDOM order."
- "Nurturer uses real sentences 'This is a…' 'Where is the…' 'Jump!' (not only isolated words)."
- "Nurturer asks about the newest word more often than other words"
- "Nurturer asks about any words GPs are having difficulty with more often than other words"
- "No one but the nurturer talks, and the nurturer only talks in the host way!"
- "If a GP makes a mistake, the nurturer responds naturally… 'No. That's not the cow. That's the horse, where's the cow?'"
- "When one GP is stuck… other GPs may also help if the person wants them to."
- "Some objects may be temporarily removed to make the choice easier… Go back to just two items… then go back to the full set."
- "Keep adding—always one at a time—until the words for every item in the plan for that game has been understood by all the GPs"
- "At that point, record a one minute sample of that game that includes all the items."
- "Then make a recording of the picture dictionary."

**Don't:**
- "Don't start with just one item nor with three or more items!"
- "After those first two items, don't add two or more new items at any one time."
- "Don't do a lot of 'point and listen'… simply say the new word, not more than two times, and then begin asking 'Where is…' questions again."
- "GPs don't talk. During listening games, you want to be hearing only the nurturer's voice and no other (not even the coach's voice)."
- "No one writes. You are learning to hear at this point."
- "Don't try to master new words… You'll learn to hear and understand many new words rather than trying to fully master a much smaller number."

Sample of the nurturer's actual talk (fruit dozen): *"This is an apple. This is an orange. Where is the orange? Where is the apple? Where is the apple? Where is the orange? This is a pear. Where is the pear? Where is the apple? Where is the pear? …"*

## 3. What the NURTURER does

- **Identity:** a host-world member who **plays**, explicitly *not* a teacher: "the nurturer... is playing with the GPs in ways that let them understand what the nurturer is saying, and not 'teaching them the language.'" The urge to repeat a new word many times "stems from the nurturer's desire to act as a teacher rather than a nurturer!"
- **Register:** "Talk to the GPs as you would talk to a group of friends who are your social equals… informal play time." If culture forces a respectful register for older GPs, use both registers and let everyone hear the difference tied to age.
- **Language discipline:** speaks **only the host language** during games — including corrections ("No, that's not the man. That is the woman. Where is the man?" delivered "naturally and kindly"). Sole exception: formulaic Lexicarry expressions "may as well use translations, if possible, to identify their approximate meaning" (they are formulas understood as wholes). The Lexicarry-strips key adds: "In general we do not ask for a translation of our own language expressions. Rather show or explain the situation and ask what the host person would say in a similar situation in their world."
- **Core mechanics (1A):** start with two; add one at a time; say the new word once (at most twice) then immediately question; randomize both items and responders ("Give a command… without addressing any GP in particular, and then wait so they can all think… Then say the name of one GP"); weight questions to newest/weakest words; use real sentences; demonstrate actions herself the first time only; use puppets to demonstrate before GPs act (MP8: puppet "assistant" shown "Take the red candy. Give the red candy to the dog"); break long sentences "into natural pieces with short pauses" for those who need it.
- **Struggler protocol:** "go back to just two photos that that particular GP struggles with and then… start adding in more… (This principle of helping a struggler by narrowing the new items to just two and building back up again applies in a wide variety of games…)"
- **Speed:** "Skilled nurturers keep the games going quickly and don't wait and wait trying to decide what to say next!" — cue cards exist partly to cure nurturer decision-paralysis and "ruts" (using only a few favorite words).
- **Uses cue cards** to guarantee coverage of *all* learned words; draws random cards from stacks (e.g., action + family relation + body part → "Hit his brother's foot").
- **Records** the 1-minute game samples and picture dictionary entries; describes final scenes for take-home recordings; is photographed doing actions; brings **her own family photos** at MP7 ("going beyond 'play' and into more of a normal connection").
- **How the nurturer's behavior changes in Phase 1B (talking begins):**
  - Becomes an **assistant to GP speech**: "The nurturer will help them say what they are trying to say… It's like a child trying to say 'alli… alli…' and mom helps him say 'alligator'!"
  - **Recasting instead of correction** (MP22 header): "if you say 'I going,' the nurturer responds, 'Yes, you are going'… It has been shown that correction by recasting is more effective than direct correction, such as 'That's wrong, you should say it like this,' which causes stress and embarrassment."
  - **Pronunciation handling** (MP35 header): repeat mispronounced words clearly "in the course of interacting with them in the game… there should not be a drawn out pronunciation drill." In the Ladder game: "if a GP has not pronounced something to the nurturer's satisfaction by the third attempt, they should stop repeating for the time being."
  - Judges "close enough" pronunciation (Ladder of Success), sits on/beside info-gap teams as coach ("not telling them where to put the cards… just helping them communicate"), narrates Lexicarry strips as stories (MP34), and asks display questions (Day-and-Time opener).
- **Coach rules:** doesn't talk during listening games; may give host-language encouragements ("Good job!" in host words only) until the nurturer takes that role over; helps pre-select bubble expressions; helps design form-focused games. Cautionary tale (MP10): "We observed a case where GPs in Phase 2 were not able to use 'My, your, the girl's…' and when we investigated, there was a coach involved who decided that this was of minor importance. He should have asked us!!"

## 4. What the PARTICIPANT (grower/GP) does

### In sessions — response modes
- **Phase 1A (nonverbal only):** point at objects/pictures/people; **perform actions fully** ("they should run around the room, not just pretend to run in place; they should lie down somewhere, not just pretend"; if told to sit when already sitting, re-make the sitting motion); manipulate/arrange objects (Make It True; "put the duck into the pitcher"); place cue cards (the "sad" card beside the girl doll); give/take/show objects; put on/take off clothing; act out emotions ("Be sad!"); trace paths with a finger (MP32); gesture **yes/no** (introduced MP9); raise a hand when hearing a target sound; place beans on bingo boards; **listen "intensely" and stay silent** — "Are you listening without talking in any language?!"
- **Phase 1B adds constrained talking:** naming body parts of a model; showing and describing their own **family photos**; moving objects up the **Ladder of Success** by saying words/phrases/sentences; answering & asking questions in info-gap games; stating needs/responses ("I'm hungry" → "Take the egg"); role-playing buyer/seller with bargaining; giving commands to each other ("John, you tell Sue to peel the egg"); describing their own actions as they do them ("I am opening the jar"); retelling the nurturer's cartoon-strip stories "at their current level of ability"; acting strips out as role plays; drawing while others copy from spoken description; charades; answering display questions; making true statements from random cue cards; using power tools ("What am I doing?"; "What is the opposite of big?").
- **Attitude rules for GPs:** "GPs should cooperate and not try to redirect the nurturer!"; "Don't ask the nurturer for explanations. Accept what she says…"; "be willing to ignore what bits you don't understand, as long as you get the main point"; in talking games, "GPs should make a point of trying to say the words they feel are the weakest for them, rather than the ones they feel safest with… It isn't a matter of individually 'showing what I can do,' but rather attempting what is hard for me, being helped, and growing in the process"; "the group is a team, not competitors."
- GPs **choose weak words** ("low in their icebergs") to feed back into strengthening games (MP2, MP14 G7, MP32 G7); may debrief early talking experiences at the end of meetings (MP16, if they share a language).

### Homework / self-directed work (between meetings, every evening)
- **Re-live the day's recordings:** "Tonight at home, listen to those bits of the experiences of today. Relive them… try to have before you objects such as the ones used in the games… When you hear 'Where is the man' in your recording, point at the man doll… if you don't have a man doll, you can substitute the cue card… When… the nurturer says 'Run!' in the recording, be sure to actually get up and run." — "Your recordings are brief, and so keep re-living the experiences until they seem familiar to you."
- **Refresh via the Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary** only when a word can't be recalled ("Don't try to memorize or mimic the Dictionary").
- **Prepare materials:** print and cut photo sheets; **market trips** ("Go outside… buy fifteen distinct items… Don't include an object unless the GPs have some idea of its use"); bring personal family photos (before MP16); make/localize drawings.
- **Keep a growing-participation journal** (mentioned at MP11: "glean from their growing-participation journals any times they've noticed… a problem with hearing sounds accurately").
- **Periodic self-assessment:** "The GPs can take stock at various points. 'Now I have finished Phase 1A. What relationships, if any, are developing in my lifestyle participation?'… throughout Phase 1 it is really fine if very little is developing" — the nurturer relationship is the "secure nest."
- **Lifestyle participation feed-in:** GPs notice recurring real-life communication situations (subway exits, queue-joining) and bring them back; nurturer turns them into new Lexicarry-like strips, role plays, or long LISTEN & DO chains.
- Re-read the Do's/Don'ts list before day 2 and periodically ("Be prepared to discover and discuss instructions you may not have followed").

## 5. WHAT they are learning

### Vocabulary targets (the numbers)
- **Phase 1A:** first **300+ words** in 15 meetings (30–40 hours). **~150 words by Meeting 7** ("Grand Refreshing – Hurrah for 150 words from the host world!"). **~300 is the talking threshold**.
- **Phase 1B:** **add 600+ words** over 25 meetings, reaching **800–1,000+**, with the stated end-goal "an auditory recognition vocabulary of **1,000 to 1,200** vocabulary" (MP37 header) — typically hit *before* MP39.
- The iceberg section: the 100 hours' games are "designed… resulting in the ability to understand **800 to a thousand words or even more**."
- The Picture Dictionary is the literal counter: "When you finish Phase 1, your picture dictionary will have **800 or more numbered pictures** with accompanying recordings."
- The authentic inventory counts **~814 specific words named across ~70 Rough-and-Ready Dozen sets** in the guide.
- Rate notes: pace deliberately slows in 1B ("the second three hundred words are taking more days to encounter than the first three hundred"); action words need more re-encounters than nouns; emotion words learn slower than object words; some meetings are deliberately light on new words (MP12, MP21).

### Grammar / structures (receptive awareness, not mastery)
Possessives & possessive pronouns (heavily emphasized — the guide warns skipping them cripples Phase 2); subject & object pronouns incl. reflexives/reciprocals (yourself, each other); verb-form/person mapping (structured-input pronoun-drop games); singular/plural; and/or; to/from case marking; negation & negative commands; yes/no; question words (who/what/where/what-kind/how-many/why-because/whose/how/when); if…then conditionals; before/after/while sequencing; can/can't + because; want/have/see/need/like; must/may/need-to; comparatives of quantity; ordinals; relative clauses (subject and non-subject); direct vs indirect discourse; imperative singular/plural. The stated goal (MP37): "for the GPs to be reasonably aware of the major aspects of grammar that are an essential part of 'here-and-now' communication… by the time the GPs have an auditory recognition vocabulary of 1,000 to 1,200."

### Discourse & phonology skills
Listening for gist while ignoring unknown detail; following multi-clause commands; ~10 min/day sound discrimination from MP11/16 (minimal pairs, initial/medial/vowel sorting, target-sound spotting, ears-first dictation); pronunciation grown from improved hearing ("Accurate perception of what words sound like develops only through the ears, not through the eyes!" — with the documented ship/sheep experiment showing spelling does NOT improve discrimination); negotiation of meaning and communication strategies in info-gaps.

### Social expressions & cultural knowledge
~57 numbered Lexicarry functions across the phase (greetings/farewells/thanks → sneeze responses, asking time, interrupting → apologies, door answering, beggars → offering/refusing food, offering/accepting help → restaurant ordering, comforting the sad → gift giving, telling a doctor about pain, warning of danger → introductions, asking for the restroom, compliments → condolences ("comforting someone whose relative has died"), joining a table, bad-tasting food → phone answering, asking price, asking directions → delight, displeasure, congratulating on a new baby, asking for a cigarette light → pain, fear, refusing company, asking for change → asking for a seat, permission to smoke, getting attention, birthday greetings → toasts, clarification requests → encouragement to retry, car accidents/accusing, reassurance/food compliments → welcoming to home, talking to the ill, permission to speak → scolding children/excuses, disappointment, taking leave → affection, guessing, anger & calming). Register variation is explicit: the strips key distinguishes "Same as 1A if different with older person," younger/older name-asking variants, etc. Cultural learning also flows through: host kinship structure ("there can be lots of surprises" — older/younger sibling terms), family dynamics (fully by Phase 4), host register by age, local money, local calendar/prayer times, local house layout, market practices/bargaining, the town scene reflecting local city life, and the deep doctrine of "conceptual accent": the GP's concept of "man/woman/dog" keeps becoming host-like for years.

## 6. HOW they are learning it — every named activity/technique

**Core listening formats**
1. **Rough-and-Ready Dozen** (once titled "Diry [Dirty] Dozens," MP18) — THE vocabulary engine. Mechanics fully quoted in §2. ~a dozen items per set; each set ends with a 1-minute recorded sample + picture-dictionary recording.
2. **LISTEN & DO** (= TPR) — command-response: nurturer demonstrates an action once while naming it; GPs then physically obey randomized commands, whole-group first, then individuals (command → pause → name). Grows from "Stand. Sit." to "After you rest, sneeze" and multi-step chains ("You are riding on the subway. Hold on to the hand-rail…").
3. **LISTEN & POINT** — the pointing variant used in RRD and photo games.
4. **Make It True** — inverse of pointing: nurturer states something not yet true; GPs rearrange the world (stick figures, dolls, cue cards, toy house, or themselves — the MP6 "you and he" human variant) until it is true.
5. **The Us Game / Us and Our Animal Friends (MP1)** — the first game & "magic moment": "This is a man. This is a woman. Where is the woman?" → the GP points and "they are inside the wall!"
6. **US in Action (MP1 G2)** — first TPR: full-body actions in a large area.
7. **Photos of US in Action** (recurring MP1→32) — photos of the actual group members doing actions; questions cross person × action ("In which photo am I walking?"); "we" requires pointing at TWO photos; 1B flips it to GP description.
8. **Cartoon Bubbles / Lexicarry Game** (every meeting) — puppets act out a situation, then bubble pictures are questioned RRD-style ("Who is saying 'Hello'? Who is saying 'Thank you'?"); ~5–6 expressions/day, learned as unanalyzed wholes; approximate translation permitted; late-phase evolution: why-explanations (MP30) → indirect discourse (MP33) → nurturer narrates strips as stories (MP34) → GPs retell and role-play (MP35+).
9. **Name Game** (appendix; orientation) — host-world names learned RRD-style.
10. **Bag of Stuff** — cumulative container of every object ever used; feeds every strengthening game.
11. **Cue cards** — second copy of the picture dictionary cut apart; used (a) at home in place of objects, (b) as the nurturer's coverage-reminder, (c) as random-draw sentence generators (location + 2 objects; action + kin + body part).
12. **Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary** — see §9.
13. **Grand Refreshing (MP7, 14, 15, 36)** — the whole Bag of Stuff on one table; commands/questions across the entire vocabulary; combined commands ("Take the spider and run to the window"); cue-card stack checked at the end for anything missed.
14. **Gap-filling (MP9 note)** — brief interludes patching noticed holes (e.g., the missed Kazakh word for "younger sister of a brother").
15. **Structured-input games** — engineered listening that forces attention to form: **To and From Alone** (MP10: verbs omitted so case markers become the only cue), **Alone or in Groups** (MP10: singular vs plural rows), **Here-and-Now Descriptions with pronoun-drop** (MP18–20: "(He) is eating…" — GPs must hear the verb form).
16. **Sound-discrimination suite** (daily from MP11/16): **Hear the Difference?** (minimal-pair listening contrast — words must already be familiar; never first-encountered here); **Sorting Sounds into Columns** (MP18: sort the whole cue-card deck into emergent columns by initial/medial/vowel sound); **Spot That Sound** (MP22: raise a hand at the "sound of the day"); **Word Dictation** (MP24–32 window, ONLY if the script is phonemic and GPs know the alphabet: write 5–10 dictated known words from the EARS — hearing→spelling, never the reverse).
17. **Feelings suite (MP11, 14)** — emotions as commands first ("Be happy! Be angry!"), then face-card pointing, Make-It-True placement, kin combos, long-sentence gist ("Where is the frightened man's hat?").
18. **Power Tools (MP11–12, 14–15, 18–19, 38)** — learner-driving expressions: "What is this? What are they doing? Please repeat. I don't understand. I don't know. Please speak more slowly/loudly. What am I doing?" Two-way from MP19; Phase-2 prep set at MP38.
19. **Numbers suite (MP12–13, 31–32)** — quantities of visible objects (matches) 1–10; **money for unglanceable quantities** ("This is 15 pesos…"), tens to 100, then 200–1,000–million (MP31); ordinals (MP13); "Show me seven mice"; "Kill the third mouse with the wrench"; **Number Bingo** (MP13, 32: 9–12 numbers per board, beans as counters, first full row wins).
20. **World-map suite (MP13; talking at MP27–28)** — countries in sets of 12, continents, nationalities, five question forms (Who lives in/speaks/is learning/wants to go/is from) with the male figures placed in *wrong* countries and wives along the map bottom; later born-in/grew-up-in.
21. **Listening contrast with true/false (MP9 G5a)** — deliberate false statements → "No, the cat is not in the basket; the cat is beside the basket" (first negation exposure).

**Core talking formats (1B)**
22. **Helping One Another Up the Ladder of Success (MP16–17)** — THE pivotal first talking game: all objects/cards at one end of the table; 4 string-marked rungs; on each turn a GP says a word — if "close" (nurturer judges) the object moves to rung 1; objects advance only when someone EXPANDS what was said ("Dog" → "Brown dog" → "The brown dog is running"); fundamentally mispronounced sounds (b for p) send the object back for a retry next turn; escalating rounds (1 new + 1 expansion, then 2+2, 3+3…); "This is a pivotal activity, moving a large number of words upward in the iceberg from comprehension only to production." Needs a time limit and speed.
23. **Information-gap activities** (defined in the 1B intro): teams with asymmetric information behind a **barrier** must talk to solve a problem. Instances: **Sixteen Pictures** (MP18 listening-heavy, MP19 GP-driven: two 4×4 grids of 16 scenery drawings in different orders; question exchange until both sides match); **Copying Actions Through Hearing** (MP23: one side cuts out faces while described; the unseeing side replicates); **People with Emotions Around Town / Countryside** (MP23–24: describe-and-place on duplicate scenes); **One Draws, Others Listen and Copy + Fix That Drawing!** (MP27: drawing dictation onto whiteboards, then verbal correction round: "The flower isn't near the tall man's foot — it is ON the tall man's foot").
24. **Physical States and Needs (MP19–21, three-step)** — step 1: nurturer states her own state ("I'm hungry") → GP replies with an object command ("Take the egg"); step 2: states of anyone ("You are hungry" → "Give me the egg"); step 3: GPs run it entirely, with and/or combinations.
25. **Role plays / minimal role-plays** — buying & selling books with pay/prices (MP31); full bargaining ("I'll give you 100 riyals." "No, 200 riyals." "I only have 150," MP32); **The Marketplace** (MP32, 35: occupations, shops, puppet workers, fake money, full script Greetings → Do you have X? → I want Y → How much is it?); taxi-style **directions** on a street map (MP33: "Take the green car… Turn right at the stoplight"); door/beggar/phone scenarios via bubbles.
26. **Form-focused games / communicative grammar activities (MP26, MP37 headers)** — language-specific games forcing repeated real-meaning use of one troublesome form (Kazakh examples: locative possessed nouns "on my/your/her book"; possessed-noun endings by sentence role). "The key… is that the GPs are forced to use the particular forms repeatedly to express real meanings." Designed with a linguistic consultant / LLA where possible.
27. **Recasting** (MP20, 22) — the correction method for all talking games.
28. **Actions Charades (MP29)** — teams act out action cue cards; other team names the action; 2 points without prop, 1 with; corrections by the acting team ("No, I'm not swallowing, I'm eating").
29. **Story retelling (MP35)** — GPs collaboratively retell the nurturer's strip-stories "at their current level of ability… their best effort at this point."
30. **Day-and-Time opener (MP31+)** and **display questions** doctrine.
31. **Busy Picture games (MP38–40)** — question rounds then full GP description of a crowded scene (e.g., "William Wanders Off").
32. **Making Statements (MP39–40)** — random cue card → GP must produce a true statement, using power tools to self-rescue.
33. **Negation pair** — Negation-Listening (MP38: GP makes false statements, nurturer negates) and Negation-Talking (MP40: roles swap); **"Don't you dare!"** (MP38: positive/negative commands, Simon-Says quality).
34. **Relative clauses (MP39–40)** — "Show me a man who is walking" → non-subject versions "Show me a woman that a man is helping."
35. **Synonyms (MP37)**; **General Words and Plurals (MP39)**; **Yesterday's Actions** (daily strengthening ritual); **Market trip: buy one of each** (real-world resource gathering); **Action English Pictures (AEP)** — optional supplementary picture-sequence resource (MP37–40); **Plan Your Own Meeting (MP36)**.

**The word life-cycle** taught at MP4: **Encountering → Experiencing → Strengthening → Re-experiencing/Re-living → Refreshing → Re-encountering in new contexts → familiarity** — every activity above maps onto one or more of these stages.

## 7. Stages / sub-phases & the grower's state

### Phase 1A (Meeting Plans 1–15; 30–40 hours; 300+ words)
- **Comprehension-only. The GP does not speak at all during games** — not even in their own language; no writing; no reading. "These are listening games, and so you are not supposed to know them well enough to recall them and use them in speech."
- State of the grower: silent, physically responsive, listening "furiously"; accumulating an iceberg of receptive words; often anxious that "I'm not learning anything!" — the prescribed reframe: "You are learning exactly what you are supposed to be learning… It's in your iceberg."
- The grower has a triple **foreign accent**: in hearing ("you don't hear what they hear"), in concepts ("conceptual accent" — host 'man'/'woman' only minimally shared), and in expression. Sleep/brain-fatigue is normalized ("don't be surprised if your brain is tired!").
- Can, by end of 1A: point at or physically execute anything named among 300+ words including multi-attribute commands ("Give a small green candy to the girl's younger brother"), answer yes/no, and has the recording/re-living routine ingrained. Cannot yet: produce words reliably, read, write, or converse.
- Note: many GPs are "initially uncomfortable with the idea of not speaking… Most quickly get used to it… It becomes fun and relaxing."

### Phase 1B (Meeting Plans 16–40; 50–60 [up to 80] hours; +600 words → ~1,000–1,200)
- **Constrained two-way interaction begins at the ~300-word threshold.** "A new goal… is to get most of their three hundred vocabulary items from the level of understanding to the level of talking." Many words are "immediately available… for speaking. Others will require some prompting."
- Emotional reality flagged: "Early talking can cause a surprising rise in anxiety, and at times it seems to stir some very deep emotions indeed… the activities below are designed to 'break people in gently.'"
- Talking is **constrained**: games bound what must be said so GPs reuse similar patterns, yet are "motivated to say things they have never said before… expressing themselves in their own words, conveying real meanings" — never parroting/memorizing.
- **Listening games continue daily** for all new vocabulary; new words enter listening ≥1 meeting before talking (experimentally relaxed at MP32 G6, and at MP34 G3 words are for the first time encountered in a talking activity then reinforced by listening — "a foretaste of Phase 2A").
- Mid-1B state: asks/answers name-age-origin questions, states needs, completes info-gaps, gives/follows directions, bargains, uses power tools; still "keeps needing assistance from the nurturer" — by design. "There is much GPs can say with help from the nurturer that they cannot say without help, and talking with help is an important way to grow."
- Hearing state: discriminates most initially-identical sound pairs; sorts words by sounds; optionally writes from dictation (the ONLY writing in Phase 1).
- End-1B state: see §1 "done" list. "Progress happens. Perfection is not the starting point. (It is not the finishing point either!)" (MP31 header).
- Sub-stage nuance within 1B: MPs 16–17 are transitional (Ladder game dominates); MP31 introduces the daily opener ritual; MPs 39–40 are consolidation/review since the 1,000-word goal usually lands early.

- **Photos:** printed sheets of the GPs & nurturer performing actions (draft quality, multiple per page, scissor-cut); nurturer's family photos (MP7); GPs' family photos (MP16); digital photos of table arrangements/final scenes to accompany recordings.

## 9. How much RECORDING they need to do

**"TWO KINDS OF SOUND RECORDINGS: (1) Recorded Samples of each game; (2) Numbered talking picture dictionary."** A recording device is on the materials list for virtually every game; from MP2: "Usually, when you 'record' you create these two types… From now on, unless there is some variation, we'll just say 'record.'"

### (1) Per-game samples
- **When:** "As soon as the last item in the plan is added, turn your recording device on and let it go on recording until all the words in the game have been questioned."
- **Length:** "the whole recording should not take more than a minute" (MP1); bubble-picture recordings "might be two minutes long"; several games say "record a few minutes' sample," "record all of this activity" (Show Your Emotions MP14, Photos-in-action refreshes, negation games, relative clauses MP40), or "record most of this activity."
- **Content:** every new item of the game, in random order, in the nurturer's voice only.
- **When NOT:** games with no new words skip the dictionary but usually still record a sample ("Since there are no new locations or objects, no need for the Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary, but you can still record a two-minute sample").
- **Scene-description recordings:** at the end of arrangement games the nurturer records a spoken description of the final scene, paired with a **digital photo** (MP9 G5b, MP17 G5, MP23 G7, MP27 G3B) — "best if videotaped or accompanied by a digital photo." **Video** explicitly recommended for the MP28 house-rooms tour and MP35 story retelling ("This makes a great video-recording").
- **In 1B talking games:** "You do not need to record yourself or other GPs talking. However, sometimes you may want to record the nurturer, at the end of an activity, summarizing what took place" (MP16 note). Many 1B plans still open with "Remember to record each activity."

### (2) Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary
- Recorded **at the end of each game or at latest by the end of each day**: "if you don't finish a Meeting Plan on a given day, be sure to do the Talking Picture Dictionary at least by the end of the day."
- Format: pictures numbered sequentially across the whole phase, recorded as *"One. This is an old man. Old man. Two. This is an old woman. Old woman. Three. This is a man. Man…"* — "Suppose you had fifteen new words this time. The next time… you will then begin with the number 'sixteen.'"
- End-of-phase size: **800+ numbered pictures with accompanying recordings**.
- Bubble expressions get their own dictionary convention (MP16): "record the expressions in the order in which they occur in the Lexicarry or the graphics packet."
- Function: refresh-only lookup. "Don't try to memorize or mimic the Dictionary. It is just for when you can't recall a word while re-living a game."

### Re-listening (the consumption side)
- **Every evening**, the GP re-lives ALL of the day's game samples **while physically responding** — pointing at real objects or cue cards, actually standing/running on command — "keep re-living the experiences until they seem familiar to you." No total minutes are prescribed; the samples are short (1–2 min each × ~4–8 games/plan × ~2 plans/day ≈ 10–30 minutes of raw audio, looped to familiarity).
- Recordings are also the fallback review channel "when you cannot reassemble the objects that were used in the gathering" (use cue cards instead).
- 1B listening-before-talking exists partly "to allow those who want to, the opportunity to listen to their recordings in between meetings" before words must be spoken.

## 10. Milestones & phase-specific terminology

### Milestones / progress markers
- **Pre-M1:** host-world name received (Name Game); group photos taken; orientation completed.
- **M1:** the **magic moment** — first comprehension; "your main accomplishment… was to launch a personal relationship with a host person."
- **M7:** **"Grand Refreshing – Hurrah for 150 words from the host world!"**
- **End of 1A / M15 (~30–40 h):** **300+ words** understood; the talking threshold; nonverbal command of possessives, locations, plurals, numbers, family, emotions, body parts, colors, foods, tools.
- **M16–17:** first speech — a large portion of the first 300 words spoken via the Ladder of Success; first self-descriptions (family photos, names/ages).
- **Mid-1B:** two-way constrained exchanges, info-gap completion, needs/states, directions, bargaining role-plays, power-tool usage; sound pairs discriminated; (optionally) dictation from hearing.
- **~M39:** **1,000-word goal typically already reached**; MPs 39–40 = consolidation.
- **M40 / 100 hours:** ~1,000–1,200-word auditory vocabulary; 800+-entry talking dictionary; story retelling, busy-picture description, negation, relative clauses, day/date/time fluency → **"You're ready to start Phase 2! Congratulations! Celebrate!"**

### Glossary of Phase-1 terminology (as used in THIS guide)
- **Growing Participator (GP)** — the learner, reframed as a participant growing into a host world, never a "language student."
- **Nurturer** — the host person who "creates a space for the GPs inside the wall of noise"; provider of "tender loving care"; playmate, not teacher.
- **Coach** — an additional GP (novice or advanced) who silently trains the nurturer.
- **LLA (Language Learning Advisor)** — outside expert who may supply minimal-pair sets or form-focused game designs.
- **Host world / home world** — the new group's lived world vs. the GP's origin world; each has an enormous **"box of 'pieces of life'"** (men, clothing, jobs, weddings, practices…).
- **Languacultural world** — language+culture as one lived whole.
- **Practices** — repeated, shared, purposeful actions of a group; "Using a word is a practice."
- **Wall of noise** — host speech as impenetrable sound to outsiders; "For members of the group… an instant window into one another's thoughts. For the four outsiders… a wall."
- **The magic moment** — the first instant of understanding-and-pointing.
- **Special-growth participation** vs **lifestyle participation** — structured nurturer sessions vs everyday-life interaction; sessions are **"supercharged participation meetings"** ("It might take many weeks to engage in as much fruitful participation in the community as we can experience in just a few hours").
- **Here-and-now games** — Phase-1 activities about only what is visible/performable now; two types: **listening games** and **two-way conversation games**.
- **Rough-and-Ready Dozen** — the core format (see §2, §6); "rough-and-ready" itself = gist-level, good-enough understanding ("Rough-and-ready understanding… not perfectly mastered").
- **LISTEN & DO / LISTEN & POINT** — the TPR command format and pointing variant.
- **Make It True** — rearrange-the-world response format.
- **The Iceberg Principle** — "really pay good attention to new words when first encountering them… keep re-encountering them in new contexts"; never force mastery; words sink and rise. Refrain: **"It's in your iceberg."** Local metaphor substitutes: "a volcano, a well, and a submerged hippo or crocodile."
- **Encountering → Experiencing → Strengthening → Re-experiencing/Re-living → Refreshing → Re-encountering** — the documented life-cycle of a word (MP4 header, worked example: "window").
- **Numbered Talking Picture Dictionary** — the cumulative numbered picture+audio dictionary.
- **Cue cards** — cut-apart dictionary copies; reminders, substitutes, game pieces.
- **Bag of Stuff** — the ever-growing object collection.
- **Power tools** — "expressions which GPs use to learn more host words and phrases: 'What is this? Please repeat?' etc."
- **Bubble Pictures / Lexicarry(-like) strips** — empty-speech-bubble situation pictures for formulaic expressions.
- **Information gap activities** — barrier games forcing communication to resolve asymmetric information.
- **Structured input** — listening engineered to force perception of grammatical form (pronoun-drop, verb-omission).
- **Form-focused games / communicative grammar activities** — language-specific repeated-real-meaning grammar games.
- **Recasting** — correction by naturally restating the GP's utterance correctly.
- **Negotiation of meaning / communication strategies** — the productive struggle in talking games ("GPs trying to say challenging things, getting stuck, using communication strategies").
- **Display questions** — questions asked so the GP can display ability (Day-and-Time opener); defended as "language play."
- **Listening furiously** — the pre-talking imperative to maximize hearing acuity (MP11 header "Comments on 'Listening Furiously'").
- **Hearing accent / conceptual accent / foreign accent** — the GP's triple home-world filter.
- **Gap-filling** — brief interludes patching vocabulary holes.
- **Sound discrimination problem** — "two words that host people hear as different, but a GP cannot" (drives Hear-the-Difference games).
- **Growing-participation journal** — the GP's log (used for sound problems).
- **Phase 1A / Phase 1B** — listening-only vs +constrained-talking sub-phases.
- **Name Game** — receiving/learning host-world names.
- **AEP (Action English Pictures)** — optional picture-sequence supplement.
- (Note: "massaging" is NOT a Phase-1 term — it belongs to later phases' recording techniques. Phase 1's equivalents are "strengthening" and "refreshing.")

App also files it under 1B (`parts[1]`) and kind "speaking," but power tools are *introduced in 1A* (MP11–12) as listening before the talking threshold.
